## Definition
Software 2.0 refers to a paradigm shift in programming where the primary "code" is not written by humans using explicit logic (Software 1.0), but is instead learned by machines through data and optimization. In this paradigm, the "program" is a set of weights in a [[Deep Neural Networks]] architecture, and the "compiler" is the training process (gradient descent) guided by an [[Autodiff]] engine.

## Key Characteristics
- **Data-Driven Logic**: Instead of `if-then` statements, behavior is derived from patterns in large datasets.
- **Optimization as Compilation**: The process of training a model serves as the mechanism to "compile" data into a functional program.
- **High-Dimensionality**: Software 2.0 programs operate in high-dimensional spaces, making them difficult for humans to read or debug linearly but highly effective at complex tasks like [[Computer Vision]] and [[Natural Language Processing (NLP)]].
- **Generalization**: Unlike Software 1.0, which is rigid, Software 2.0 is designed to generalize to unseen inputs based on learned features.

## Context and Influence
The concept is heavily associated with the work of **Andrej Karpathy**, who has championed the transition from manual engineering to neural network-based systems. This shift is fundamental to the development of [[Large Language Models (LLMs)]], autonomous vehicles (as seen in his work at Tesla), and robotics.
