# Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)

## Overview
Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are a class of neural networks designed to process sequential data. Unlike standard feedforward networks, RNNs possess "memory" by utilizing internal loops that allow information from previous steps in a sequence to influence the current output.

## Key Characteristics
- **Sequential Processing**: Ideal for data where the order matters, such as time series, speech, and natural language.
- **Hidden State**: Maintains a hidden state vector that acts as a summary of past inputs.
- **Weight Sharing**: The same weights are applied at each time step, allowing the network to generalize across different positions in a sequence.

## Notable Research and Context
- **Theoretical Foundations**: A significant body of work explores the "Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks" in various modeling tasks.
- **Academic Influence**: RNNs have been a core focus of major research initiatives, notably within the doctoral research and curriculum development at [[Stanford University]].
- **Related Architectures**: Often studied alongside and in contrast to [[Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)]] and [[Large Language Models (LLMs)]].
