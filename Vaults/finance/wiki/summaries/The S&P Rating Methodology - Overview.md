# The S&P Rating Methodology - Overview

This overview details the analytical framework employed by [[S&P]] for sovereign credit rating evaluation, based on their 2017 methodology document. Presented by [[Alex Stomper]] at HU Berlin in 2024, the framework decomposes creditworthiness into five interconnected assessment pillars: [[Institutional Assessment]], [[Economic Assessment]], [[External Assessment]], [[Fiscal Assessment]], and [[Monetary Assessment]].

The [[Institutional Assessment]] evaluates the stability, transparency, and accountability of a country's policymaking environment and civil society. It incorporates subjective indicators such as debt repayment culture and potential external security risks, adjusting the initial score based on these qualitative factors. The [[Economic Assessment]] measures the underlying economic foundation by analyzing GDP per capita, growth trajectories, and economic diversification to project future tax capacity. The [[External Assessment]] examines a nation's cross-border financial position, focusing on currency status, foreign exchange liquidity, and the balance of residents' external assets and liabilities.

At the core of the rating process is the [[Fiscal Assessment]], which determines the sustainability of public finances. It is divided into two segments: fiscal flows (assessing flexibility, long-term trends, and vulnerabilities) and fiscal stocks (evaluating the debt burden, funding access, and contingent liabilities). Contingent liabilities are further segmented into banking sector exposures (evaluated via BICRA), non-financial public enterprises, and off-budget guarantees. The [[Monetary Assessment]] examines the central bank's capacity to fulfill its mandate, weighing the exchange rate regime against the credibility of monetary policy, which is measured by institutional independence, policy effectiveness, price stability, and financial market depth.

Despite the structured criteria, the methodology explicitly acknowledges significant room for [[Discretionary Judgment]]. Analysts apply subjective adjustments for factors like cohesive civil society, event risk, and extreme liquidity constraints. The indicative rating can be overridden by more than one notch based on unspecified qualitative factors, highlighting the tension between standardized metrics and analyst discretion. This lack of full replicability forms the basis for a proposed Citizen Science initiative, which aims to systematically apply S&P's methodology to European countries to document where the process remains opaque or inconsistently applied.
