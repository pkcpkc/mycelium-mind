# IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation: Belgium Country Report

The IMF’s 2025 Article IV consultation highlights that while the Belgian economy has shown resilience to recent shocks, it faces significant headwinds from slowing growth, persistent core inflation, and declining labor-cost competitiveness. The report underscores the urgent need for sustained fiscal consolidation to address structural deficits and a projected public debt trajectory exceeding 120% of GDP by 2030 under unchanged policies. Staff recommends a seven-year adjustment plan targeting an annual structural primary balance improvement of 0.6 percentage points, prioritizing the rationalization of social benefits and the public wage bill while preserving and enhancing public investment in infrastructure, healthcare, and education.

Structural reforms are identified as critical for long-term growth and stability. The IMF strongly advocates overhauling the [[Automatic wage indexation]] system and the 1996 wage law to reduce fiscal pressures and improve labor market flexibility. Key labor market interventions include widening the income gap between work and non-work, limiting unemployment benefits, and promoting lifelong learning to address severe labor market fragmentation and skills mismatches. On the fiscal side, tax reforms should gradually shift the burden from labor to capital through the introduction of a [[Capital gains tax]] and reduction of tax expenditures, alongside pension adjustments that raise the effective retirement age to counteract demographic aging pressures.

The financial sector remains broadly stable with moderate systemic risks, though the IMF stresses maintaining macroprudential buffers and accelerating legislative actions from the 2023 [[Financial Sector Assessment Program]]. Product market reforms, including streamlined insolvency regimes and reduced regulatory barriers, are essential to improve firm dynamics and total factor productivity. Furthermore, deepening the EU single market and advancing a capital market union are highlighted as vital for enhancing Belgian firms’ competitiveness. Finally, while progress has been made on decarbonization, achieving climate objectives requires stronger federal-regional coordination, a coherent national strategy, and the timely implementation of carbon taxation alongside fossil fuel subsidy phase-outs.

(source: IMF 2025 Belgium Country Report)
