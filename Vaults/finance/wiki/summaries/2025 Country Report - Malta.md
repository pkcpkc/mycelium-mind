# 2025 Country Report - Malta

The [[European Commission]]'s 2025 Country Report on [[Malta]] delivers a rigorous, multi-dimensional evaluation of the island nation's economic, social, and environmental trajectory. Macroeconomic indicators remain highly resilient, with GDP growth projected to sustain a robust pace of approximately 4% through 2026, underpinned by strong domestic demand and service sector exports. The labour market exhibits exceptional performance, featuring one of the EU's highest employment rates (83%) and remarkably low unemployment (3.1%). Concurrently, fiscal consolidation is progressing steadily, with the general government deficit forecast to decline to 3.2% of GDP in 2025 and public debt stabilizing below the 50% threshold.

Despite this strong aggregate growth, structural bottlenecks severely constrain long-term competitiveness and sustainability. [[Research and Innovation]] spending is critically underfunded, registering among the lowest levels in the EU (0.64% of GDP), a situation exacerbated by a severe shortage of highly skilled professionals in STEM fields. The business environment is further hampered by heavy administrative burdens, complex permitting regimes, and a justice system notorious for protracted civil proceedings. While digital public service delivery is advanced, perceptions of regulatory complexity and corruption persist. Although legislative reforms targeting outbound payments are underway to curb aggressive tax planning, risks remain embedded in the corporate tax framework.

Environmental sustainability and the green transition face substantial headwinds. Malta's renewable energy capacity remains minimal, with solar installations constituting a modest share of an electricity mix that still heavily relies on fossil fuels. Persistent fossil fuel subsidies distort market incentives and delay decarbonization efforts. The transport sector emerges as the largest source of emissions, with worsening traffic congestion and an exceptionally low adoption rate of alternative-fuel vehicles. Waste management infrastructure lags behind EU benchmarks, characterized by high per-capita waste generation and low recycling rates. Furthermore, acute water stress threatens agricultural, tourism, and energy sectors, compounded by ongoing compliance gaps in wastewater treatment across key agglomerations.

Social fairness and inclusive growth require immediate policy intervention. While headline poverty rates sit below the EU average, vulnerabilities are intensely concentrated among non-EU nationals, persons with disabilities, and single-parent households. The gender employment and pension gaps remain among the widest in the EU, and child poverty is on a concerning upward trend. The education system struggles with systemic skill mismatches, low participation in vocational and adult learning, and a stratified school structure that amplifies socioeconomic disparities. High out-of-pocket healthcare costs and mounting housing affordability pressures further strain low-income and vulnerable populations.

The [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRF) and Cohesion Policy funds serve as critical catalysts for addressing these challenges, financing targeted reforms in green infrastructure, digital upskilling, healthcare modernization, and social inclusion. Successful and timely execution of these instruments, coupled with accelerated judicial reforms and strategic investments in human capital, will be decisive for [[Malta]] to align with EU sustainability targets, enhance labor market inclusivity, and secure long-term economic resilience.
