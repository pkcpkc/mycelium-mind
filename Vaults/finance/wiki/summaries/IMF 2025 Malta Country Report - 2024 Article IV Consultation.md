# IMF 2025 Malta Country Report - 2024 Article IV Consultation

The 2024 [[Article IV Consultation]] for [[Malta]], conducted by the [[International Monetary Fund]] (IMF), highlights a decade of robust economic expansion driven primarily by export-oriented service sectors like [[Tourism]] and [[Online Gaming]]. While GDP per capita has converged with the EU average, this growth model faces mounting capacity constraints due to an influx of foreign workers and tourists, straining infrastructure and public services. Recent macroeconomic data shows growth moderating from double-digit peaks to an estimated 5% in 2024, with inflation stabilizing near the 2% target. The labor market remains exceptionally tight, heavily reliant on non-EU migration, while the [[Real Estate]] sector shows renewed activity and rising rents.

The IMF Executive Board endorsed the staff appraisal, projecting that [[Malta]] will continue to outperform broader European averages over the medium term, though structural bottlenecks will temper potential growth. Downside risks are tilted to the geopolitical and domestic fronts, including regional conflicts, persistent wage pressures, and potential cyber threats. To ensure sustainable development, the IMF emphasizes a strategic pivot from labor-intensive growth to a productivity-driven model. Key fiscal recommendations include accelerating consolidation under the EU’s new fiscal framework, phasing out fixed [[Energy]] subsidies to redirect resources toward green investment, innovation, and health services, and establishing a long-term fiscal framework aligned with the recently launched [[Malta Vision 2050]]. Additionally, a clear roadmap for [[Corporate Income Tax]] reform must be developed to comply with the EU’s Pillar II directive, preventing revenue leakage to other jurisdictions.

On the financial stability front, while the banking sector remains well-capitalized and liquid, supervisors are urged to tighten macroprudential measures. This involves raising the sectoral systemic risk buffer to address concentrated exposures in residential and commercial [[Real Estate]], closing data gaps, and enhancing cyber risk assessments. Structurally, the report underscores the need to accelerate innovation, expand venture capital support, and reform education to boost STEM enrollment and digital skills. Continued progress in [[Anti-Money Laundering]] and judicial reforms is also critical for investor confidence. Finally, meeting ambitious climate targets requires accelerated adoption of renewable energy and sustainable tourism practices to balance economic gains with environmental resilience.
