# 2025 Country Report - Slovakia

The **2025 Country Report - Slovakia** provides a high-fidelity synthesis of the nation's economic health, structural hurdles, and social progress. The report highlights a period of moderate growth (2.1% in 2024) tempered by significant concerns regarding deteriorating competitiveness, high government debt, and a persistent shortage of skilled labor.

### Economic Outlook and Competitiveness
Slovakia's economy faces a dual challenge: growing inflationary pressures (projected at 4.0% in 2025) and a declining competitive position. Key drivers of this deterioration include:
- **Labor Productivity:** Growth in productivity has failed to keep pace with rising unit labor costs.
- **Innovation Gap:** A significant gap exists between Slovakia and other EU countries regarding R&D expenditure and the adoption of advanced digital technologies (AI, cloud analytics).
- **Investment Barriers:** Fragmented governance, an unpredictable regulatory environment, and high energy costs act as bottlenecks for both public and private investment.

### Fiscal Policy and Sustainability
The report emphasizes the need for a more efficient and fairer tax system. Current issues include:
- **Tax Mix:** A disproportionately high share of revenue comes from labor taxes rather than capital or environmental taxes.
- **Fiscal Deficit:** Despite consolidation measures, the government deficit remains elevated due to permanent spending measures (e.g., 13th pension payment, healthcare spending).
- **Ageing Population:** Rising costs for pensions and long-term care pose significant risks to medium-term fiscal sustainability.

### Innovation, Business Environment, and Productivity
Slovakia ranks as one of the least favorable business environments in the EU. The report identifies several critical areas for reform:
- **Regulatory Burden:** Rapid legislative changes and a lack of binding "better regulation" frameworks create uncertainty for investors.
- **Capital Markets:** The underdeveloped capital market limits access to finance for startups and SMEs, forcing a heavy reliance on bank loans.
- **Digital Transformation:** While there is a promising trend in AI adoption, digital infrastructure—particularly in rural areas—remains subpar compared to EU targets.

### Decarbonization and Energy
Slovakia's industrial sector (30% of GDP) is a major source of emissions. Key findings include:
- **Energy Affordability:** High electricity and gas prices hinder industrial competitiveness.
- **Renewable Energy:** The current share of renewables in the electricity mix (24%) is significantly below the EU average (47%).
- **Critical Raw Materials:** High dependency on imports from non-EU countries creates supply chain vulnerabilities for the green transition.

### Social Fairness and Human Capital
Structural inequalities persist, particularly regarding:
- **Skills Mismatches:** A severe shortage of ICT specialists and healthcare workers hampers growth.
- **Marginalized Groups:** The Roma population remains disproportionately affected by unemployment and social exclusion.
- **Education:** Low proficiency in basic skills among students and a shortage of qualified teachers are primary concerns.
- **Housing:** A severe shortage of social rental housing and high house prices relative to incomes limit affordability.

### Key Recommendations
To improve competitiveness and social fairness, the report suggests:
1. **Accelerating RRP implementation** to foster the green and digital transitions.
2. **Rebalancing the tax mix** to favor capital and environmental taxation over labor taxes.
3. **Strengthening governance** by reforming local administration and improving anti-corruption measures.
4. **Investing in human capital** through targeted upskilling, improved early childhood education, and STEM promotion.
5. **Diversifying energy sources** and accelerating the deployment of renewable infrastructure.
