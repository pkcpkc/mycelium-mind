# IMF 2025 Poland Country Report: Article IV Consultation

The [[Economic Growth]] of the Republic of Poland is projected to accelerate to 3.5% in 2025, driven by a robust rebound in domestic demand, particularly private consumption fueled by strong nominal wage growth and declining inflation. This recovery is further supported by the absorption of [[NGEU Funds]], though net exports remain a drag due to weak Euro Area demand. Despite the positive near-term outlook, medium-term growth is expected to moderate to slightly below 3% due to demographic headwinds and the unwinding of temporary fund inflows.

Fiscal policy presents a significant challenge, with the deficit widening to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 amid expansionary measures, including permanent increases in public sector wages and social benefits, alongside elevated defense spending. The IMF staff recommends a modest fiscal adjustment in 2025 to rebuild buffers and support disinflation, with a medium-term consolidation plan aiming to reduce the deficit to 2.9% by 2028 and stabilize public debt around 60-65% of GDP. The authorities have introduced a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan, though the IMF emphasizes the need to frontload consolidation measures and improve fiscal transparency, including establishing an independent fiscal council.

Inflation has declined substantially from its 2023 peak but remains elevated above the National Bank of Poland's target range, largely due to a tight labor market and strong wage growth. Core inflation is projected to peak before mid-2025 and gradually moderate toward the target by year-end. Consequently, [[Monetary Policy]] should remain appropriately tight, with interest rate cuts delayed until wage growth decelerates and inflation shows a sustained downward trajectory. The IMF notes that fiscal consolidation could create room for additional monetary easing.

The [[Financial Sector Stability]] remains sound, characterized by high capital and liquidity buffers, though private credit recovery is sluggish. Legal risks surrounding legacy Swiss Franc mortgages and potential WIBOR benchmark litigation pose overhangs, though provisioning has mitigated immediate threats. The IMF advises against further regulatory tightening that could stifle credit growth, recommending the removal of distortions like the bank asset tax and allowing mortgage credit holidays to expire. Furthermore, shallow capital markets and low household savings constrain investment, highlighting the need for deeper financial market development.

Structural reforms are critical to sustaining long-term convergence and addressing demographic pressures, particularly [[Population Aging]], which threatens pension adequacy and labor supply. The report underscores the importance of integrating refugees and foreign workers into the labor market, improving vocational training, and enhancing childcare infrastructure. Additionally, securing a credible [[Climate Transition]] is vital for competitiveness, requiring extended carbon pricing, accelerated green project permitting, and phasing out coal dependency while managing social impacts on mining regions. Risks to the outlook remain tilted downside, stemming from potential European slowdowns, geopolitical tensions, and upside inflation risks from supply shocks.
