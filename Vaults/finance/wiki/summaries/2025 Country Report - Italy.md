# 2025 Country Report - Italy

The 2025 Country Report provides a high-fidelity synthesis of Italy's economic, social, and structural landscape. While the country has shown resilience, particularly in employment growth and fiscal balance improvements, it faces significant hurdles regarding productivity, high public debt, and regional disparities.

## Economic Developments and Fiscal Policy
Italy's economic growth remains moderate, supported by the **Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)**. While GDP growth stabilized in 2024, high public debt and a "snow-ball" effect from interest costs remain primary vulnerabilities. The primary budget balance turned positive in 2024 for the first time since 2019, largely due to the phase-out of housing renovation tax credits and energy crisis mitigation measures. However, the debt-to-GDP ratio is projected to rise through 2026 due to lagged fiscal impacts of past tax credits.

## Productivity and Innovation
A persistent productivity gap is driven by:
- **Innovation Capacity:** Low R&D expenditure (0.52% of GDP) and a lack of private investment in high-tech sectors.
- **Skills Mismatch:** Italy has one of the highest skills mismatch rates in the EU, particularly in construction, healthcare, and ICT.
- **Capital Markets:** The Italian stock exchange remains small; the economy is heavily dependent on bank credit, which can limit funding for innovative startups.
- **University System:** Research careers are often unattractive due to low remuneration and job insecurity, hindering the commercialization of research.

## Business Environment and Justice
Italy is making strides in simplifying administrative procedures and reducing the backlog in the judicial system. Key progress includes:
- **Competition:** Implementation of annual competition laws to open up services and retail sectors.
- **Public Procurement:** Reforms aimed at increasing digitalization, transparency, and the speed of contract awards.
- **Digitalization:** Significant progress in 5G coverage (99.5%) and the "IO" app for public services, though rural-urban divides persist.

## Sustainability and Climate Risk
Italy is a leader in clean technologies (solar PV, wind, and heat pumps) but faces extreme climate risks:
- **Climate Vulnerability:** Italy is among the most exposed EU states to extreme weather, with significant economic losses in infrastructure and agriculture.
- **Water Stress:** High demand in agriculture and manufacturing leads to severe water stress, exacerbated by aging infrastructure and leakage.
- **Circular Economy:** Italy performs well in circular material use (20.8%) but faces challenges in waste management infrastructure in the South.
- **Energy Transition:** High electricity prices for industry (driven by gas mix and taxes) stifle competitiveness. Efforts to increase renewable share and PPA (Power Purchase Agreement) uptake are ongoing.

## Social Fairness and Labour Market
The labour market shows robust employment growth but faces structural issues:
- **Demographics:** A shrinking working-age population and a high old-age dependency ratio threaten the pension system's sustainability.
- **Gender Gap:** Female participation remains low due to insufficient childcare and long-term care (LTC) services.
- **In-work Poverty:** High rates of poverty among part-time and fixed-term workers persist, exacerbated by low work intensity and wage stagnation.
- **Regional Disparities:** The "Mezzogiorno" (South) continues to lag significantly in GDP per head, infrastructure quality, and female employment.

## Key Recommendations
To boost competitiveness and social fairness, the report suggests:
- Accelerating RRP implementation, specifically the REPowerEU chapter.
- Shifting the tax burden from labour to other sources to increase growth potential.
- Strengthening the vocational education and training (VET) system to address short-term skills shortages.
- Expanding childcare and LTC services to boost women's labour market participation.
- Implementing a clear industrial strategy to reduce territorial divides and foster innovation in the South.
