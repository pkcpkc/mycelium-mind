# Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)

This document outlines the updated framework issued by `[[S&P Global Ratings]]` on December 18, 2017, for evaluating the creditworthiness of sovereign governments and monetary authorities. The methodology replaces the 2014 criteria and introduces three primary structural changes: capping the local-currency rating at one notch above the foreign-currency rating (previously two), basing the initial fiscal assessment on changes in net rather than gross general government debt, and utilizing Current Account Payments (CAP) instead of Current Account Receipts (CAR) for external ratio denominators.

The core evaluation rests on a five-pillar framework that assigns each factor a score from 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest). These pillars are: `[[Institutional Assessment]]`, which examines policymaking effectiveness, stability, transparency, debt payment culture, and security risks; `[[Economic Assessment]]`, anchored by GDP per capita and adjusted for growth prospects, economic diversity, and data quality; `[[External Assessment]]`, analyzing currency status, liquidity relative to financing needs, and external indebtedness measured via narrow net external debt; `[[Fiscal Assessment]]`, which splits into fiscal performance/flexibility (tracking changes in net debt/GDP and revenue/expenditure flexibility) and `[[Debt Burden]]` (considering debt levels, interest costs, funding structure, and contingent liabilities); and `[[Monetary Assessment]]`, weighing exchange rate regimes (40% weight) against monetary policy credibility, price stability, and financial market depth (60% weight).

The pillar scores are aggregated into an `[[Institutional and Economic Profile]]` and a `[[Flexibility and Performance Profile]]`, which intersect in an indicative rating matrix. Final foreign-currency ratings typically fall within one notch of this matrix, with adjustments for transition dynamics or temporary shifts. Supplemental adjustment factors can override the indicative rating by up to one notch for extreme conditions, such as severely weak external liquidity, high fiscal debt burdens, or imminent event risks. Positive adjustments apply to sovereigns with exceptionally large liquid financial assets.

For local-currency ratings, the document establishes that a one-notch uplift over the foreign-currency rating is permissible only if the sovereign maintains independent monetary policy, possesses deep local capital markets, and has sufficient institutional and fiscal flexibility to prioritize domestic obligations. In distress scenarios (e.g., `[[Foreign-Currency Rating]]` near 'CCC'), the local-currency rating may diverge further if default risks differ materially between currency types. Special provisions address members of `[[Monetary Union]]`s, adjusting their external and monetary scores for policy synchronization and liquidity constraints. The methodology also details the rating of `[[Monetary Authorities]]` within unions, applying weighted average ratings of member sovereigns with uplifts based on the authority's monetary flexibility. Contingent liability risks, particularly from banking systems evaluated via the `[[BICRA]]` framework, are integrated into the fiscal debt burden assessment. The document concludes with comprehensive appendices defining key metrics, glossaries, and references to related rating criteria.
