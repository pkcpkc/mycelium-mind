# EU Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report Malta

The 2025 [[Rule of Law Report]] for Malta outlines a mixed landscape of institutional developments, highlighting both procedural advancements and persistent structural challenges. In the justice sector, perceived judicial independence remains high, with ongoing parliamentary discussions on a constitutional reform to involve the judiciary in appointing the Chief Justice. The transfer of non-summary prosecutions from the Police to the Office of the Attorney General has been completed, though severe resource shortages continue to drive lengthy proceedings and case backlogs. Digitalisation initiatives are underway to modernise court processes, while reforms aim to address the independence of specialised tribunals following critical European Court of Human Rights rulings.

The anti-corruption framework faces significant scrutiny, with public and expert perception of corruption remaining high. While the Financial Crimes Investigation Department and the Attorney General have expanded their capacities, the number of final convictions in high-level cases remains low. The Permanent Commission Against Corruption continues to operate with limited resources and has yet to achieve concrete prosecutorial outcomes. Integrity measures for politicians and high-level officials remain inadequate, particularly regarding asset declarations and lobbying rules. Notably, the European Court of Justice ruled in April 2025 that Malta's investor citizenship scheme violates EU law, marking a major legal setback.

Media freedom and pluralism present critical risks. The government is preparing legislation to expand the media regulator's oversight of market mergers and state advertising, yet no progress has been made on ensuring the editorial independence of public service media. Access to official documents remains restricted, and while a law targeting cross-border [[Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation]] was enacted, broader protections for journalists remain pending. The unresolved assassination of journalist [[Daphne Caruana Galizia]] continues to cast a long shadow over anti-corruption efforts and media safety.

Institutional checks and balances show limited advancement. There is no formal framework for public participation in legislation, and draft laws to establish a [[National Human Rights Institution]] and regulate civil society are still pending. A recent reform increasing the threshold for magisterial inquiries has drawn strong criticism for potentially hindering accountability in high-level offences. Furthermore, the implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments continues to lag, with over two-thirds of recent leading judgments still pending execution. Overall, the report calls for sustained efforts to strengthen judicial efficiency, anti-corruption enforcement, media independence, and democratic safeguards.
