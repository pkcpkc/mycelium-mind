# 2025 Rule of Law Report Slovakia

The 2025 Rule of Law Report by the European Commission provides a critical assessment of Slovakia's rule of law situation, noting that while some legislative adjustments have been made, significant challenges persist across multiple institutional domains. In the [[Justice System]], perceived judicial independence remains low among both the general public and businesses. Proposed amendments to judges' laws aim to improve court efficiency and introduce a second-instance review for disciplinary decisions, but concerns remain regarding the [[Judicial Council]]'s expanded role and insufficient safeguards for dismissing its non-judge members. The power of the [[Prosecutor General]] to annul final prosecutorial decisions continues to raise independence concerns, compounded by the dissolution of specialised anti-corruption bodies like the Special Prosecutor's Office and National Crime Agency, which has led to investigation delays and a notable drop in corruption cases.

Regarding the [[Anti-Corruption Framework]], high perceived corruption levels persist, exacerbated by 2024 criminal law reforms that shortened statutes of limitations and reduced penalties, directly impacting ongoing high-level cases. Although a new National Anti-Corruption Strategy is in preparation, progress on regulating lobbying, strengthening asset declarations, and reforming conflicts of interest rules remains limited. Public procurement reforms have inadvertently lowered oversight safeguards, increasing corruption risks for businesses and deterring investment.

In the realm of [[Media Pluralism and Media Freedom]], the establishment of a new public service broadcaster entity has sparked concerns over political influence and editorial independence. Amendments to the [[Freedom of Information Act]] have introduced potential paywalls for extensive information requests and extended response times, while the safety of journalists has deteriorated, marked by increased verbal attacks, SLAPPs, and abandoned plans for defamation law reform.

Finally, institutional checks and balances face strain due to the frequent use of [[Fast-Track Legislation]], which systematically bypasses meaningful stakeholder consultation. The [[Civil Society]] environment has narrowed, marked by new reporting burdens for NGOs, funding cuts for human rights advocates, and increased political polarisation. Constitutional amendment discussions also raise questions regarding the primacy of EU law. Overall, the Commission recommends that Slovakia address these gaps to align with European standards and restore robust institutional safeguards.
