# EU Commission (2025) 2025 Country Report Austria

The 2025 [[European Commission]] Country Report for [[Austria]] highlights a challenging macroeconomic landscape characterized by a third consecutive year of recession, with GDP projected to contract by 0.3%. The downturn is primarily driven by industrial weakness, declining exports, and persistent cost pressures that have eroded the country's competitiveness since 2019. Despite a rebound in real disposable household incomes, consumer demand remains subdued as savings rates hit multi-decade highs. Concurrently, [[fiscal consolidation]] efforts are strained by rising public expenditure, largely fueled by ageing-related costs in pensions and healthcare, pushing the government deficit well above the 4% GDP threshold.

A critical structural challenge lies in Austria's [[labour market]], which, despite resilient employment rates, faces severe [[skills shortages]] and a continuous decline in average working hours. The widespread adoption of part-time work, particularly among highly educated women, combined with low participation rates for older workers and migrants, constrains economic potential. The report emphasizes the urgent need to expand high-quality early childhood education and care (ECEC) to boost maternal labour force participation and alleviate poverty risks among single-parent households. Additionally, the [[pension system]] faces long-term fiscal sustainability pressures due to relatively early retirement ages and demographic shifts.

On the innovation front, [[Austria]] boasts above-average R&D expenditure and strong science-business collaboration. However, this investment fails to translate into proportional business dynamism or productivity growth. The report identifies a lack of [[venture capital]], limited access to risk financing for SMEs, and a regulatory environment burdened by complex permitting procedures and high administrative costs as primary bottlenecks. Streamlining [[regulatory burden]], modernizing professional services regulations, and fostering a more competitive business environment are highlighted as essential steps to unlock high-impact innovation.

The transition toward a sustainable economy presents both opportunities and hurdles. While [[Austria]] has successfully phased out coal and increased renewable energy generation to 87% of its electricity mix, it faces high industrial energy prices and grid expansion delays. The report stresses the need to accelerate permitting for net-zero industries, diversify gas supplies following the end of Russian imports, and phase out environmentally harmful fossil fuel subsidies. Progress in the [[circular economy]] is noted, though material footprint remains high, requiring increased investment in recycling infrastructure and resource efficiency.

Social fairness indicators show mixed results. While overall poverty rates remain below the EU average, risks are disproportionately high for children, single parents, and individuals with a migrant background. Rising [[housing affordability]] pressures and stagnant wage growth for lower-income groups exacerbate inequality. The report concludes with a comprehensive set of recommendations focusing on prudent fiscal management, targeted tax reforms to shift the burden away from labour, accelerated green and digital transitions, and structural labour market reforms to harness the full potential of Austria's workforce.
