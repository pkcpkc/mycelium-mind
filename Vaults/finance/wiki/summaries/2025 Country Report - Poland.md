# 2025 Country Report - Poland

The European Commission's 2025 Country Report provides a high-fidelity synthesis of Poland's economic landscape, highlighting its position as one of the fastest-growing economies in the EU. While GDP growth is accelerating, driven by strong private consumption and investment, the report identifies several structural challenges regarding competitiveness, fiscal sustainability, and social fairness.

## Economic Growth and Fiscal Challenges
Poland's economy showed robust growth in 2024 (2.9%), with projections for 2025 remaining positive. However, the report notes a rising budget deficit (reaching 5.3% of GDP in 2023), leading to the initiation of an excessive deficit procedure. To counter this, Poland has endorsed a medium-term fiscal-structural plan to reduce the deficit below 3% by 2028. Key fiscal risks include rising defense spending and the need to improve public spending efficiency, particularly in social programs and large-scale infrastructure projects.

## Innovation and Digital Transformation
While Poland is an "emerging innovator," it faces significant hurdles in science-business collaboration and the adoption of advanced digital technologies by SMEs. Only 4.9% of Polish SMEs have adopted AI, compared to an EU average of 12.6%. The report emphasizes the need for:
- **Digital Infrastructure:** Accelerating 5G rollout and increasing digital intensity in the business sector.
- **Research Career Systems:** Improving salaries and mobility to attract top researchers to the public sector.
- **Regulatory Simplification:** Reducing the administrative burden on startups and simplifying the complex tax framework, where Poland currently ranks last in the EU for tax complexity.

## Decarbonisation and Energy Affordability
Poland remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels (70% of electricity generation), which contributes to high electricity prices—among the highest in the EU for industrial consumers. The report highlights:
- **Clean Tech Hub:** Poland is a major manufacturer of lithium batteries (60% of EU production).
- **Energy Transition:** The need to decarbonize the district heating sector (69% coal-reliant) and improve grid flexibility through storage and demand-side response.
- **Fossil Fuel Subsidies:** A call to phase out subsidies for coal mining and diesel in agriculture, which currently represent 0.92% of GDP.

## Skills, Education, and Social Fairness
A critical finding is the decline in basic skills (reading, math, science) among 15-year-olds, exacerbated by inequalities in rural and vocational schools. Key areas for reform include:
- **STEM Enrolment:** Addressing the low proportion of students in natural sciences.
- **Adult Learning:** Boosting participation in lifelong learning, which currently lags significantly behind the EU average.
- **Inclusion:** Closing the disability employment gap (one of the widest in the EU) and improving childcare access to support women's labor market participation.

## Sustainability and Climate Resilience
Poland faces severe risks from water scarcity, droughts, and floods. The report notes:
- **Water Management:** A significant deterioration in the ecological status of surface water bodies.
- **Circular Economy:** Poland's circular material use rate (7.5%) is below the EU average (11.8%).
- **Air Quality:** Significant health impacts from PM2.5 emissions, requiring an estimated €1.6 billion annual investment in pollution prevention.

## Regional Disparities
The report highlights a stark divide between the capital region (Warszawski Stołeczny), which leads in productivity and innovation, and eastern regions like Świętokrzyskie, which suffer from "talent development traps" and higher risk of population decline.
