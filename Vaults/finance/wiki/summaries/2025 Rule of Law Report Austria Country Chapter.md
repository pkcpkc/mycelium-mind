# 2025 Rule of Law Report: Austria Country Chapter

The European Commission’s 2025 [[Rule of Law]] Report delivers a detailed evaluation of Austria’s institutional and legal landscape across four core pillars. In the justice system, perceived judicial independence remains high among both the public and businesses. However, systemic alignment with European standards lags, particularly regarding the appointment of administrative court presidents, where consistent judicial involvement is still absent. Progress toward establishing an independent [[Federal Prosecution Office]] remains limited, though the new government program commits to drafting legislation that would separate prosecutorial supervision from political influence. Currently, prosecutors endure heavy reporting burdens and remain subject to individual instructions by the Minister of Justice, underscoring the urgency of structural reform. While digitalisation advances through the Justice 3.0 initiative and overall case efficiency is strong, challenges persist, including high court fees in complex cases, a Constitutional Court ruling mandating expanded legal aid access, and an estimated need for 200 additional judicial posts to manage workload.

Regarding the anti-corruption framework, Austria continues to score well on corruption perception indices, and its National Anti-Corruption Strategy is actively implemented. Nevertheless, critical gaps remain: the lobbying framework is narrowly scoped and lacks robust enforcement mechanisms, revolving-door rules are minimal, and effective asset declaration systems for Members of Parliament have yet to be enacted. Investigations into high-level corruption proceed, but prosecutors face intense public and political scrutiny. Public procurement is flagged as a high-risk sector for corruption, prompting ongoing oversight by the Court of Audit and competition authorities. Meanwhile, whistleblower channels function effectively, and transparency has improved with the establishment of a public register for parliamentary groups.

On media pluralism and freedom, regulatory bodies like KommAustria and the RTR maintain operational independence. Following a Constitutional Court ruling, the governance structure of public broadcaster ORF was reformed to enhance autonomy. Targeted funding for quality journalism shows positive results, yet the private media sector faces deteriorating economic conditions. State advertising expenditures have doubled, raising concerns about market distortion and political proximity to certain outlets. Preparations for the Freedom of Information Act are on track for a September 2025 launch, and journalist safety measures continue to evolve amid persistent verbal attacks, though strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPPs) remain low.

Finally, institutional checks and balances demonstrate steady improvements. The Ombudsman Board functions effectively, with planned reforms to its nomination procedure to guarantee political independence. The government has pledged transparent, merit-based appointments for high-level independent authority positions, addressing long-standing concerns over politicisation. Austria’s implementation rate for [[European Court of Human Rights]] judgments has risen to 72%, and recent tax reforms for civil society organisations have successfully expanded eligibility for tax-exempt donations, fostering a resilient and open civic space.
