# 2025 Rule of Law Report: Italy

The 2025 assessment evaluates Italy's [[rule of law]] across four pillars, noting institutional progress alongside structural challenges. In the [[justice system]], perceived independence remains average, with the High Council for the Judiciary executing new oversight mandates following comprehensive reforms. A proposed constitutional separation of [[judges]] and prosecutors faces parliamentary review and judicial pushback, while the Constitutional Court validates recent [[tax court]] adjustments. Despite ongoing magistrate recruitment, persistent staffing shortages and technical hurdles delay the full digitalisation of criminal courts. Although [[judicial efficiency]] shows marginal improvement and backlog reduction, the length of proceedings remains a critical concern under Council of Europe supervision.

The [[anti-corruption framework]] shows mixed results: the National Anti-Corruption Plan was updated and ANAC's capacity expanded, yet public perception of corruption remains relatively high. The abrogation of the abuse of public office offence was constitutionally upheld, but corruption convictions declined. While some progress exists on [[conflict of interest]] regulations, comprehensive [[lobbying]] transparency and unified [[political party financing]] registers remain stalled. High-risk vulnerabilities persist in [[public procurement]], particularly regarding organised crime infiltration.

Regarding [[media pluralism]] and freedom, the regulator AGCOM operates independently with stable funding. Public broadcaster RAI secured a licence fee reversal, though governance reforms lag. Digital media concentration rules were modernised, but access to judicial information restrictions and defamation law amendments raise free press concerns. Journalists face escalating threats, legal harassment (SLAPPs), and surveillance via spyware, with protective measures proving insufficient.

Finally, institutional [[checks and balances]] face strain as the government advances a constitutional reform for direct prime ministerial election, amid criticism over excessive urgency decrees. Proposed reforms to the Court of Auditors spark debates on administrative liability, while the lagging implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments highlights accountability gaps. Efforts to establish a [[National Human Rights Institution]] have stalled, and newly enacted security legislation has drawn UN and Council of Europe warnings regarding potential constraints on civic space and fundamental freedoms.
