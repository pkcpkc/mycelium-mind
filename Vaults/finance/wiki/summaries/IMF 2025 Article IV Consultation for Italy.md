# IMF 2025 Article IV Consultation for Italy

The [[IMF]] Executive Board concluded its 2025 Article IV Consultation with [[Italy]], acknowledging the economy's continued moderate expansion and resilient labor market while emphasizing that pressing structural challenges require accelerated reform momentum. In 2024, real [[GDP]] grew by 0.7%, supported by [[National Recovery and Resilience Plan]] (NRRP) infrastructure spending and net exports. The [[Labor Market]] reached record employment levels, with the employment rate hitting 62.7%, though female [[Labor Force Participation]] remains below the [[European Union]] average and youth unemployment persists. Inflation has gradually returned toward the [[ECB]]'s 2% target, settling near 1.7% in mid-2025, while the [[Current Account]] turned to a surplus of 1.1% of GDP.

Fiscal performance improved markedly, with the headline deficit shrinking to 3.4% of GDP and a primary surplus of 0.4% achieved in 2024, driven by strong revenue collection and improved [[Tax Compliance]]. However, [[Public Debt]] remains persistently high at 135.3% of GDP. IMF staff and Executive Directors stress that sustained consolidation is essential to place the debt trajectory firmly on a downward path, recommending an additional primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027. This adjustment should be achieved through rationalizing [[Tax Expenditures]], improving efficiency, and phasing out unproductive incentives, while ensuring that new spending is fully compensated. The authorities' Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) outlines a gradual seven-year adjustment, though staff caution that without identified underlying measures, debt could remain elevated due to adverse interest-growth differentials and demographic spending pressures.

Structural reforms are identified as critical to durably lifting growth and offsetting adverse demographic trends. [[Population Aging]] is intensifying, with the old-age dependency ratio projected to rise sharply, constraining potential growth and straining pension systems. [[Productivity Growth]] has been persistently weak, lagging peer economies due to limited innovation, low R&D investment, and a shortage of high-skilled workers. The IMF emphasizes that full and timely implementation of the NRRP is vital to modernize infrastructure, streamline public administration, and boost human capital. Beyond the NRRP, future reforms must focus on expanding the labor supply (particularly among women and older workers), upgrading skills, reviving private sector dynamism through better access to risk capital, and deepening [[EU]] single market integration. Industrial policy should be carefully targeted and coordinated at the EU level to avoid market distortions.

The [[Financial Sector]] continues to demonstrate robustness, with major banks reporting record profitability, sound asset quality, and ample capital buffers. Non-performing loan ratios remain low and stable. Macroprudential settings are adequate, with the Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB) set at 1% and the Countercyclical Capital Buffer (CCyB) maintained at zero. However, vigilance remains necessary regarding vulnerabilities in Less Significant Institutions (LSIs), the sovereign-bank nexus, and potential loan quality deterioration amid trade uncertainties. The IMF also welcomes progress in strengthening the [[Anti-Money Laundering]] and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) framework.

The near-term outlook is projected to moderate to 0.5% growth in 2025 before a temporary pickup to 0.8% in 2026, supported by peak NRRP spending and favorable trade spillovers. Downside risks significantly outweigh upside potential, driven by escalating global trade tensions, intensifying regional conflicts, tighter financial conditions, and macro-critical climate-related shocks. Additionally, inefficient NRRP implementation or delays in structural reforms could dampen productivity gains. To enhance energy security and mitigate climate risks, accelerating the transition to renewable energy and investing in resilient infrastructure are deemed macro-critical priorities. The IMF recommends that the next Article IV consultation proceed on the standard 12-month cycle.
