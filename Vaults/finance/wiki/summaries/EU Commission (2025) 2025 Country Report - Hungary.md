# EU Commission (2025) 2025 Country Report - Hungary

The 2025 Country Report for Hungary provides a high-fidelity synthesis of the nation's economic standing, highlighting a period of slow recovery characterized by high inflation and significant structural vulnerabilities. While GDP growth is projected to rebound slowly (0.8% in 2025, 2.5% in 2026), the economy faces persistent challenges regarding cost competitiveness, government financing needs, and a distortive business environment.

### Economic Developments and Fiscal Policy
Hungary's economic outlook is clouded by high inflation (which peaked at 17.0% in 2023) and a significant drop in investment, which saw the largest decrease in the EU in 2024. Public debt remains high (73.5% of GDP in 2024) with elevated debt-servicing costs. The report criticizes the lack of a stable medium-term budgetary framework, noting that frequent revisions and the "state of danger" decree allow for excessive expansionary bias and reduced transparency. Furthermore, the widespread use of poorly targeted subsidized loans to households and corporations is noted as a distortion of financial market functioning.

### Innovation, Business Environment, and Productivity
A major finding is that Hungary's labor productivity remains among the lowest in the EU, stagnant at levels seen 15 years ago. This is attributed to a lack of skills and high costs rather than an increase in employment. The business environment is hampered by:
- **Regulatory Volatility:** A surge in decrees over laws and frequent legislative amendments.
- **Market Distortions:** State intervention in business transactions, high corporate taxes on specific sectors (retail, banking, energy), and a lack of equal treatment for companies.
- **R&D Stagnation:** Public R&D spending is among the lowest in the EU (0.4% of GDP), hindering fundamental research and scientific excellence.

### Decarbonisation and Energy
Hungary is a leader in electric vehicle (EV) battery manufacturing but faces a high dependency on Russian fossil fuels. While solar energy production has surged, the grid lacks the necessary flexibility and storage infrastructure to manage intermittent supply. The report highlights a high energy price for businesses, which is one of the highest in the EU, and calls for the phasing out of fossil fuel subsidies that do not address energy poverty.

### Social Fairness and Education
Poverty and social exclusion are on the rise, reversing previous trends. The report identifies significant gaps in:
- **Education:** High rates of early school leaving and a low tertiary education attainment rate (32.3% for 25-34 year-olds).
- **Healthcare:** A hospital-centric system with low spending on prevention and a shortage of health professionals.
- **Housing:** Rapidly increasing house prices (the highest increase in the EU since 2010) and a shortage of social housing.

### Key Findings & Recommendations
The Commission recommends that Hungary:
1. **Accelerate the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP)** and cohesion policy implementation.
2. **Reform the pension system** to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability.
3. **Strengthen the anti-corruption framework** and improve judicial independence.
4. **Simplify the tax system** and reduce sector-specific taxes to create a level playing field.
5. **Invest in water resilience** and transition to a circular economy.
6. **Improve social dialogue** with employers and trade unions, particularly in the public sector.
