# 2025 Rule of Law Report: Romania

The European Commission's 2025 [[Rule of Law Report]] for [[Romania]] provides a comprehensive assessment of the country's institutional landscape, highlighting both progress and persistent challenges across four key pillars. 

In the [[Justice System]], significant strides have been made in addressing the [[Venice Commission]]'s recommendations on the [[Justice Laws]], with legislative steps underway to extend the mandates of high-ranking prosecutors and strengthen safeguards for judicial police independence. Perceived judicial independence remains average, though transparency in disciplinary sanctions has improved. The [[Ministry of Justice]] is evaluating a centralized IT system reform, with commitments to preserve judicial independence. Quality and efficiency metrics show mixed results: while new recruitments have improved occupancy rates for judges and prosecutors, the length of civil and administrative proceedings has increased, and court backlogs remain substantial.

The [[Anti-Corruption Framework]] maintains a positive operational track record, with the [[National Anti-Corruption Directorate]] (DNA) securing numerous convictions. However, systemic risks have emerged following High Court of Cassation and Justice rulings on the [[Statute of Limitations]], which have led to the closure of many corruption cases and annulled convictions. The [[National Integrity Agency]] (ANI) continues to manage asset declarations and conflicts of interest effectively, though a Constitutional Court ruling on spousal/children's asset declarations poses transparency challenges. Progress on lobbying regulations for Members of Parliament and political party financing transparency remains stagnant. Corruption risks in [[Public Procurement]] persist, prompting ongoing digitalization efforts under the Recovery and Resilience Plan.

Regarding [[Media Pluralism]] and [[Media Freedom]], no progress has been made on securing editorial independence for public service media. The [[National Audiovisual Council]] (CNA) faces resource constraints, while opaque political advertising and state funding continue to threaten journalistic independence. Transparency of media ownership remains a high risk, and threats or harassment against journalists persist, alongside concerns over [[Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation]] (SLAPPs).

Under [[Other Institutional Issues]], the report notes concerning legislative unpredictability and the frequent use of [[Government Emergency Ordinances]], which undermines regulatory stability. The implementation of [[European Court of Human Rights]] judgments remains slow, with over 100 leading judgments pending. Institutional governance was marked by the Constitutional Court's cancellation of the 2024 Presidential election first round due to electoral irregularities, followed by a repeat in May 2025. Accreditation for two [[National Human Rights Institutions]] remains stalled, and the [[Civic Space]] continues to face structural challenges, prompting the adoption of a 2025-2030 Strategy for Open Government to mitigate NGO funding and operational barriers.

(source: [EU Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report Romania](https://commission.europa.eu/publications/2025-rule-law-report-romania_en))
