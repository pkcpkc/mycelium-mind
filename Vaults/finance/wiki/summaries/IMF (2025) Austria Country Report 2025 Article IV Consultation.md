# IMF (2025) Austria Country Report: 2025 Article IV Consultation

The document presents the findings of the [[IMF Article IV Consultation]] for [[Austria]], evaluating a challenging post-recession economic environment shaped by two consecutive years of contraction triggered by energy price shocks, euro area [[Monetary Policy]] tightening, and subdued domestic and external demand. Despite a marginal recovery in 2025Q1, [[GDP Growth]] is projected to remain near zero for the year before gradually rebounding in 2026, constrained by planned [[Fiscal Consolidation]], heightened global trade policy uncertainty, and sluggish productivity. Inflation, while easing from recent peaks, remains structurally elevated at approximately 3 percent due to sticky services prices and sustained nominal wage growth (7-8%), consistently outpacing the euro area average. The fiscal deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, pushing [[Public Debt]] to 81% of GDP. The IMF advises near-term fiscal flexibility to permit automatic stabilizers to operate during downside scenarios, while strongly recommending a bold medium-term consolidation framework to reduce the deficit below 2% of GDP. Priority structural spending reforms include gradually lowering high pension replacement rates, capping public sector wage increases, enhancing healthcare efficiency, and eliminating environmentally harmful subsidies. Revenue-side recommendations emphasize broadening the tax base through increases in property, inheritance, gift, and excise taxes.

Structurally, [[Austria]] faces pronounced [[Demographic Aging]] headwinds, with aging-related expenditures projected to add roughly 2% of GDP to fiscal pressures by 2030. The report underscores that expanding labor supply—particularly among women and elderly workers, scaling childcare and eldercare infrastructure, and improving immigrant integration—could offset more than two decades of demographic decline. Reducing regulatory barriers in services, accelerating [[Green Transition]] project approvals, and alleviating housing supply bottlenecks are identified as vital levers for restoring productivity. The analysis also highlights the necessity of deepening the [[EU Single Market]], notably through a Savings and Investment Union and a harmonized 28th corporate regime, to unlock economies of scale and enhance capital market financing for startups.

Financially, the banking sector maintains robust [[Financial Sector Stability]], characterized by a CET1 capital ratio of approximately 17.7% and strong profitability driven by CESEE operations and wide net interest margins. Nevertheless, vulnerabilities are emerging in the [[Commercial Real Estate Risk]] segment, with non-performing loan ratios climbing to roughly 5% amid past rate hikes and declining collateral values. The IMF recommends preserving prudent macroprudential oversight, including legislating borrower-based mortgage limits (KIM-V regulation) as permanent instruments prior to their July 2025 expiration, closing CRE data gaps, and urging banks to retain profits for capital buffers and cybersecurity resilience. Systemic risks are tilted downside, primarily stemming from potential escalation in global trade tensions, protracted weak economic sentiment, and persistent inflation differentials that threaten long-term competitiveness. Austrian authorities broadly concur with staff assessments, pledging targeted fiscal adjustments, comprehensive labor market reforms, and sustained cooperation on financial stability and anti-corruption initiatives.
