# IMF 2025 Romania Article IV Consultation Report

The [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV consultation with [[Romania]] highlights a period of economic adjustment marked by sluggish growth, widening twin deficits, and necessary fiscal consolidation. Following a 2024 real GDP growth of just 0.9%, the economy is projected to gradually recover at 1.0% in 2025 and 1.4% in 2026, supported by accelerated [[NGEU]]-funded investments and a moderation in private consumption. The primary macroeconomic challenge stems from deteriorating public finances, driven by costly pension reforms and rising public wages, which pushed the fiscal deficit to 8.7% of GDP in 2024 and widened the current account deficit to 8.2%. To address these imbalances, the Romanian authorities have implemented a comprehensive fiscal reform package for 2025–26, including VAT rate increases, higher excise and dividend taxes, and a freeze on public sector wages and pensions. The immediate goal is to reduce the deficit below 6% of GDP by 2026, though further medium-term adjustments targeting a deficit below 3% by 2030 are deemed critical to stabilize public debt and restore market confidence.

Inflation remains a persistent concern, with headline prices surging to 9.9% in September 2025 following the removal of electricity price caps and tax adjustments. Core inflation remains elevated due to strong nominal wage growth and services demand. Consequently, the [[National Bank of Romania]] (NBR), under Governor [[Mugur Isarescu]], has adopted a cautious monetary stance, pausing policy rate cuts and maintaining the benchmark rate at 6.5%. The [[IMF]] staff recommends that rate reductions should only resume once inflation and wage growth show sustained moderation, while also advocating for a gradual increase in exchange rate flexibility to enhance resilience against external shocks and address competitiveness issues.

The financial sector exhibits robust fundamentals, with banks maintaining high capital adequacy and liquidity buffers. However, emerging vulnerabilities include a growing sovereign-bank nexus, sizable unhedged foreign exchange corporate loans, and buoyant consumer credit growth. Supervisory vigilance is emphasized to monitor asset quality and calibrate macroprudential policies. Structurally, the report underscores the urgency of accelerating reforms to unlock stalled EU funds, improve public investment management, and enhance state-owned enterprise (SOE) governance. Boosting labor force participation, particularly among women, and advancing the transition to a low-carbon economy through mechanisms like the EU Emissions Trading System 2 (ETS2) are highlighted as vital for long-term growth potential. While risks to the growth outlook are tilted downward due to geopolitical uncertainties and potential trade barriers, upside inflation risks remain anchored by domestic wage pressures and energy price volatility. The authorities broadly concur with the staff's assessment, reaffirming their commitment to fiscal discipline, structural modernization, and securing Romania's economic stability amid a challenging global environment.
