# IMF 2025 France Country Report: Article IV Consultation

The [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation for [[France]] underscores the economy's resilience in 2024, driven by the Paris Olympics and robust labor market conditions, despite prevailing high uncertainty and tight financial conditions. Real GDP growth is projected to moderate to 0.6% in 2025 before recovering to 1% in 2026, with disinflation progressing steadily and inflation expectations anchored. However, the report highlights significant headwinds, including geoeconomic fragmentation, trade tensions, and domestic policy uncertainty, which weigh on private investment and consumer confidence.

A central focus of the consultation is the urgent need for fiscal consolidation to address high and rising public debt, which reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024. Following fiscal slippages in 2023 and 2024, the French authorities have committed to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), aiming to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. The IMF staff recommends a frontloaded structural fiscal effort, emphasizing rationalization of current spending across central, local, and social security levels rather than further tax increases, given France's already high tax-to-GDP ratio. Implementation risks remain elevated due to political dynamics and potential revenue shortfalls.

Structural reforms are identified as critical for sustaining long-term growth and facilitating fiscal adjustment. France is encouraged to leverage its position in the [[Green Transition]] and [[Artificial Intelligence]] sectors by enhancing innovation financing, streamlining R&D tax expenditures, and advancing the [[EU Savings and Investment Union]] to improve capital allocation. Labor market reforms are also prioritized, including measures to increase female labor force participation, integrate migrants, and address skill mismatches. The [[Pension Reform]] of 2023, which raised the retirement age to 64, is noted as a necessary step to counter demographic pressures, though further harmonization and healthy aging policies are recommended.

The financial sector demonstrates robust resilience, with banks maintaining strong capital and liquidity buffers post-2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP). The IMF commends the macroprudential framework but stresses the need for continued monitoring of non-bank financial institutions, cyber risks, and climate-related vulnerabilities. Given France's deep integration with the European Union, deeper single market integration and coordinated industrial policies are highlighted as vital for reducing fragmentation and boosting competitiveness.

Overall, the report presents a balanced assessment: while short-term growth faces headwinds and fiscal dynamics remain challenging, France's strong institutional framework and committed reform agenda provide a pathway toward sustainable debt trajectories and enhanced productivity.
