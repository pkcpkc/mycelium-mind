# IMF (2025) Slovakia Country Report

The IMF's 2025 [[Article IV Consultation]] for the [[Slovak Republic]] highlights an economy navigating a complex transition characterized by modest growth recovery, persistent inflationary pressures, and significant medium-term fiscal challenges. Following a sharp slowdown in 2022–2023, growth accelerated to 2.0 percent in 2024, driven by robust private consumption fueled by real wage growth and expanded pension benefits. However, the near-term outlook points to a deceleration to 1.8 percent in 2025 due to planned [[fiscal consolidation]], with a projected rebound to 2.1 percent in 2026 as external demand improves and EU-funded investments continue. Inflation, which peaked above 15 percent in early 2023, has largely moderated but experienced a temporary uptick in late 2024 and early 2025 due to higher indirect taxes and global food prices, with projections indicating a gradual return to the 2 percent target by early 2027.

A central focus of the report is the necessity for aggressive [[fiscal consolidation]] to reverse a widening deficit that reached 5.8 percent of GDP in 2024. The Slovak government’s four-year plan aims to reduce the headline deficit to 2.2 percent of GDP by 2028, a target endorsed by IMF staff as appropriately ambitious. To achieve this, the report recommends prioritizing expenditure-based measures over revenue increases to maintain a balanced approach, suggesting reforms such as reducing VAT exemptions, implementing market-value-based property taxes, trimming departmental budgets, and phasing out temporary energy support. Additionally, safeguarding the national fiscal framework by reforming the constitutional debt brake before 2026 and strengthening the independent Council for Budgetary Responsibility is deemed critical to prevent disruptive consolidation and ensure long-term debt sustainability.

The [[Financial sector resilience]] assessment indicates a banking system that remains well-capitalized and liquid, with stress tests confirming its ability to withstand severe macro-financial shocks. Nevertheless, vulnerabilities persist in the residential and commercial real estate (CRE) markets, particularly regarding upcoming mortgage repricing and elevated office vacancy rates. The report advises maintaining the current macroprudential stance while exploring the adoption of a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer (pnCCyB) and closing loopholes in borrower-based measures. It also highlights the need to enhance supervision of less significant institutions and strengthen the crisis management framework, including ensuring the operational independence and legal protection of supervisors.

Structurally, the Slovak economy faces headwinds from an aging population, labor shortages, and over-reliance on the automotive sector. To sustain productivity growth and diversify the economy, the IMF stresses the importance of accelerating innovation, expanding Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs), and deepening integration with the European single market to foster cross-border capital flows. Addressing demographic pressures requires policies to boost labor force participation among women and the elderly, streamline migrant integration, and reform pension systems. Furthermore, governance reforms are urgently needed to combat corruption, enhance judicial independence, and improve public administration efficiency, as weak institutional quality currently threatens the disbursement of critical EU funds. On the environmental front, Slovakia is urged to expeditiously implement the EU’s ETS II scheme for transport and buildings while gradually raising environmental levies to meet its 2030 and 2050 climate targets.
