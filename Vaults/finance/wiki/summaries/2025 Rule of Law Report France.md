# 2025 Rule of Law Report: France

The **[[2025 Rule of Law Report]]** for **[[France]]**, published by the European Commission on 8 July 2025, provides a comprehensive assessment of the rule of law situation in the country. The report highlights significant developments across judicial reforms, anti-corruption frameworks, media pluralism, and institutional checks and balances. While France has made progress in digitalising the justice system, enhancing anti-corruption structures, and improving media ownership transparency, it faces ongoing challenges regarding the efficiency of courts, the perception of judicial independence, and the practical implementation of rights for journalists.

## Justice System
The level of perceived judicial independence in France remains average among both the general public and businesses. The **[[Law of 20 November 2023]]** reforming the status of magistrates is being implemented, featuring changes such as the repeal of age limits for admission and the introduction of 360° evaluations for court presidents. The **[[Superior Council of Magistracy]]** has actively defended judicial independence, particularly in response to personal attacks on magistrates following decisions related to national elections. Budgetary efforts are focused on increasing the number of magistrates and improving digitalisation through tools like **[[Portalis]]**, though implementation speed and technical issues persist. Efficiency remains a concern, with high average times to resolve cases, particularly in bribery and family law matters. The Ministry of Justice has launched "flash missions" to explore solutions for reducing backlogs and improving sentence execution. France continues to face challenges in implementing **[[European Court of Human Rights]]** judgments, with a significant number of leading judgments pending.

## Anti-Corruption Framework
France maintains a relatively low perception of corruption, scoring 67/100 in the **[[Corruption Perceptions Index]]**. The **[[Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA)]]** has been reorganised to enhance effectiveness, separating public and private sector functions. The **[[High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP)]]** has expanded its resources to handle increased workloads, including the examination of asset declarations. Prosecution efforts have intensified, with additional resources allocated to the **[[Central Office for Combating Corruption and Tax Offences (OCLCIFF)]]** and the **[[National Financial Prosecutor (PNF)]]**. New ethics measures have been adopted for Members of Parliament, ministers, and magistrates. Whistleblower reports are increasing, prompting calls for better support and protection. Initiatives are underway to combat corruption linked to organised drug trafficking and port activities.

## Media Pluralism and Media Freedom
The media regulator, **[[ARCOM]]**, has expanded its scope under the **[[Digital Services Act (DSA)]]** and is preparing for the **[[European Media Freedom Act (EMFA)]**. Significant progress has been made in improving media ownership transparency, with a national database currently in preparation. The proposed reform of public broadcasting governance, aiming to create a holding company named **[[France Médias]]**, has sparked debate regarding editorial autonomy and job security, leading to calls for strikes. Journalists report difficulties in exercising the right to access official documents, with the Council of State's recent decisions raising concerns about potential barriers. A legislative proposal based on the **[[États généraux de l'information]]** consultation is being prepared to safeguard the right to information. Additionally, a bill on neighbouring rights seeks to ensure fair compensation for press content used by digital platforms.

## Checks and Balances
Legislative simplification efforts are welcomed by businesses, though concerns persist regarding the consultation process and the use of accelerated procedures. The financial environment for civil society organisations is stable, but the civic space is considered narrowed, with reports of intimidation and attacks. Discussions are ongoing regarding the use of **[[Artificial Intelligence (AI)]]** in the justice system, with studies launched to ensure appropriate safeguards. The **[[National Consultative Commission on Human Rights (CNC DH)]]** highlights the need for better implementation of its recommendations and protection of human rights defenders.
