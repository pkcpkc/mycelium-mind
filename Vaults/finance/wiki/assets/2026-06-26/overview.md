### Overview

![Sovereign Issuer Criteria Framework](overview.png)

### Institutional assessment

The institutional assessment reflects our view of how a government's institutions and policymaking affect a sovereign's credit fundamentals by:

* delivering sustainable public finances,
* promoting balanced economic growth, and
* responding to economic or political shocks.

It also reflects our view of the transparency and accountability of data, processes, and institutions; a sovereign's debt repayment culture; and potential external and domestic security risks.

Example for a question to be answered: To which extent can one count on a country's policies to remain stable?

### Economic assessment

* the country's income levels as measured by its GDP per capita, indicating broader potential tax and funding bases upon which to draw, which generally support creditworthiness,
* growth prospects, and
* economic diversity and volatility.

Example for a question to be answered: To which extent does future economic growth expand a country's tax base?

### External assessment

A country's external assessment, which refers to the transactions and positions of all residents (public- and private-sector entities) vis-à-vis the rest of the world, is primarily driven by our view of:

* the status of a sovereign's currency in international transactions,
* the country's external liquidity, which provides an indication of the economy's ability to generate the foreign exchange necessary to meet its public- and private-sector obligations to nonresidents, and
* the country's external position, which shows residents' assets and liabilities (in both foreign and local currency) relative to the rest of the world.

Example for a question to be answered: To which extent does a country need inflows of foreign currency in order to meet financial obligations denominated in this foreign currency?

### Fiscal assessment

The fiscal assessment reflects our view of the sustainability of a sovereign's deficits and its debt burden:

* fiscal flexibility, long-term fiscal trends and vulnerabilities,
* debt structure and funding access, and
* potential risks arising from contingent liabilities.

Given the many dimensions that this assessment captures, the analysis is divided into two segments, "fiscal performance and flexibility" and "debt burden."

The fiscal assessment is, obviously, the heart of the rating process.

### Monetary assessment

The monetary assessment considers our view of the monetary authority's ability to fulfill its mandate while sustaining a balanced economy and attenuating any major economic or financial shocks.

We derive the monetary assessment by analyzing:

* the exchange rate regime, which influences a sovereign's ability to coordinate monetary policy with fiscal and other economic policies to support sustainable economic growth, and
* the credibility of monetary policy as measured, among other factors, by inflation trends over an economic cycle and the effects of market-oriented monetary mechanisms on the real economy, which is largely a function of the depth and diversification of a country's financial system and capital markets.

Example for a question to be answered by the monetary assessment: To which extent does an increase in a country's money supply affect the country's exchange rate and inflation?

### Institutional assessment - details

The assessment combines the following elements:

* Effectiveness, stability, and predictability of policymaking, political institutions, and civil society
* Transparency and accountability of institutions, data, and processes.
  These criteria are further defined in a table listing a set of "indicators". See Table 2 on page 8 of the methodology document.

The final step of the institutional assessment is an adjustment of an initial assessment to account for the following issues:

* Debt payment culture. A sovereign with a weak debt payment culture receives an institutional assessment of '6'.
* External security risks. The institutional assessment is one or two categories worse than the initial assessment when there is risk of war, but the risk is not expected to materialize within two to three years.

### Fiscal assessment - details: Contingent liabilities

There are three main types of contingent liabilities:

* financial institutions' related contingent liabilities (financial institutions comprise public and private depository corporations and nondepository financial institutions),
* contingent liabilities related to nonfinancial public-sector enterprises (NFPEs),
* guarantees and off-budget contingent liabilities.

The first type of contingent liabilities is the subject of a separate assessment: Banking Industry Country Risk Analysis (BICRA).

### Monetary assessment - details:

The two elements of the monetary assessment concern:

* the exchange rate regime of a country, and
* the credibility of its monetary policy.

### Monetary assessment: The credibility of monetary policy

Here are some criteria for assessing the credibility of monetary policy:

* independence of the monetary authority,
* effectiveness and toolkit of monetary policy,
* price stability, as it is observed in the country,
* can the central bank act as lender of last resort?
* depth and breadth of the financial markets and the financial system more generally.

See Table 8b on page 28 of the rating methodology document.

### Supplemental adjustment factors - details:

* extremely weak external liquidity,
* extremely high fiscal debt burden,
* very high institutional risk and high debt burden,
* "event" risk.

### Upshot

The rating process is complex. It is based on a lot of "received wisdom", - discussed in the methodology document.

There are many areas in which the rating process is not fully spelled out. This leaves some necessary room for discretion, but it generates a need to track how the discretion is being used.

How replicable are sovereign credit ratings?

## Room for discretionary judgement

### Example: Institutional assessment

* Relies on subjective factors like:

  + "Effectiveness, stability, and predictability of policymaking"
  + "Transparency and accountability of institutions"
  + "Debt payment culture"
* Example: "Cohesive civil society".

### Explicit room for discretionary judgement

* "Supplemental adjustment factors" are applied at analyst discretion.

  + "Event risk"
  + "Extremely weak external liquidity"
  + "Very high institutional risk"
* The indicative rating can be overridden based on unspecified factors.

  + Ratings may differ by more than one notch from the indicative rating if analysts deem it necessary.
