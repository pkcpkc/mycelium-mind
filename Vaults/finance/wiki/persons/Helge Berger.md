## Biography & Context

Helge Berger is an International Monetary Fund (IMF) official who has played a central role in the Fund's 2025 Article IV Consultations for France, Italy, and Romania. His work has focused extensively on evaluating macroeconomic trajectories, fiscal consolidation challenges, and structural reform imperatives across these major European economies amid high uncertainty, tight financial conditions, and geoeconomic fragmentation.

In the context of France, Berger's analysis emphasized urgent needs for pension adjustments, labor market integration, and strategic investments in AI and the green transition, alongside careful oversight of financial sector stability and debt sustainability. Concurrently, his involvement in the Italy consultation highlighted the country's moderate economic growth and improved fiscal performance, while underscoring the pressing need for accelerated structural reforms to address demographic headwinds, weak productivity growth, and persistently high public debt.

In the 2025 Romania Article IV Consultation, Berger assessed Romania's economic adjustment amid sluggish growth and widening twin deficits. The analysis highlighted the urgent need for fiscal consolidation following costly pension reforms and rising public wages, which pushed the deficit to 8.7% of GDP in 2024. Berger's recommendations emphasized reducing the deficit below 6% by 2026 through measures including VAT increases and public sector wage freezes, while addressing structural bottlenecks such as stalled EU funds, state-owned enterprise governance, and the need to boost labor force participation, particularly among women. His work in Romania underscores a consistent focus on stabilizing public debt and enhancing growth potential through deep structural modernization.

Berger's synthesis across these consultations underscores a consistent IMF focus on rationalizing tax expenditures, modernizing public administration via National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) implementation, expanding labor force participation, and navigating external shocks to secure long-term growth potential. His research and policy recommendations consistently bridge macroeconomic stability with mid-term structural modernization, positioning him as a key analyst on European fiscal and labor market dynamics.
