# Inflation Targeting

## Summary

Inflation targeting is a monetary policy framework where a central bank publicly announces and commits to a specific numerical inflation rate, using interest rates and other macroeconomic tools to steer aggregate demand and anchor long-term price expectations. Successful implementation relies on transparent communication, independent central banking, and unimpeded policy transmission channels. In recent international assessments, such as the [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV consultations with Hungary and Poland, this framework is closely monitored to ensure that persistent inflation deviations are addressed through appropriate monetary stances and structural adjustments. For example, the [[National Bank of Poland]] recently navigated elevated core inflation driven by a tight labor market and strong wage growth, maintaining a restrictive stance until sustained disinflation trajectories were confirmed. The framework also demonstrates strong interplay with fiscal policy, where coordinated consolidation can alleviate demand pressures and create necessary room for subsequent monetary easing.

## Key Details

- **Explicit Numerical Targets:** Central banks define a clear, publicly communicated inflation goal (e.g., 3 percent), which serves as the primary anchor for market expectations and wage/price setting behavior.
- **Policy Transmission Integrity:** Effective targeting requires that interest rate changes fully pass through to lending, deposit, and bond markets; regulatory measures like margin caps, subsidized lending, or price controls can disrupt these channels and delay inflation convergence.
- **Data-Dependent Flexibility:** While committed to a target, central banks typically employ a forward-looking, data-driven approach that allows for temporary deviations in response to supply shocks, exchange rate movements, or growth forecasts.
- **Exchange Rate as Shock Absorber:** Floating exchange rates often complement inflation targeting by allowing external imbalances to adjust naturally, reducing the need for abrupt domestic interest rate hikes.
- **Macroeconomic Stabilization Benefits:** By reducing uncertainty and anchoring expectations, inflation targeting lowers sovereign risk premia, encourages long-term investment, and creates a stable environment for fiscal consolidation and structural reforms.
- **Labor Market and Wage Dynamics Integration:** Inflation trajectories are frequently influenced by domestic labor market tightness and nominal wage growth, requiring central banks to maintain restrictive stances until wage pressures decelerate and align with the target range.
- **Fiscal-Monetary Policy Synergy:** Coordinated fiscal consolidation and structural adjustments can significantly impact inflation dynamics by reducing aggregate demand pressures, thereby creating necessary policy space for subsequent monetary easing while maintaining the inflation target.
- **Contemporary Policy Execution:** Recent central bank assessments highlight how target achievement depends on sustained disinflation trajectories, with rate cuts typically delayed until core inflation peaks and demonstrates consistent downward momentum toward the designated range.
