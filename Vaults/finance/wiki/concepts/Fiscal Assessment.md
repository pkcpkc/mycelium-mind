# Fiscal Assessment

## Summary

Fiscal Assessment is a core evaluation pillar within S&P Global Ratings' sovereign creditworthiness framework. It determines the sustainability of public finances by systematically evaluating a government's fiscal health, including its ability to manage public finances, service debt obligations, and maintain flexibility during economic stress. The assessment is scored on a scale from 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest) and contributes directly to the broader Institutional and Economic Profile used in rating determinations. Structurally, it is divided into two segments: fiscal flows (assessing flexibility, long-term trends, and vulnerabilities) and fiscal stocks (evaluating the debt burden, funding access, and contingent liabilities).

## Key Details

- **Dual-Component Structure**: Divides into fiscal flows (performance/flexibility) and fiscal stocks (debt burden/contingent liabilities).
- **Fiscal Flows & Flexibility**: Tracks changes in net debt-to-GDP ratios over time and evaluates the flexibility of government revenues and expenditures to adjust during economic downturns or shocks.
- **Fiscal Stocks & Debt Burden**: Considers absolute debt levels, interest costs relative to GDP, funding structure resilience, and the magnitude of contingent liabilities.
- **Net vs. Gross Debt Focus**: The 2017 methodology update shifted the initial fiscal baseline to focus on changes in *net* general government debt rather than gross debt, providing a more accurate view of true fiscal burden and policy impact.
- **Contingent Liability Integration**: Risks from off-balance-sheet obligations are evaluated and integrated into the debt burden score. These liabilities are further segmented into banking sector exposures (evaluated via BICRA), non-financial public enterprises, and off-budget guarantees.
- **Scoring & Aggregation**: Scores from this pillar are aggregated with Institutional and Economic Assessments to form the Institutional and Economic Profile, which intersects with flexibility metrics in the indicative rating matrix.
- **Methodology Context**: Part of the S&P sovereign credit rating framework published in 2017, which explicitly acknowledges room for analyst discretion despite standardized metrics.
