# Commercial Real Estate Risk

## Summary
Commercial Real Estate (CRE) risk refers to the financial vulnerabilities associated with property investments, lending, and collateral valuation, which intensify during periods of monetary tightening, economic contraction, or declining asset prices. In recent macroeconomic assessments, this segment has emerged as a distinct vulnerability within the broader banking sector, requiring targeted macroprudential oversight to prevent systemic spillovers.

## Key Details
- **Non-Performing Loan (NPL) Increase:** CRE-related NPL ratios have climbed to approximately 5%, indicating rising credit stress among property borrowers.
- **Primary Drivers:** The risk is largely attributed to past interest rate hikes and corresponding declines in commercial property collateral values, which have compressed borrower equity and reduced refinancing options.
- **Macroeconomic Context:** The vulnerability is situated within a post-recession environment characterized by subdued demand, high inflation, and planned fiscal consolidation, which collectively dampen property market liquidity and tenant profitability.
- **Regulatory & Oversight Recommendations:** Authorities are advised to close CRE data gaps, maintain prudent macroprudential frameworks, and permanently legislate borrower-based mortgage limits (e.g., KIM-V regulation) to curb excessive leverage. Banks are also encouraged to retain earnings to bolster capital buffers and enhance cybersecurity resilience.
