# Labour Productivity

## Summary

Labour productivity is a key indicator of economic efficiency, measuring the output produced per unit of labor input. In the context of the French economy, it serves as a critical metric for assessing competitiveness and industrial health. Current structural weaknesses, including high taxes on production and a significant lack of skilled labor, continue to weigh heavily on the country's productivity levels.

## Key Details

- **Structural Inhibitors:** High production taxes and a persistent "skills gap" are identified as primary factors suppressing productivity growth in France.
- **Skills Shortages:** High vacancy rates in critical sectors such as automotive, construction, and care hinder the ability of firms to optimize labor output.
- **Innovation Linkage:** Stagnating R&D intensity (currently 1.44% for businesses) limits the technological advancement necessary to drive long-term productivity gains.
- **SME Barriers:** Small and medium-sized enterprises face specific hurdles, such as late payments and a lack of basic digitalization, which prevent them from achieving higher productivity.
- **Policy Recommendations:** To improve productivity, the report suggests aligning production taxes with EU averages and implementing targeted upskilling for green and digital transitions.
