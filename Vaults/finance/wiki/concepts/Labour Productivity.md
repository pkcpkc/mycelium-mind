# Labour Productivity

## Summary

Labour productivity is a key indicator of economic efficiency, measuring the output produced per unit of labor input. In the context of the French economy, it serves as a critical metric for assessing competitiveness and industrial health. While France's competitiveness is showing signs of improvement due to lower inflation relative to trading partners and certain tax reductions, labor productivity growth remains a significant structural concern.

## Key Details

- **Structural Inhibitors:** High production taxes and a persistent "skills gap" are identified as primary factors suppressing productivity growth in France.
- **Skills Shortages:** High vacancy rates in critical sectors such as automotive, construction, and care hinder the ability of firms to optimize labor output. A significant gap exists in the availability of adequately trained workers across these industries.
- **Innovation Linkage:** Stagnating R&D intensity (currently 1.44% for businesses) limits the technological advancement necessary to drive long-term productivity gains. The report notes that intensity remains below the 3% target, partly due to a lack of efficiency in public support schemes like the *crédit impôt recherche* (CIR).
- **SME Barriers:** Small and medium-sized enterprises face specific hurdles, such as late payments and a lack of basic digitalization (only 68.5% have reached basic levels), which prevent them from achieving higher productivity.
- **Corporate Debt:** High indebtedness levels among non-financial corporations (NFCs) are noted as a factor that can hamper investment and resilience, indirectly impacting productivity potential.
- **Policy Recommendations:** To improve productivity, the report suggests aligning production taxes with EU averages, improving academia-business collaboration, and implementing targeted upskilling for green and digital transitions.
