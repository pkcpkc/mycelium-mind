# Population Aging

## Summary

Population aging refers to a structural demographic shift where the median age of a population increases due to declining fertility rates and rising life expectancy. This trend fundamentally alters labor market dynamics, reduces the working-age population share, and creates long-term fiscal pressures on public finances. In contemporary economic planning, it also serves as a primary driver for structural reforms aimed at sustaining long-term growth, maintaining labor supply adequacy, and offsetting medium-term growth moderation.

## Key Details

- **Demographic Metrics**: Characterized by a sharp rise in the old-age dependency ratio, indicating a growing proportion of retirees relative to the working-age population.
- **Economic Impact**: Constrains potential GDP growth by tightening labor supply and reducing overall workforce participation rates, often leading to moderated medium-term economic convergence.
- **Fiscal & Systemic Strain**: Places significant and escalating pressure on public pension systems, healthcare expenditures, and long-term debt sustainability.
- **Policy & Structural Responses**: Drives the urgent need for structural reforms, including measures to expand labor supply (particularly among women and older workers), enhance skills training, and boost productivity growth to offset demographic headwinds.
- **Labor Market Integration & Support Systems**: Necessitates targeted interventions such as integrating foreign workers and refugees into the labor market, expanding vocational training programs, and developing childcare infrastructure to sustain participation rates and mitigate dependency pressures.
