# Rule of Law Mechanism

## Summary

The Rule of Law Mechanism refers to the institutional, legal, and procedural frameworks that ensure government accountability, legal predictability, and adherence to democratic principles. In the context of recent European Commission assessments, such as the 2025 Rule of Law Report on Hungary, it serves as a benchmark for evaluating systemic compliance across key pillars including judicial independence, anti-corruption measures, media freedom, and institutional checks and balances. Persistent gaps in these areas often indicate structural vulnerabilities that undermine democratic resilience, public trust, and investment climates.

## Key Details

- **Judicial Independence:** Perceived independence remains low among citizens and businesses. Recent legislative changes have altered judge appointment criteria, raised minimum age requirements, and extended service limits, sparking concerns over compromised autonomy. Structural safeguards are absent, and undue pressure persists through disciplinary actions against critical judges and relaxed prosecutor selection criteria.
- **Anti-Corruption Framework:** High levels of perceived corruption and declining final convictions for high-level offenses. Implementation of national strategies faces operational hurdles, particularly for oversight bodies like the Integrity Authority. Legislative gaps remain in lobbying regulation, revolving doors, asset declarations, and political party financing. Public procurement transparency shows slight improvements but remains vulnerable to fragmented oversight and preferential treatment.
- **Media Pluralism & Freedom:** Regulatory bodies lack functional independence, and state advertising heavily favors pro-government outlets. Public service media remains financially and politically dependent on the ruling majority. Journalists face targeted pressure, including smear campaigns, selective denial of press access, and investigations under sovereignty protection laws, while access to public information remains restricted.
- **Checks and Balances & Civic Space:** Executive overreliance on emergency powers bypasses parliamentary oversight, creating legal uncertainty. Constitutional courts face scrutiny over political appointment processes and expanded review powers. Civil society operates in an obstructed environment, with independent NGOs targeted by sovereignty offices and proposed legislation threatening foreign funding.
- **International Compliance:** Lagging implementation of European Court of Human Rights judgments (over 50 pending) highlights systemic deficits in adhering to supranational legal obligations.
