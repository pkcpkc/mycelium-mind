# Labour Market Participation

## Summary

Labour Market Participation refers to the share of the working-age population that is either employed or actively seeking employment. In Belgium's 2025 economic assessment, participation exhibits paradoxical dynamics where record employment levels (72.3%) coexist with activity rates below the EU average, driven by low engagement among specific demographic groups and structural barriers within the tax and benefits system.

## Key Details

- **Paradoxical Dynamics:** While overall employment reached a record 72.3%, the participation rate remains below the EU average due to low activity rates, particularly among low-skilled workers, migrants, and older adults.
- **Structural Disincentives:** The [[Tax and Benefits System]] creates strong disincentives to work, including high labour taxes, an unemployment trap, and non-means-tested benefits that prolong inactivity.
- **Activation and Shortages:** High labour shortages persist alongside weak activation policies, exacerbating skill mismatches for the green and digital transitions.
- **Regional Disparities:** Social fairness issues manifest in the labour market, with Brussels and Wallonia facing higher poverty and exclusion rates compared to Flanders.
- **Reform Measures:** Federal plans include limiting unemployment benefits to two years, shifting the tax burden away from labour, and implementing a pension bonus-malus mechanism to improve long-term sustainability.
- **Educational Links:** Educational inequalities, such as declining student performance and early tracking mechanisms, contribute to skill mismatches and hinder labour market integration.
