# Net General Government Debt

## Summary

Net General Government Debt is a fiscal metric representing gross general government debt minus financial assets, used as a core indicator for assessing sovereign fiscal performance and debt sustainability.

## Key Details

- **Methodological Shift:** In the S&P Global Ratings sovereign rating framework, the initial fiscal assessment explicitly bases its evaluation on changes in net rather than gross general government debt to better capture underlying fiscal trajectories and policy effectiveness.
- **Fiscal Assessment Integration:** The metric is a primary driver in the Fiscal Assessment pillar, specifically influencing both fiscal performance/flexibility (via net debt-to-GDP trends and revenue/expenditure adjustments) and the broader Debt Burden evaluation.
- **Economic Context:** Changes in net debt are analyzed alongside GDP per capita and growth prospects to determine a sovereign's capacity to service obligations without compromising economic stability or data quality.
- **Contingent Liability Interaction:** While net debt captures explicit liabilities, fiscal assessments also factor in implicit and contingent liabilities (e.g., banking sector stress evaluated via BICRA) that can rapidly expand the effective debt burden and impact rating outcomes.
