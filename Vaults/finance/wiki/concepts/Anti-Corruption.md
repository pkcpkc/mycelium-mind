# Anti-Corruption

## Summary

Anti-corruption encompasses the legal, institutional, and regulatory frameworks established to prevent, detect, investigate, and penalize corrupt practices in both public and private sectors. In the context of France's 2025 Rule of Law Report, anti-corruption efforts are characterized by ongoing institutional reforms, enhanced prosecutorial resources, and strengthened transparency mechanisms, contributing to a relatively low perception of corruption (67/100 on the Corruption Perceptions Index).

## Key Details

- **Institutional Reorganization**: The Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) has been restructured to clearly separate oversight functions for the public and private sectors, improving operational efficiency.
- **Transparency & Asset Declaration**: The High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) has expanded its capacity to process increased volumes of asset declarations and manage compliance for public officials.
- **Prosecutorial Capacity**: Significant resource allocation has been directed toward the Central Office for Combating Corruption and Tax Offences (OCLCIFF) and the National Financial Prosecutor (PNF) to intensify investigations and prosecutions.
- **Ethical Standards**: New ethics regulations have been implemented for Members of Parliament, ministers, and magistrates to mitigate conflicts of interest and enhance accountability.
- **Whistleblower Protection**: Rising numbers of whistleblower reports highlight systemic issues but also underscore the need for improved legal safeguards and practical support mechanisms.
- **Targeted Sectors**: Anti-corruption initiatives are increasingly focused on high-risk areas, including organized drug trafficking networks and port logistics operations.
- **Performance Metrics**: France maintains a Corruption Perceptions Index score of 67/100, reflecting a relatively low perceived corruption level compared to international benchmarks.
