# Macroeconomic Policy

## Summary

Macroeconomic policy encompasses the government and central bank strategies used to manage aggregate demand, supply-side constraints, and long-term growth trajectories. Recent assessments highlight a critical pivot from labor-intensive expansion toward productivity-driven models to address capacity constraints, inflation stabilization, and infrastructure strains. Effective policy requires coordinated fiscal consolidation, robust financial sector supervision, and structural reforms in innovation, labor markets, and climate resilience to ensure sustainable development.

## Key Details

- **Growth and Structural Pivot**: Transitioning from reliance on labor-intensive sectors (e.g., tourism, gaming) to a productivity-driven economy; managing growth moderation and addressing bottlenecks in infrastructure and public services caused by population influxes.
- **Fiscal Consolidation**: Accelerating budget adjustments under updated fiscal frameworks; phasing out fixed energy subsidies to redirect resources toward green investment, health, and innovation; establishing long-term fiscal roadmaps aligned with national visions.
- **Tax Reform**: Developing clear roadmaps for corporate income tax adjustments to ensure compliance with international directives (e.g., Pillar II) and prevent revenue leakage.
- **Financial Stability**: Maintaining well-capitalized banking systems while tightening macroprudential measures, including sectoral systemic risk buffers for real estate exposures, closing data gaps, and enhancing cyber risk assessments.
- **Structural Reforms**: Expanding support for innovation and venture capital; reforming education systems to boost STEM enrollment and digital skills; progressing in anti-money laundering (AML) and judicial reforms to bolster investor confidence.
- **Climate Integration**: Aligning economic policies with ambitious climate targets through accelerated renewable energy adoption and sustainable tourism practices.
