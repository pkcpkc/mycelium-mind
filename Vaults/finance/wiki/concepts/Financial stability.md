# Financial Stability

## Summary

Financial stability refers to the condition where the financial system can withstand economic shocks and continue to allocate resources efficiently. Recent IMF Article IV consultations (2025) for advanced economies like Belgium and France indicate that while financial sectors maintain robust capital and liquidity buffers post-FSAP, ongoing macroprudential oversight is critical. The assessment emphasizes the need for accelerated legislative implementation, integration with fiscal consolidation efforts, and proactive monitoring of emerging risks such as non-bank financial institutions, cyber threats, and climate-related vulnerabilities to sustain long-term economic resilience.

In the context of specific jurisdictions such as Malta, financial stability remains anchored by well-capitalized and liquid banking sectors. However, supervisory priorities have shifted toward tightening macroprudential frameworks to address concentrated exposures in residential and commercial real estate, closing critical data gaps, and enhancing cyber risk assessments. These measures are essential to safeguard systemic resilience amid structural growth constraints, rapid service-sector expansion, and evolving fiscal landscapes.

## Key Details

- **Stability Status**: Financial sectors across major economies remain broadly stable, supported by strong capital and liquidity buffers, though moderate systemic risks require continuous vigilance. In specific jurisdictions like Malta, banking sectors are well-capitalized but face structural pressures from rapid growth in tourism and gaming-driven economies.
- **Macroprudential Buffers & Framework**: The IMF commends existing macroprudential frameworks and stresses the necessity of maintaining buffers to mitigate identified risks. Recent assessments highlight the need to raise sectoral systemic risk buffers specifically for concentrated real estate exposures and to enforce stricter supervisory measures targeting housing market dynamics.
- **Legislative & Regulatory Implementation**: Acceleration of post-FSAP legislative actions is required to close regulatory gaps, enhance oversight, and align financial regulation with broader structural reforms. This includes mandating improved data reporting standards to eliminate information asymmetries and support accurate macroprudential stress testing.
- **Emerging Risk Monitoring**: Continued surveillance is prioritized for non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs), cyber risks, and climate-related vulnerabilities. Supervisors are increasingly required to integrate dynamic cyber risk assessments into macroprudential frameworks and real-time monitoring systems.
- **Integration with Fiscal & Structural Policy**: Financial stability is intrinsically linked to fiscal consolidation, labor market reforms, pension adjustments, and coordinated industrial policies. In open economies, managing capacity constraints, wage pressures, and infrastructure bottlenecks while transitioning to productivity-driven growth models is vital for sustaining debt trajectories and sector-wide resilience.
