# Magyar Nemzeti Bank

## Summary

The central bank of Hungary, tasked with issuing the national currency, formulating monetary policy, and ensuring macroeconomic stability. It operates under a formal inflation targeting regime set at 3 percent annually, utilizing interest rate policy, exchange rate flexibility, and foreign exchange reserves to manage economic cycles and absorb external shocks.

## Key Details

- Maintains a formal inflation target of 3 percent, aiming to anchor long-term inflation expectations and guide price stability.
- Implements a data-dependent monetary policy stance, adjusting policy rates to durably converge inflation toward the target amid high uncertainty.
- Has previously deployed regulatory measures such as voluntary interest rate ceilings on retail deposits, though these are noted as distorting market pricing and weakening monetary transmission.
- Relies on flexible exchange rates and robust foreign exchange reserve coverage as primary shock absorbers for the domestic economy.
- Monitors financial sector vulnerabilities, including sovereign-bank nexus exposures, corporate debt risks, commercial real estate valuations, and housing market dynamics influenced by subsidized lending.
- Coordinates with fiscal authorities and supervisory bodies on macroprudential calibration, ensuring that borrower-based limits are strictly tied to financial stability objectives rather than non-stability goals.
