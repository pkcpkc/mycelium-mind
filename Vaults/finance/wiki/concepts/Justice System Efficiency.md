# Justice System Efficiency

## Summary

Justice System Efficiency encompasses the capacity of the judicial framework to resolve cases within reasonable timeframes and with adequate resource allocation. In Belgium's 2025 Rule of Law Report, this concept is defined by persistent chronic backlogs and structural resource deficiencies. Although recent measures indicate incremental progress through increased transparency in magistrate recruitment and new digital file management initiatives, long-term structural reforms remain essential to fully address systemic inefficiencies.

## Key Details

- **Chronic Backlogs**: Significant delays persist within the judicial system, particularly concentrated at the Court of Appeal of Brussels.
- **Structural Resource Deficiencies**: The system suffers from insufficient resources to handle caseloads effectively.
- **Recent Incremental Measures**:
  - Implementation of transparency protocols by publishing all magistrate vacancies.
  - Introduction of a file management project funded by the Recovery and Resilience Facility.
- **Digitalisation Challenges**: The Court of Audit has criticized the digitalisation efforts for inadequate oversight and potential fraud risks, though initial corrective action plans have been submitted.
- **Reform Status**: While progress is noted, comprehensive long-term structural reforms are still required to sustain improvements.
