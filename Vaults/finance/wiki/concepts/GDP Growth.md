# GDP Growth

## Summary

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) Growth is a macroeconomic indicator that measures the annualized percentage change in the total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country. It serves as a primary gauge of economic health, expansion, and contraction over specific periods, heavily influenced by domestic demand, external trade conditions, monetary policy, and structural productivity factors.

## Key Details

- **Post-Recession Recovery Dynamics:** Growth trajectories can be severely disrupted by compounding shocks such as energy price spikes, aggressive monetary tightening, and subdued domestic/external demand, leading to consecutive years of contraction before marginal recovery phases emerge.
- **Growth Constraints:** Near-term growth projections are frequently constrained by simultaneous macroeconomic headwinds, including planned fiscal consolidation, heightened global trade policy uncertainty, and sluggish productivity improvements.
- **Structural & Demographic Impacts:** Aging populations and demographic shifts can suppress long-term growth capacity by increasing fiscal pressures (e.g., aging-related expenditures potentially adding ~2% of GDP to budget deficits by 2030), necessitating proactive labor supply expansions and productivity reforms.
- **Reform-Driven Expansion:** Sustained or accelerated growth typically requires targeted structural interventions, including deregulation in services, accelerated green infrastructure approvals, housing supply improvements, and labor market integration (women, elderly workers, and immigrants) to unlock economies of scale and productivity gains.
