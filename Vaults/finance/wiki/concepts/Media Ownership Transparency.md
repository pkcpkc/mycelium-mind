# Media Ownership Transparency

## Summary

Media ownership transparency refers to the systematic disclosure and public accessibility of information regarding the entities and individuals that control media outlets. It serves as a foundational safeguard for media pluralism, preventing hidden corporate or political capture and enabling public accountability. In the context of France's 2025 Rule of Law Report, it represents a key area where regulatory and legislative efforts are being consolidated to enhance oversight of the media landscape.

## Key Details

- Significant progress has been made in improving media ownership transparency in France, with a national database currently in preparation to centralize and publish ownership data.
- The initiative is overseen by [[ARCOM]], the French media regulator, which has expanded its supervisory scope under the [[Digital Services Act (DSA)]] and in preparation for the [[European Media Freedom Act (EMFA)]].
- Transparency mechanisms are evaluated as part of the EU [[Rule of Law]] reporting framework to assess member states' compliance with democratic standards and independent media safeguards.
- The upcoming national database aims to mitigate risks of opaque ownership structures, ensuring that journalists, civil society, and the public can effectively monitor potential conflicts of interest or undue influence in media coverage.
