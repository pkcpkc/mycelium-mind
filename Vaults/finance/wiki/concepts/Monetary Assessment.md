# Monetary Assessment

## Summary
The Monetary Assessment is one of the five core evaluation pillars in S&P Global Ratings' sovereign creditworthiness framework, operating alongside the Institutional, Economic, External, and Fiscal Assessments. It serves as a critical determinant of sovereign ratings, particularly for local-currency ratings where independent monetary policy is a prerequisite for rating uplifts. The assessment evaluates a country's exchange rate regime and the central bank's capacity to fulfill its mandate, with monetary policy credibility specifically measured by institutional independence, policy effectiveness, price stability, and financial market depth. It assigns a score from 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest), contributing directly to the broader Institutional and Economic Profile.

## Key Details
- **Weighting Structure:** The assessment allocates 40% weight to the exchange rate regime and 60% weight to monetary policy credibility, price stability, and financial market depth.
- **Scoring Scale:** Scores range from 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest), contributing to the broader Institutional and Economic Profile.
- **Monetary Union Adjustments:** For sovereigns belonging to a monetary union, the monetary assessment score is adjusted to reflect policy synchronization with the union and shared liquidity constraints.
- **Monetary Authorities:** Ratings for monetary authorities within unions are derived from the weighted average ratings of member sovereigns, with potential uplifts if the authority possesses significant monetary flexibility.
- **Local-Currency Rating Impact:** A sovereign may receive a one-notch uplift for its local-currency rating over its foreign-currency rating only if it maintains an independent monetary policy, possesses deep local capital markets, and demonstrates sufficient institutional and fiscal flexibility.
- **Central Bank Mandate Capacity:** The assessment explicitly examines the central bank's operational capacity to fulfill its statutory mandate, weighing exchange rate regime stability against the broader credibility of monetary policy.
- **Credibility Metrics:** Monetary policy credibility is systematically evaluated across four dimensions: institutional independence, policy effectiveness, track record of price stability, and depth of local financial markets.
- **Framework Integration:** As one of five interconnected assessment pillars, it interacts closely with the Fiscal and External Assessments to form a comprehensive view of sovereign credit fundamentals. The methodology acknowledges significant room for discretionary judgment, allowing analysts to apply subjective adjustments for qualitative factors that may override standardized metrics.
