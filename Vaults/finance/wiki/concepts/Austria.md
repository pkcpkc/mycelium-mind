# Austria

## Summary

Austria is a Central European economy navigating a complex macroeconomic landscape characterized by structural challenges, fiscal consolidation pressures, and targeted reform priorities outlined by both the IMF and the European Commission. 

> [!warning] Projection Divergence
> Conflicting short-term outlooks exist between institutional forecasts: the IMF projects a marginal 2025 rebound with near-zero growth, while the European Commission's 2025 Country Report highlights a third consecutive year of recession with a projected GDP contraction of 0.3%. Both assessments agree that long-term recovery hinges on structural reforms, fiscal prudence, and addressing productivity bottlenecks.

Despite a marginal rebound in real disposable household incomes and historically high savings rates, consumer demand remains subdued. The country faces structurally elevated inflation (~3%), widening fiscal deficits (exceeding 4% of GDP), and significant demographic aging pressures. Concurrently, Austria exhibits a notable innovation-to-productivity gap, with high R&D expenditure failing to translate into proportional business dynamism due to regulatory bottlenecks and limited risk financing. Structural reforms targeting labour market participation, pension sustainability, green transition acceleration, and regulatory simplification are prioritized to restore long-term competitiveness and fiscal stability.

## Key Details

- **Macroeconomic & Fiscal Environment:** Projected GDP contraction of 0.3% in 2025 following three years of recession, with a gradual rebound expected in 2026. Fiscal deficit widened to 4.7% of GDP in 2024, pushing public debt to 81%. Rising public expenditure is largely fueled by ageing-related costs in pensions and healthcare, straining consolidation efforts. Consumer demand remains subdued despite high savings rates, while inflation remains structurally elevated at ~3% due to sticky services prices and nominal wage growth (7-8%) outpacing the euro area average.
- **Labour Market & Demographics:** Despite resilient overall employment rates, the economy faces severe skills shortages and a continuous decline in average working hours. High prevalence of part-time work (particularly among highly educated women), combined with low participation rates for older workers and migrants, constrains economic potential. There is an urgent need to expand high-quality early childhood education and care (ECEC) to boost maternal labour force participation, alleviate poverty risks, and address demographic headwinds. The pension system faces long-term fiscal sustainability pressures due to relatively early retirement ages.
- **Innovation & Business Environment:** Austria maintains above-average R&D expenditure and strong science-business collaboration. However, this investment fails to translate into proportional business dynamism or productivity growth. Primary bottlenecks include a lack of venture capital, limited access to risk financing for SMEs, and a regulatory environment burdened by complex permitting procedures and high administrative costs. Streamlining regulatory burdens, modernizing professional services regulations, and fostering a more competitive business environment are essential to unlocking high-impact innovation.
- **Green Transition & Energy:** Austria has successfully phased out coal and increased renewable energy generation to 87% of its electricity mix. Challenges remain in the form of high industrial energy prices, grid expansion delays, and material footprint issues requiring increased recycling infrastructure investment. Accelerating permitting for net-zero industries, diversifying gas supplies, and phasing out environmentally harmful fossil fuel subsidies are critical next steps to balance decarbonisation with energy affordability.
- **Financial Sector & Risks:** The banking sector maintains robust capitalization (CET1 ~17.7%) but faces emerging vulnerabilities in commercial real estate, with NPL ratios rising to ~5%. Systemic risks are downside-biased due to potential trade tensions, prolonged weak sentiment, persistent inflation differentials, and stagnant wage growth for lower-income groups.
- **Social Fairness & Housing:** Social indicators show mixed results. While overall poverty rates remain below the EU average, risks are disproportionately high for children, single parents, and individuals with a migrant background. Rising housing affordability pressures and stagnant wages exacerbate inequality. Recommendations focus on targeted tax reforms to shift the burden away from labour, accelerated green/digital transitions, and structural labour market reforms to harness workforce potential.
- **Strategic Reforms & Integration:** Recommendations emphasize deepening EU Single Market integration (e.g., Savings and Investment Union), improving immigrant integration, expanding childcare/eldercare infrastructure, and alleviating housing supply bottlenecks to unlock economies of scale and boost productivity.
