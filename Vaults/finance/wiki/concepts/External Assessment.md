# External Assessment

## Summary

External Assessment is one of the five core pillars utilized by [[S&P Global Ratings]] to evaluate sovereign creditworthiness. It examines a nation's cross-border financial position, specifically focusing on currency status, foreign exchange liquidity adequacy relative to short-term financing needs, and the balance of residents' external assets and liabilities. Formally integrated into the updated [[Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)]], it serves as a critical gauge of external sector vulnerabilities alongside the Institutional, Economic, Fiscal, and Monetary Assessments.

## Key Details

- **Currency Status**: Evaluates the international role, convertibility, and stability of the local currency within global trade and financial markets.
- **Liquidity vs. Financing Needs**: Assesses whether a sovereign's liquid financial resources (foreign exchange liquidity) are sufficient to cover short-term external financing obligations and buffer against capital flight or market stress.
- **External Indebtedness & Asset/Liability Balance**: Quantified using a "narrow net external debt" metric, which isolates liabilities impacting the sovereign's external balance sheet. It also tracks the broader balance of residents' external assets and liabilities to gauge the net international investment position.
- **Methodological Context**: Formally integrated into the [[Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)]], replacing prior criteria. The framework's application and nuances were further detailed in contexts such as the 2024 HU Berlin presentation by [[Alex Stomper]], which highlighted how analysts apply subjective adjustments and discretionary judgment across all pillars.
- **Interaction with Other Pillars**: Works in tandem with the [[Institutional Assessment]], [[Economic Assessment]], [[Monetary Assessment]], and [[Fiscal Assessment]] to form the profiles used in the indicative rating matrix.
