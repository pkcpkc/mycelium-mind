# Housing Affordability

## Summary
Housing affordability refers to the capacity of households, particularly lower-income demographics, to secure and maintain adequate housing without financial strain. It is a fundamental metric for assessing social fairness, economic stability, and the effectiveness of national welfare policies.

## Key Details
- Rising housing affordability pressures are a primary driver of exacerbated inequality, especially when compounded by stagnant wage growth for lower-income groups.
- Identified as a key social fairness challenge in Austria by the European Commission's 2025 Country Report, reflecting broader macroeconomic and demographic headwinds.
- Closely interacts with labour market dynamics, as high housing cost burdens can offset wage gains and reduce disposable income for essential services.
- Requires coordinated policy interventions, including housing supply expansion, targeted rental subsidies, and structural tax reforms, to mitigate affordability crises and support vulnerable populations.
