# Monetary Union

## Summary
A monetary union refers to an arrangement where multiple sovereign members adopt a common currency and delegate monetary policy authority to a central institution. In sovereign credit rating frameworks, union membership significantly alters risk profiles by synchronizing policy responses, pooling liquidity constraints, and modifying how individual member creditworthiness and central authority ratings are calculated.

## Key Details
- **Policy Synchronization & Liquidity Constraints:** Members of a monetary union have their external and monetary assessment scores adjusted to reflect synchronized policy frameworks and shared liquidity limitations, as individual members lose independent control over monetary tools.
- **Rating of Monetary Authorities:** The creditworthiness of a central monetary authority within a union is determined by applying a weighted average rating of its member sovereigns, with potential rating uplifts granted based on the authority's specific monetary flexibility and institutional backing.
- **Local-Currency Rating Implications:** Sovereigns within a monetary union typically lack independent monetary policy, which restricts the standard one-notch local-currency rating uplift over the foreign-currency rating. Uplifts are generally only permissible when independent monetary policy, deep local capital markets, and sufficient fiscal/institutional flexibility are maintained.
- **Methodology Integration:** Under the 2017 Sovereign Rating Methodology, union-specific factors are systematically integrated into the five-pillar assessment framework to ensure accurate credit risk evaluation and appropriate rating matrix placement.
