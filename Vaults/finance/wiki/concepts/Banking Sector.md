# Banking Sector

## Summary

The banking sector operates as a critical pillar of national economies, demonstrating robust fundamentals across diverse jurisdictions. In Italy, major banks report record profitability, sound asset quality, and ample capital buffers, though ongoing monitoring is required for smaller institutions and sovereign-bank linkages. Similarly, Romania's financial sector maintains high capital adequacy and liquidity buffers, yet faces emerging vulnerabilities tied to sovereign exposure, unhedged foreign exchange loans, and rapid consumer credit growth. Across markets, regulatory frameworks and macroprudential policies are continuously calibrated to safeguard financial stability and support sustainable economic development.

## Key Details

- **Profitability & Asset Quality:** Major banks in mature markets (e.g., Italy) report record profitability and stable, low non-performing loan ratios. In emerging markets like Romania, asset quality remains generally sound but requires close supervision due to rapid credit expansion and shifting economic conditions.
- **Capital & Liquidity Buffers:** Systems maintain ample capital buffers, including macroprudential tools like the Systemic Risk Buffer (SyRB) and Countercyclical Capital Buffer (CCyB). Romanian banks similarly exhibit high capital adequacy and robust liquidity positions to absorb potential shocks.
- **Vulnerabilities & Risks:** Vigilance is required for Less Significant Institutions (LSIs), potential loan quality deterioration, and the sovereign-bank nexus. Additional risks include sizable unhedged foreign exchange corporate loans and buoyant consumer credit growth, which can amplify sensitivity to interest rate changes and currency volatility.
- **Regulatory & Supervisory Framework:** Progress continues in strengthening AML/CFT frameworks and enhancing financial integrity. Supervisory vigilance is emphasized to monitor asset quality, calibrate macroprudential policies, and address emerging credit, liquidity, and FX exposures across jurisdictions.
- **Policy Context:** Operates within broader regional and international frameworks (e.g., EU/ECB in Europe), with macroprudential settings aligned to mitigate systemic risks, stabilize public debt linkages, and support structural reforms that enhance long-term economic resilience.
