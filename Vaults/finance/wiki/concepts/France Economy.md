# France Economy

## Summary

The [[France Economy]] is a major advanced economy within the European Union, characterized by robust labor markets and resilience driven by factors such as the Paris Olympics, though it faces significant headwinds from high public debt, geoeconomic fragmentation, and policy uncertainty. Recent assessments emphasize the urgent need for frontloaded fiscal consolidation to restore debt sustainability, comprehensive structural reforms to enhance productivity and competitiveness, and continued financial sector oversight to mitigate risks associated with non-bank institutions and climate vulnerabilities.

## Key Details

- **Growth and Inflation Dynamics:** Real GDP growth is projected to moderate to 0.6% in 2025 before recovering to 1% in 2026. Disinflation is progressing steadily, with inflation expectations remaining anchored, contributing to economic stability despite tight financial conditions.
- **Fiscal Consolidation:** Public debt reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, highlighting the necessity of fiscal adjustment. The authorities have committed to the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP), aiming to reduce the structural deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. The strategy focuses on rationalizing current spending across central, local, and social security levels rather than increasing taxes, given France's already high tax-to-GDP ratio.
- **Structural Reforms:**
    - **Labor Market:** Reforms target increased female labor force participation, better integration of migrants, and addressing skill mismatches to improve employment outcomes.
    - **Pension System:** The 2023 [[Pension Reform]] raised the retirement age to 64, addressing demographic pressures, with further recommendations for harmonization and healthy aging policies.
    - **Innovation and Transition:** Strategic priorities include leveraging strengths in the [[Green Transition]] and [[Artificial Intelligence]] sectors by enhancing innovation financing, streamlining R&D tax expenditures, and advancing capital allocation mechanisms.
- **Financial Sector Resilience:** Banks maintain strong capital and liquidity buffers following the 2025 Financial Sector Assessment Program (FSAP). The macroprudential framework is effective, though ongoing monitoring of non-bank financial institutions, cyber risks, and climate-related vulnerabilities is essential.
- **European Integration:** France's deep integration with the EU underscores the importance of coordinated industrial policies and deeper single market integration to reduce fragmentation, boost competitiveness, and support broader economic stability.
