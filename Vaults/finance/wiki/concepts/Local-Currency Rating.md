# Local-Currency Rating

## Summary

A sovereign credit assessment that evaluates a government's capacity and willingness to service debt obligations denominated in its own currency. Unlike foreign-currency ratings, which prioritize external liquidity and access to hard currency, local-currency ratings heavily emphasize domestic monetary policy independence, the depth of local capital markets, and the institutional/fiscal flexibility to prioritize domestic obligations during stress.

## Key Details

- **Rating Cap & Uplift Criteria:** The local-currency rating is generally capped at one notch above the foreign-currency rating. A one-notch uplift is permissible only if the sovereign maintains independent monetary policy, possesses deep local capital markets, and demonstrates sufficient institutional and fiscal flexibility to prioritize domestic debt obligations.
- **Distress Divergence:** In severe distress scenarios (e.g., when the foreign-currency rating approaches 'CCC'), the local-currency rating may diverge further from the foreign-currency rating if default risks differ materially between domestic and foreign currency obligations.
- **Monetary Union Adjustments:** For sovereigns within a monetary union, external and monetary assessment scores are adjusted to account for synchronized policy frameworks and shared liquidity constraints, often resulting in averaged or unified rating approaches for union members.
- **Methodological Integration:** The rating is derived from the intersection of the Institutional and Economic Profile and the Flexibility and Performance Profile within the sovereign rating framework, incorporating fiscal debt burden assessments that include contingent liabilities and monetary policy credibility metrics.
- **Core Differentiators:** Focuses on domestic revenue generation capacity, central bank independence, local market depth, and the sovereign's ability to manage domestic debt rollovers without external financing pressure.
