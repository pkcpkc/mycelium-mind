# National Council for the Judiciary

## Summary

The National Council for the Judiciary (NCJ) is a constitutional body in Poland responsible for safeguarding the independence of courts and judges. The institution has been at the center of significant rule of law disputes, particularly concerning its post-2017 composition and the validity of judges appointed by it. As of 2025, the Polish government is advancing legislative measures to resolve the status of these judges, a process overseen by international bodies to ensure compliance with EU standards and judicial independence.

## Key Details

- The post-2017 version of the National Council for the Judiciary has been controversial, with concerns regarding its independence and legitimacy raised by EU institutions and the Venice Commission.
- The Polish government is advancing draft legislation to resolve the status of judges appointed under the post-2017 National Council for the Judiciary.
- This legislative process is closely monitored by the Venice Commission to ensure proportionality and judicial review.
- Resolving the status of judges linked to the NCJ is a key component of Poland's implementation of the Rule of Law Action Plan.
- The NCJ remains a focal point in broader efforts to restore judicial independence and separate the executive from the judiciary, including progress made in separating the office of the Minister of Justice from that of the Prosecutor General.
