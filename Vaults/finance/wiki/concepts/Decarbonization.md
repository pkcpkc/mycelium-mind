# Decarbonization

## Summary

Decarbonization refers to the reduction of carbon dioxide emissions associated with human activities, primarily through the displacement of fossil fuels by low-carbon energy sources and the implementation of efficiency measures across key economic sectors. In the context of national policy assessments, such as the 2025 Country Report on Malta and the 2025 Country Report on Romania, decarbonization is recognized as a critical pillar of the green transition. It requires significant structural reforms to overcome entrenched dependencies on carbon-intensive infrastructure, market distortions, and high energy prices while aligning with EU-wide social and environmental goals.

## Key Details

- **Energy Mix Constraints**: Decarbonization efforts face headwinds in regions where the electricity mix remains heavily reliant on fossil fuels, with renewable capacity (e.g., solar installations) constituting only a modest share of total generation.
- **Subsidy Distortions**: Persistent fossil fuel subsidies can distort market incentives, delaying the adoption of cleaner technologies and actively hindering progress toward decarbonization targets.
- **Transport Sector Impact**: The transport sector often emerges as the largest source of emissions, characterized by worsening traffic congestion and exceptionally low adoption rates of alternative-fuel vehicles.
- **Waste Management Gaps**: Inefficient waste management infrastructure, including high per-capita waste generation and low recycling rates, contributes to carbon intensity and undermines broader decarbonization goals.
- **Policy Funding Mechanisms**: Public financial instruments, such as the Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) and Cohesion Policy funds, serve as critical enablers by financing green infrastructure reforms and supporting decarbonization pathways.
- **Industrial and Grid Modernization**: Effective decarbonization requires addressing structural bottlenecks in energy storage and grid capacity. Strategies include accelerating the decommissioning of coal and lignite plants and implementing "contracts for difference" (CfD) and power purchase agreements (PPAs) to support industrial decarbonization.
- **Fiscal Incentives**: Transitioning involves shifting the fiscal burden from electricity to fossil fuels to incentivize electrification and protecting vulnerable consumers from price spikes during the transition.
