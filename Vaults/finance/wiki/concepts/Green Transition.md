# Green Transition

## Summary

The Green Transition encompasses the systemic realignment of economic activities, industrial policies, and fiscal frameworks toward sustainability and reduced environmental impact. In recent macroeconomic assessments, accelerating project approvals for green initiatives and eliminating environmentally harmful subsidies are highlighted as essential structural reforms to restore productivity and support post-recession recovery. Concurrently, international financial institutions emphasize that achieving decarbonization targets requires robust multi-level governance, including stronger federal-regional coordination, coherent national climate strategies, and the strategic implementation of carbon taxation alongside fossil fuel subsidy phase-outs to correct market distortions and align fiscal consolidation with sustainability objectives. 

Recent policy analyses, such as the IMF's 2025 consultation on France, further underscore the transition's role in national structural reform agendas, highlighting its integration with innovation financing, R&D tax optimization, and the monitoring of climate-related financial vulnerabilities to ensure long-term economic resilience. Expanding on this framework, recent consultations on Italy (2025) classify the transition to renewable energy and resilient infrastructure as macro-critical priorities. Furthermore, in the context of Slovakia (2025), the Green Transition is identified as a cornerstone for industrial decarbonization, requiring a shift away from high-emission industrial outputs and addressing dependencies on non-EU critical raw materials to ensure supply chain security.

## Key Details

- **Regulatory Acceleration:** Streamlining administrative and approval processes for Green Transition projects is emphasized as a vital lever to unlock investment, reduce bottlenecks, and boost overall productivity.
- **Fiscal & Subsidy Reform:** Structural spending recommendations include phasing out environmentally harmful and fossil fuel subsidies to correct market distortions, ensure fiscal consolidation aligns with sustainability objectives, and support long-term decarbonization.
- **Macroeconomic Integration:** The transition is positioned as a core component of broader structural reforms, intersecting with labor market adaptation, demographic challenges, and EU Single Market integration to enhance long-term competitiveness.
- **Investment & Innovation:** Accelerating green project pipelines supports capital market financing needs and fosters innovation, particularly for startups and SMEs seeking sustainable growth pathways.
- **Policy Coordination & Governance:** Effective implementation of climate objectives often demands strengthened multi-level governance frameworks. For instance, achieving national decarbonization goals requires tighter federal-regional coordination and unified strategic planning to ensure policy coherence across jurisdictions.
- **Carbon Pricing Mechanisms:** The timely introduction and scaling of carbon taxation are identified as essential market-based instruments to internalize environmental costs, drive private-sector decarbonization, and complement subsidy reforms.
- **Innovation & R&D Financing:** National reform agendas increasingly prioritize enhancing green innovation financing and streamlining R&D tax expenditures to accelerate technological deployment, improve capital allocation toward sustainable enterprises, and bridge the funding gap for scalable climate solutions.
- **Climate Risk & Financial Stability:** Financial sector oversight now explicitly incorporates climate-related vulnerabilities, requiring enhanced monitoring of non-bank institutions and stress-testing against decarbonization scenarios to prevent systemic disruptions and ensure macroprudential resilience.
- **National Implementation Frameworks:** Countries are leveraging the Green Transition as a cornerstone of broader fiscal consolidation and productivity enhancement strategies, aligning industrial policy with EU capital markets integration to foster sustainable growth and offset short-term economic headwinds.
- **Macro-Critical Climate Priorities:** Recent IMF assessments (e.g., Italy 2025) identify climate-related shocks as significant downside risks to economic stability, elevating the Green Transition to a macro-critical priority that must be integrated into long-term fiscal and structural planning.
- **Resilient Infrastructure & Energy Security:** Transitioning to renewable energy and investing in climate-resilient infrastructure are highlighted as essential measures to enhance national energy security, mitigate vulnerability to external shocks, and support sustainable economic continuity.
- **NRRP & Recovery Alignment:** National recovery mechanisms, such as the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP), are increasingly leveraged to fund green infrastructure and public administration modernization, though efficient implementation remains crucial to realizing productivity gains and avoiding fiscal strain.
- **Industrial Decarbonization:** In specific contexts like Slovakia, the transition focuses on decarbonizing industrial sectors (representing ~30% of GDP), reducing reliance on non-EU critical raw materials, and increasing the share of renewables in the electricity mix to meet EU averages.
