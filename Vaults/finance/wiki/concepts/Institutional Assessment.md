# Institutional Assessment

## Summary

The Institutional Assessment is a foundational pillar within the [[S&P Global Ratings]] Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017) and broader sovereign creditworthiness frameworks. It provides a qualitative and structural evaluation of a government's institutional capacity, focusing specifically on the stability, transparency, and accountability of the policymaking environment and civil society. This assessment is subsequently aggregated with economic factors to determine long-term sovereign credit profiles.

## Key Details

- Serves as a primary component of the five-pillar sovereign rating framework, alongside [[Economic Assessment]], [[External Assessment]], [[Fiscal Assessment]], and [[Monetary Assessment]].
- Evaluates core governance and stability metrics, including policymaking effectiveness, political and economic stability, transparency of government operations, and the structural strength of civil society.
- Incorporates subjective and qualitative indicators to adjust initial scores, notably debt repayment culture and potential external security risks.
- Assigned a numerical score from 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest), reflecting the resilience of institutional foundations and their capacity to navigate economic shocks.
- Combined with the [[Economic Assessment]] score to form the [[Institutional and Economic Profile]], which serves as the baseline for determining indicative sovereign ratings.
- Heavily influenced by [[Discretionary Judgment]] due to the qualitative nature of civil society cohesion, event risk, and governance nuances, allowing analysts to apply subjective adjustments outside rigid quantitative metrics.
