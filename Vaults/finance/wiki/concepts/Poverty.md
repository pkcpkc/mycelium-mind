# Poverty

## Summary

Poverty is a condition of lacking sufficient resources, income, or access to basic necessities such as food, housing, and healthcare. In the context of modern economic reports, it is often analyzed through the lens of "social fairness" and is frequently influenced by inflation, labor productivity, and structural economic policies.

## Key Details

- **Economic Drivers:** Poverty can be exacerbated by high inflation (e.g., peaking at 17.0% in certain regions), high energy prices, and a lack of cost-competitiveness.
- **Social Context:** In recent assessments (e.g., Hungary 2025), poverty and social exclusion are noted to be on the rise, reversing previous trends.
- **Structural Factors:** Lack of social housing, high costs of living, and inadequate healthcare systems contribute to the persistence of poverty.
- **Energy Poverty:** A specific form of poverty where households cannot afford adequate heating or electricity, often linked to a reliance on fossil fuel subsidies.
- **Policy Implications:** Addressing poverty involves social inclusion, reforming pension systems for sustainability, and improving social dialogue between employers and trade unions.
