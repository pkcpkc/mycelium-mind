# Decarbonisation

## Summary

Decarbonisation represents the systemic transition away from carbon-intensive economic activities and fossil fuel reliance toward low-carbon or zero-emission alternatives. It involves restructuring energy systems, optimizing industrial processes, and implementing regulatory frameworks to mitigate climate change impacts while maintaining economic competitiveness. Real-world implementations highlight that progress often hinges on overcoming sector-specific bottlenecks, aligning fiscal and tax policies with climate goals, and ensuring equitable transitions across manufacturing and energy grids. In specific national contexts like France, this involves balancing high low-carbon electricity shares (driven by nuclear) with the accelerated deployment of solar, wind, and hydrogen to meet ambitious greenhouse gas reduction targets.

## Key Details

- **Renewable Energy Expansion:** Accelerating the shift from coal and fossil fuels to renewable sources, with national targets focusing on achieving high renewable shares in electricity generation.
- **Infrastructure & Permitting Bottlenecks:** Grid expansion delays, grid congestion, and complex administrative procedures significantly slow down the deployment of net-zero industries and clean energy projects. Public acceptance issues further complicate project siting and infrastructure development.
- **Policy Interventions:** Requires diversifying energy supply chains, streamlining permitting processes for green initiatives, and systematically phasing out environmentally harmful fossil fuel subsidies. Establishing clear phase-out timelines is critical to prevent undermining electrification incentives and energy transition goals.
- **Circular Economy Synergy:** Integrating resource efficiency and waste reduction strategies to lower overall material footprints and support sustainable industrial decarbonisation.
- **Affordability & Competitiveness:** Addressing high industrial energy prices to ensure the transition does not erode national competitiveness or exacerbate social inequalities. Price disparities between electricity and gas, often driven by heavy taxation and levies, can disproportionately lag industrial decarbonisation efforts.
- **Manufacturing Sector Impact:** Heavy industry and manufacturing can account for a substantial portion of national GHG emissions (e.g., ~30% in regional assessments), requiring targeted electrification strategies, technology upgrades, and reduced energy costs to meet climate targets.
- **Building & Transport Decarbonisation:** Specific focus areas include deep renovations of buildings (currently hindered by skilled labor shortages) and the transition of road transport through rail support and electric vehicle incentives.
- **Skills & Social Fairness:** Successful decarbonisation requires addressing the "skills gap" through targeted upskilling for green transitions and ensuring that the move toward a low-carbon economy does not exacerbate poverty or social exclusion.
