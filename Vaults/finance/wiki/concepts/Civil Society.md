# Civil Society

## Summary

Civil society encompasses independent non-governmental organizations, advocacy groups, and citizen initiatives that operate between the state and private sector to foster democratic participation, institutional accountability, and public interest representation. Across EU member states, the operational environment for civil society varies significantly based on national legislative frameworks and political climates. In Poland's 2025 landscape, civil society has benefited from institutional reforms that shifted civic space ratings from "obstructed" to "narrowed," alongside strengthened consultation rules and anti-SLAPP protections. Conversely, in Slovakia, the civil society environment has actively narrowed due to systemic pressures including new NGO reporting burdens, targeted funding cuts for human rights advocates, and heightened political polarization, often exacerbated by fast-track legislative processes that bypass stakeholder engagement.

## Key Details

- **Civic Space Assessment:** The operational environment for civil society shows divergent regional trends. In Poland, targeted institutional and legislative reforms have measurably improved conditions, upgrading civic space ratings from "obstructed" to "narrowed." In contrast, Slovakia's environment has narrowed due to administrative burdens and political pressures.
- **Legislative Consultation Framework:** Strengthened consultation rules in some jurisdictions grant civil society organizations structured opportunities to review drafts, submit feedback, and participate in policy formulation. However, the frequent use of fast-track legislation in other regions systematically bypasses meaningful stakeholder consultation.
- **Fundamental Rights & Oversight Synergy:** Civil society actors collaborate closely with independent constitutional bodies, particularly the Ombudsperson, to monitor compliance with fundamental rights, track EU alignment, and report systemic abuses.
- **Protection Against Legal Harassment:** Ongoing implementation of anti-SLAPP directives and reforms to protect journalistic sources directly benefit civil society by reducing Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation that historically targeted activists and independent researchers.
- **Funding & Administrative Pressures:** Civil society organizations face varying administrative and financial landscapes. While some regions provide structured support, others have implemented new reporting burdens for NGOs and executed targeted funding cuts for human rights advocates, significantly impacting operational capacity.
- **EU Rule of Law Integration:** The functional capacity and legal protections afforded to civil society are explicitly monitored within the European Commission's Rule of Law Report framework, with progress tied to the execution of the Rule of Law Action Plan and Venice Commission recommendations.
