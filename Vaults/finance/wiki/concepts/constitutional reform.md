# Constitutional Reform

## Summary

Constitutional reform refers to the proposed changes to Italy's foundational legal framework, most notably the government's initiative to introduce the direct election of the Prime Minister. This reform is situated within a broader context of institutional strain and ongoing reviews of Italy's rule of law, governance structures, and democratic checks and balances.

## Key Details

- **Direct Prime Ministerial Election:** The core proposal involves amending the constitution to allow for the direct popular election of the head of government, shifting away from the current parliamentary appointment system.
- **Institutional Balance & Checks:** The reform has drawn significant criticism and debate, particularly concerning its potential impact on the separation of powers, parliamentary sovereignty, and the system of checks and balances.
- **Legislative Process & Urgency Decrees:** The advancement of this reform has been accompanied by criticism over the government's reliance on excessive urgency decrees, raising concerns about parliamentary scrutiny and democratic transparency.
- **Broader Institutional Context:** The reform is part of a wider landscape of proposed institutional changes, including debates over the Court of Auditors' administrative liability and stalled efforts to establish a National Human Rights Institution.
- **Rule of Law Implications:** The push for constitutional change occurs alongside ongoing assessments by the EU Commission regarding judicial efficiency, anti-corruption measures, and fundamental freedoms, highlighting the sensitivity of structural governance changes in Italy.
