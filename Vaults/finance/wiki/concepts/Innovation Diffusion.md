# Innovation Diffusion

## Summary

Innovation diffusion refers to the systematic spread of research and development outputs, technological advancements, and operational best practices from leading organizations to the broader economy, particularly SMEs and adjacent sectors. In Belgium's 2025 economic landscape, this process remains concentrated, with national R&D spending largely benefiting a small number of large firms rather than generating widespread spillover effects.

## Key Details

- **High R&D Intensity, Low Spillovers:** Belgium maintains a strong national R&D commitment at 3.3% of GDP, but the financial and technological benefits are heavily concentrated within a few large enterprises.
- **SME Exclusion:** Limited knowledge transfer and technology adoption among small and medium-sized enterprises restrict broader productivity gains and competitive resilience.
- **Structural Constraints:** High energy costs, skilled labour shortages, and regulatory burdens further impede the effective diffusion of innovations across firm sizes and economic sectors.
- **Economic & Fiscal Impact:** Weak diffusion undermines business dynamism, constrains labour market participation, and limits the economy's capacity to drive sustainable fiscal consolidation without targeted policy interventions.
