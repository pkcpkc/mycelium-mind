# Civic Space

## Summary

Civic space refers to the environment in which individuals and groups can express themselves, assemble, and associate freely. Under the European Commission's 2025 Rule of Law Reports, civic space faces diverging pressures across member states. In Belgium, emerging pressures center on proposed judicial bans on demonstrations following protest-related convictions. Meanwhile, in France, the civic space is considered narrowed despite a stable financial environment for civil society organisations, due to reports of intimidation and attacks against activists. Additionally, in Romania, structural challenges to civil society operations have prompted the adoption of a 2025-2030 Strategy for Open Government aimed at mitigating NGO funding and operational barriers.

## Key Details

- **Emerging Pressures in Belgium:** The 2025 Rule of Law Report identifies proposed measures to impose judicial bans on demonstrations for individuals convicted of protest-related offences, triggering constitutional and human rights scrutiny.
- **Narrowed Space in France:** The financial environment for civil society organisations remains stable, but the civic space is considered narrowed due to reports of intimidation and attacks against activists and defenders.
- **Structural Challenges in Romania:** The civic space continues to face operational and funding barriers, prompting the government to adopt a 2025-2030 Strategy for Open Government to address these structural constraints and support civil society operations.
- **Constitutional and Human Rights Scrutiny:** Proposed restrictions in Belgium are under review for alignment with constitutional principles and human rights obligations, reflecting broader EU concerns about the practical implementation of fundamental freedoms.
- **Civil Society Ecosystem:** Financial stability for NGOs does not automatically guarantee operational safety, as demonstrated by the contrast between funding conditions and on-the-ground intimidation risks.
