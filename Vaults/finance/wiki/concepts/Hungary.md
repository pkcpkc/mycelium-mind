# Hungary

## Summary
[[Hungary]] is a Central European country and EU member state facing economic headwinds as of 2025, characterized by output stagnation and [[Inflation]] persistently above the [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]]'s 3 percent target. The [[IMF]]'s 2025 Article IV Consultation emphasizes the need for high-quality fiscal consolidation, removal of market-distorting interventions, and comprehensive structural reforms to boost competitiveness. A critical pathway for recovery involves advancing governance and rule-of-law reforms to unlock suspended [[EU Funds]], which are essential for investment confidence and external financing.

## Key Details
- **Economic Outlook:** GDP growth projected at 0.7% in 2025 and 2% in 2026, driven by consumption and nascent investment. Inflation expected to decelerate to 4.5% in Q4 2025, converging toward the central bank's target by 2027.
- **Fiscal Position:** Public debt stands at approximately 73.5% of GDP. The [[IMF]] recommends targeting a structural primary surplus of approximately 1.75% of GDP by the medium term to rebuild fiscal buffers.
- **Monetary Policy:** The central bank maintains a 3% [[Inflation Targeting]] mandate. The [[IMF]] advises maintaining a tight monetary stance through 2026 and phasing out retail margin caps and interest rate ceilings to improve monetary transmission.
- **Regulatory Environment:** Interventions such as price and margin caps, windfall taxes, and subsidized lending schemes have distorted market signals. Recommendations include broadening the tax base and phasing out distortive retail energy subsidies in favor of targeted cash transfers.
- **Structural Reforms:** Priority areas include streamlining insolvency frameworks, expanding risk capital access, accelerating the green transition, and improving governance to unlock [[EU Funds]].
- **Financial Sector:** The sector is well-capitalized but faces risks from the sovereign-bank nexus, corporate vulnerabilities, and commercial real estate valuations. Macroprudential measures should focus strictly on financial stability.
- **Risks:** Key downside risks include delays in [[EU Funds]] disbursements due to rule-of-law conditionality, geopolitical fragmentation, and fiscal impacts from upcoming national elections.
- **State-Owned Enterprises:** Enhanced monitoring of SOEs and off-balance-sheet contingent liabilities is required to mitigate hidden fiscal risks.
