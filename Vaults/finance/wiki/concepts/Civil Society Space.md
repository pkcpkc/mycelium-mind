# Civil Society Space

## Summary

Civil Society Space refers to the legal, political, and social environment that enables independent non-governmental organizations (NGOs), advocacy groups, and civic initiatives to operate, mobilize, and influence public policy. A healthy civil society space is a cornerstone of democratic governance, acting as a bridge between citizens and the state while ensuring transparency, accountability, and pluralism. In recent assessments of Hungary’s democratic backsliding, this space has been severely constrained and officially rated as obstructed.

## Key Details

- **Current Status (Hungary, 2025):** The European Commission’s 2025 Rule of Law Report rates Hungary’s civil society space as "obstructed," citing systematic restrictions on independent civic organizations.
- **State Surveillance & Targeting:** The Sovereignty Protection Office has been actively monitoring and targeting independent NGOs, frequently framing their advocacy work as threats to national sovereignty.
- **Legislative Restrictions:** A proposed "transparency" law introduces stringent reporting and funding disclosure mandates specifically designed to restrict and penalize foreign funding for civil society organizations.
- **EU Commission Recommendations:** The report explicitly calls on Hungary to foster a safe civic space, protect NGOs from political harassment, and ensure the unimpeded flow of cross-border and foreign funding.
- **Broader Democratic Impact:** A constrained civil society space directly undermines institutional checks and balances, reduces public oversight of public procurement and anti-corruption efforts, and contributes to the broader erosion of media pluralism and judicial independence.
