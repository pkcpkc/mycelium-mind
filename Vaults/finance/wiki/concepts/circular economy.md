# Circular Economy

## Summary

The circular economy represents an economic system designed to eliminate waste and continuously utilize resources through recycling, reuse, and efficient material management. In the context of Austria's recent economic landscape, progress in adopting circular principles has been acknowledged, though significant challenges remain regarding material consumption patterns and infrastructure readiness. Furthermore, the European Commission identifies the transition to a circular economy as a key recommendation for structural reform in Hungary to improve water resilience and resource management. In Italy, the country demonstrates a relatively strong performance in circular material use (20.8%), though infrastructure limitations persist, particularly in the southern regions. Additionally, the 2025 Country Report for Poland highlights that the country's circular material use rate (7.5%) currently falls below the EU average (11.8%), marking it as a key area for sustainability improvement. For Romania, the circular economy is identified as a core pillar within the broader framework of the 2025 Country Report, integrated into the nation's goals for economic convergence and sustainable development.

## Key Details

- Recognized for measurable progress in Austria's transition toward a sustainable and decarbonised economy.
- The national material footprint remains high, indicating substantial room for improvement in resource consumption patterns.
- Requires targeted and increased investment in modern recycling infrastructure to support closed-loop systems.
- Enhancing overall resource efficiency is critical to aligning industrial practices with sustainability targets.
- Functions as a foundational pillar alongside decarbonisation and renewable energy expansion in national green transition policies.
- Recommended by the EU Commission as a critical path for improving water resilience and resource management in Hungary.
- Italy performs well in circular material use (20.8%), but faces specific challenges regarding waste management infrastructure in the South.
- Poland's circular material use rate is currently 7.5%, which is below the EU average of 11.8%.
- Implementation in Poland is linked to broader goals for climate resilience and addressing environmental degradation.
- Identified as a key strategic area in the 2025 Country Report for Romania, supporting the transition from a low-cost growth model to one driven by innovation and high-tech investment.
