# National Human Rights Institutions

## Summary
National Human Rights Institutions (NHRIs) are independent state bodies established to monitor, protect, and promote human rights at the national level. In Romania, the institutionalization of these bodies remains a key indicator of democratic resilience and rule of law compliance. According to the 2025 EU Rule of Law Report, accreditation for two NHRIs remains stalled, reflecting broader structural and political challenges within the country's civic space and governance framework.

## Key Details
- Serve as independent oversight mechanisms to ensure state compliance with domestic constitutional guarantees and international human rights obligations.
- Typically require accreditation by the Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions (GANHRI) based on adherence to the Paris Principles.
- In Romania, stalled accreditation highlights ongoing friction between executive legislative priorities and the operational independence of rights-monitoring bodies.
- Their effectiveness is closely tied to transparent funding, protection from political interference, and integration into broader anti-corruption and civic space initiatives.
- Frequently referenced in EU rule of law assessments as a barometer for institutional governance and democratic backsliding risks.
