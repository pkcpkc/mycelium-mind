# Labor Market Dynamics

## Summary

Poland's labor market is currently tight, driving strong nominal wage growth that supports domestic consumption but contributes to inflationary pressures above the central bank's target. Medium-term dynamics are constrained by demographic headwinds, specifically population aging, which threatens labor supply and pension adequacy. Structural reforms focus on integrating refugees and foreign workers, enhancing vocational training, and improving childcare infrastructure to bolster labor supply and long-term growth potential.

## Key Details

- **Labor Market Tightness:** The labor market is tight, leading to strong wage growth that acts as a key driver of private consumption but also sustains core inflation, delaying monetary policy easing until wage deceleration is observed.
- **Wage Dynamics:** Strong nominal wage growth fuels economic recovery; however, persistent wage pressures require monitoring to ensure alignment with disinflation goals and fiscal sustainability.
- **Demographic Headwinds:** Population aging poses significant risks to labor supply and the sustainability of pension systems, necessitating policy interventions to mitigate supply constraints and maintain economic convergence.
- **Refugee and Foreign Worker Integration:** A critical structural priority is the integration of Ukrainian refugees and foreign workers into the labor market to address labor shortages and support economic activity.
- **Human Capital and Infrastructure:** Recommendations include improving vocational training systems and expanding childcare infrastructure to increase labor force participation, efficiency, and workforce resilience.
- **Public Sector Compensation:** Permanent increases in public sector wages and social benefits have contributed to fiscal expansion, highlighting the need for balanced wage dynamics within broader fiscal consolidation efforts.
