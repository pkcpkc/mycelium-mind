# Discretionary Judgment

## Summary

Discretionary judgment refers to the subjective adjustments and qualitative overrides applied by rating analysts within structured evaluation frameworks. In sovereign credit rating methodologies, it allows analysts to adjust indicative scores based on factors that are difficult to quantify or model, such as cohesive civil society, event risk, and extreme liquidity constraints. This practice highlights the inherent tension between standardized quantitative metrics and the nuanced, experience-based assessments required in complex financial environments.

## Key Details

- Allows for subjective adjustments to indicative ratings based on qualitative factors like civil society cohesion, event risk, and liquidity constraints.
- Can override indicative ratings by more than one notch due to unspecified qualitative considerations, introducing opacity into the rating process.
- Creates a structural tension between standardized, transparent metrics and opaque, experience-driven analyst assessments.
- The lack of full replicability due to discretionary elements has motivated citizen science initiatives to systematically document and standardize the application of rating methodologies across jurisdictions.
