# Media Freedom

## Summary
Media freedom represents the legal and operational safeguards protecting journalists, ensuring editorial independence of public service broadcasting, and maintaining a pluralistic media landscape against market consolidation and legal intimidation. In recent EU assessments of Malta, this domain faces structural vulnerabilities including restricted transparency, pending broader protections, and lingering impacts from high-profile attacks on press safety.

## Key Details
- Legislative efforts are underway to expand the national media regulator's oversight of market mergers and state advertising allocations.
- Editorial independence of public service media remains unaddressed, with no concrete progress reported by regulatory or parliamentary bodies.
- Access to official documents is currently restricted, limiting transparency and hindering investigative journalism.
- A targeted law addressing cross-border Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) has been enacted to shield journalists and publishers from financial and legal intimidation.
- Broader statutory protections for journalists, including safety protocols and whistleblower safeguards, remain pending implementation.
- The unresolved assassination of journalist Daphne Caruana Galizia continues to cast a long shadow over media safety, anti-corruption accountability, and public trust in investigative reporting.
