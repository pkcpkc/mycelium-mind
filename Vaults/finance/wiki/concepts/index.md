# Concepts

* [[Active Labor Market Policies]] - Interventions designed to improve labor market functioning and workforce productivity, identified as a key structural reform in Slovakia to address labor shortages and support economic diversification.
* [[Active Labour Market Policies (ALMPs)]] - Policies designed to increase the participation of individuals in the labor market through training, education, and job placement.
* [[AFA]] - France's central anti-corruption agency responsible for compliance monitoring, prevention, and detection of corruption in both public and private sectors.
* [[Ageing Costs]] - Ageing costs refer to the escalating public expenditure burden on national budgets resulting from demographic shifts, particularly pension and long-term care provisions, which are projected to drive fiscal deterioration through 2070.
* [[Aging population pressures]] - Demographic trends where an increasing proportion of older individuals strain public finances, labor markets, and social welfare systems.
* [[AI in Justice]] - The application of artificial intelligence technologies within judicial and legal systems to support case management, decision-making, and access to law.
* [[Anti-Corruption]] - A framework of laws, regulations, and institutional measures designed to prevent, detect, investigate, and penalize corrupt practices across public and private sectors.
* [[Anti-Corruption Framework]] - A comprehensive system of laws, policies, and institutional measures designed to prevent, detect, and address corruption within public administration and private sectors, with jurisdictional variations observed across EU member states including Austria, Belgium, Hungary, Italy, Malta, Poland, and Slovakia.
* [[Anti-Corruption Strategy]] - A comprehensive policy framework and institutional arrangement designed to prevent, detect, and prosecute corruption, encompassing specialized agencies, legislative measures, and transparency mechanisms.
* [[Anti-Money Laundering]] - Regulatory and operational frameworks designed to prevent the concealment of illegally obtained funds, identified as a critical structural reform for maintaining investor confidence and financial stability in Malta.
* [[ARCOM]] - France's media regulator responsible for overseeing broadcasting, digital services, and media pluralism.
* [[Article IV Consultation]] - A periodic economic assessment conducted by the International Monetary Fund (IMF) for member countries, involving staff analysis, policy dialogue, and Executive Board conclusions to monitor macroeconomic stability, fiscal sustainability, and recommend structural reforms, with recent reviews covering diverse economies like Hungary, Italy, Malta, Romania, and Slovakia.
* [[Artificial Intelligence]] - A strategic technology sector leveraged for structural reforms, innovation financing, and long-term productivity growth.
* [[Asset Declarations]] - A transparency mechanism for disclosing financial interests that, in Belgium, currently lacks independent verification and public accessibility, being restricted to criminal investigations.
* [[Austria]] - A Central European economy navigating post-recession recovery, facing fiscal consolidation pressures, demographic headwinds, structural reforms, and innovation-to-productivity gaps amid elevated inflation and financial sector vulnerabilities.
* [[Automatic wage indexation]] - A policy mechanism linking wage adjustments to inflation or economic indicators, currently under review in Belgium to enhance labor market flexibility and reduce fiscal pressures.
* [[Banking Sector]] - The banking sector encompasses financial intermediation institutions and systems, characterized by robust capital buffers and profitability in mature markets like Italy, while requiring ongoing supervisory vigilance over sovereign-bank linkages, foreign exchange exposures, and consumer credit growth in emerging economies like Romania.
* [[BICRA]] - S&P Global Ratings framework for assessing banking system contingent liabilities within sovereign credit evaluations.
* [[Business Dynamism]] - The vitality of market entry, firm growth, and resource reallocation, often constrained by regulatory burdens and innovation concentration.
* [[Business Environment]] - The regulatory, administrative, and institutional conditions shaping commercial operations, investment climate, and long-term competitiveness within a jurisdiction.
* [[Capital market union]] - An EU policy initiative aimed at integrating fragmented national capital markets into a unified European market to improve corporate financing, enhance economic competitiveness, and strengthen long-term growth.
* [[Carbon Tax]] - A policy instrument that imposes a fee on the carbon content of fuels, intended to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.
* [[Checks and Balances]] - A governance framework designed to distribute power across state institutions, often tested when emergency powers bypass legislative oversight and judicial appointments become politicized.
* [[circular economy|Circular Economy]] - An economic model focused on minimizing waste and maximizing resource efficiency, identified as a key component of Austria's sustainable transition strategy, a recommended reform for Hungary, and a target for improvement in Poland, Romania, and France.
* [[Civic Space]] - Civic space refers to the environment facilitating free expression, assembly, and association, currently facing emerging pressures in Belgium, narrowed conditions in France due to intimidation, and structural challenges in Romania requiring an Open Government Strategy, per the 2025 EU Rule of Law Reports.
* [[Civil Society]] - Independent organizations and citizen initiatives that participate in democratic processes, monitor state institutions, and advocate for public interest, serving as a core indicator of rule of law health and civic space vitality across EU member states.
* [[Civil Society Organisations]] - Non-governmental entities representing citizen interests, operating within the civic space to influence policy, uphold human rights, and contribute to democratic checks and balances.
* [[Civil Society Space]] - The operational environment and legal protections allowing independent NGOs and civic organizations to function, advocate, and receive funding without undue political interference.
* [[Climate Change Mitigation]] - Strategies and policies aimed at reducing environmental impact and greenhouse gas emissions, emphasized in macroeconomic frameworks to balance growth with long-term ecological resilience.
* [[Climate Risk]] - The potential for economic, social, and environmental damage resulting from extreme weather events and long-term climatic shifts.
* [[Climate Risks]] - Macro-critical threats to economic stability and growth, necessitating mitigation through renewable energy transition and resilient infrastructure investment.
* [[Climate Transition]] - A strategic economic and policy shift toward low-carbon systems, essential for long-term competitiveness and sustainable growth.
* [[Commercial Real Estate Risk]] - Financial vulnerability within the commercial property sector characterized by rising non-performing loans and declining collateral values, particularly exacerbated by monetary tightening and economic slowdowns.
* [[constitutional reform|Constitutional Reform]] - A proposed amendment to Italy's constitutional framework aimed at establishing the direct election of the Prime Minister, raising debates regarding institutional balance and democratic oversight.
* [[Constitutional Tribunal]] - Poland's highest constitutional court, currently at the center of a deepening institutional crisis due to non-recognition of its rulings and blocked judicial appointments.
* [[Contingent Liabilities]] - Potential future financial obligations that may materialize as actual sovereign debt depending on triggering events, structured as fiscal stocks within sovereign rating frameworks like the S&P 2017 methodology.
* [[Corporate Income Tax]] - A direct tax levied on corporate profits, currently undergoing structural reform in Malta to align with EU Pillar II standards and prevent international revenue leakage.
* [[Corporate Productivity]] - The efficiency with which businesses and industries convert inputs into outputs, serving as a fundamental driver of long-term economic growth, competitiveness, and fiscal sustainability.
* [[Credit Fundamentals]] - The core analytical framework and criteria used to evaluate the creditworthiness of entities, particularly sovereign states, encompassing institutional, economic, external, fiscal, and monetary dimensions.
* [[Current Account Surplus]] - A macroeconomic condition where a nation's net exports, income from abroad, and transfers exceed its imports; in [[Hungary]]'s context, it relates to external vulnerabilities, energy diversification, and the impact of [[EU Funds]] on financing.
* [[decarbonisation|Decarbonisation]] - The strategic process of reducing carbon emissions and phasing out fossil fuel dependence through renewable energy adoption, industrial innovation, and targeted policy reforms.
* [[Decarbonization]] - The process of reducing carbon dioxide emissions and transitioning away from fossil fuels, involving structural shifts in energy generation, transport, waste management, and industrial practices to achieve environmental sustainability.
* [[Demographic Aging]] - A structural challenge where aging-related expenditures increase fiscal pressures, requiring labor supply expansion and infrastructure scaling to offset population decline.
* [[Demographic challenges]] - Structural economic headwinds arising from population aging, labor shortages, and shifting workforce demographics that require targeted policy interventions.
* [[Demographic Trends]] - Long-term shifts in population structure, particularly aging and dependency ratios, that significantly impact economic growth, labor markets, and fiscal sustainability.
* [[Digital Decade]] - A European Union initiative aimed at accelerating the digital transformation of the economy and society.
* [[Digital Transformation]] - The integration of digital technology into all areas of business and society, fundamentally changing how organizations operate and deliver value.
* [[Digital Transition]] - The process of integrating digital technologies into all areas of economy, society, and governance to improve productivity and sustainability.
* [[Digitalisation]] - The integration of digital technologies into institutional workflows, notably within France's justice system to enhance efficiency and modernize case management.
* [[Discretionary Judgment]] - The subjective adjustments and qualitative overrides applied by analysts within structured rating frameworks, particularly when standardized metrics cannot fully capture complex economic or institutional realities.
* [[ECB]] - The central bank for the eurozone responsible for conducting monetary policy and maintaining price stability by targeting an inflation rate of 2%.
* [[Economic Assessment]] - A core pillar in sovereign credit rating methodologies that evaluates a nation's fundamental economic strength, anchored by GDP per capita and adjusted for growth prospects, diversity, data quality, and future tax capacity.
* [[Economic Growth]] - The expansion of an economy's production capacity over time, typically measured by real GDP growth and increasingly evaluated through structural, environmental, social, and fiscal sustainability lenses.
* [[ECtHR]] - The European Court of Human Rights, an international judicial body that adjudicates claims of violations of the European Convention on Human Rights.
* [[Education]] - Systems of teaching, learning, and skill development that form the foundation of human capital and social mobility, currently facing structural inefficiencies and socioeconomic stratification in national policy analyses.
* [[Employment]] - The engagement of the workforce in economic activity, characterized by exceptional performance metrics in Malta but challenged by structural skills mismatches and persistent social inequalities.
* [[energy affordability|Energy Affordability]] - Energy affordability refers to the accessibility of energy services at costs that do not unduly burden households or impede industrial competitiveness, serving as a critical determinant of social fairness and economic resilience.
* [[Energy Security]] - Energy security denotes the reliable and affordable access to energy resources, increasingly recognized as a macro-critical priority for economic resilience and climate risk mitigation.
* [[Energy Transition]] - The global shift from fossil-based energy systems to renewable and sustainable energy sources.
* [[ETS II]] - EU emissions trading scheme targeting transport and buildings sectors.
* [[ETS2]] - EU Emissions Trading System 2, a market-based mechanism for reducing greenhouse gas emissions and facilitating the transition to a low-carbon economy.
* [[EU Funds]] - Financial resources provided by the European Union, including structural, cohesion, and NextGenerationEU (NGEU) instruments, subject to governance, rule-of-law, anti-corruption, and judicial conditionality that influence disbursement timelines and recipient macroeconomic stability.
* [[EU Single Market]] - A framework for economic integration and regulatory harmonization aimed at unlocking economies of scale, facilitating capital market financing, and reducing trade barriers within the European Union.
* [[Euro Area]] - Monetary and economic union framework influencing Slovakia's fiscal consolidation, single market integration, and structural reforms within the European context.
* [[European Commission]] - The executive branch of the European Union responsible for publishing comprehensive assessments like Country Reports, managing funding instruments such as the Recovery and Resilience Facility, and overseeing alignment with EU targets on economic, social, and environmental performance.
* [[European Court of Human Rights]] - International judicial body that ensures member states comply with the European Convention on Human Rights, serving as a key benchmark for national rule of law compliance and institutional reform.
* [[European Court of Justice]] - The supreme judicial authority of the European Union responsible for ensuring uniform interpretation and application of EU law across member states.
* [[European Public Prosecutor Office]] - The independent EU prosecutorial office responsible for investigating, prosecuting, and bringing to judgment perpetrators of crimes affecting the financial interests of the European Union.
* [[European Union]] - A political and economic union of member states that coordinates single market integration, fiscal governance, regional recovery frameworks, and climate/fiscal conditionalities for fund disbursement.
* [[External Assessment]] - A core evaluation pillar in S&P Global Ratings' sovereign creditworthiness framework that analyzes currency status, foreign exchange liquidity, the balance of residents' external assets and liabilities, and external indebtedness.
* [[Fast-Track Legislation]] - A legislative practice involving expedited parliamentary procedures that systematically bypasses meaningful stakeholder consultation, notably utilized in Slovakia to strain institutional checks and balances.
* [[Federal Prosecution Office]] - A proposed independent judicial institution in Austria intended to separate prosecutorial supervision from political influence, currently operating under direct ministerial oversight.
* [[Financial sector resilience]] - The capacity of a financial system to withstand economic shocks, manage sectoral vulnerabilities, and maintain stability through effective macroprudential oversight and crisis management frameworks.
* [[Financial Sector Stability]] - The resilience and soundness of a jurisdiction's banking and financial infrastructure, characterized by capital adequacy, liquidity, profitability, and effective macroprudential oversight; recent IMF assessments highlight robust stability in Austria, Italy, and Poland, though distinct regional vulnerabilities including CRE exposure, sovereign-bank nexus risks, legacy mortgage litigation, and shallow capital markets require sustained regulatory vigilance.
* [[Financial stability|Financial Stability]] - The resilience of the financial system to withstand economic shocks and maintain intermediation functions, requiring robust macroprudential buffers, regulatory implementation, vigilant monitoring of non-bank institutions, cyber threats, climate vulnerabilities, and sector-specific risks like real estate concentration.
* [[Fiscal Adjustment]] - The process of reducing government budget deficits and debt levels to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability.
* [[Fiscal Assessment]] - A core evaluation pillar in sovereign creditworthiness frameworks that analyzes a government's fiscal performance, flexibility, and debt burden.
* [[Fiscal Consolidation|Fiscal consolidation]] - Fiscal consolidation refers to structured government policies aimed at reducing budget deficits and stabilizing public debt, typically implemented through targeted spending reforms, revenue base broadening, and medium-term structural plans, as frequently recommended in multilateral assessments like IMF Article IV consultations and EU Commission country reports.
* [[Fiscal Deficit]] - The gap between government revenue and expenditure, indicating the need for borrowing or deficit reduction measures within a national economy.
* [[Fiscal Framework]] - A structural system for multiannual fiscal planning and budgetary governance that guides medium-term economic trajectories, public finance management, and institutional credibility.
* [[Fiscal Policy]] - The strategic use of government taxation and public expenditure to influence macroeconomic performance, ensure debt sustainability, and drive structural reforms, as illustrated by consolidation efforts in Italy, Malta, and Hungary.
* [[Fiscal Sustainability]] - Fiscal sustainability denotes the capacity of a government to maintain its fiscal trajectory without compromising future obligations, emphasizing structural primary surpluses, debt management, and the mitigation of contingent liabilities through high-quality adjustment and structural reforms.
* [[Foreign-Currency Rating]] - A sovereign credit rating designation used primarily by S&P Global Ratings to assess the creditworthiness of a national government or monetary authority specifically for its obligations denominated in foreign currency.
* [[Freedom of Information Act]] - Legislation governing public access to government information, with implementations varying significantly by jurisdiction regarding transparency, response times, and access barriers.
* [[GDP]] - Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total monetary or market value of all finished goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period.
* [[GDP Growth]] - A macroeconomic indicator measuring the annualized percentage change in a country's gross domestic product, reflecting economic expansion or contraction.
* [[GDP per Capita]] - A macroeconomic indicator measuring average economic output per person, serving as a foundational anchor in sovereign credit assessments and economic profiling.
* [[Governance]] - The institutional framework and administrative processes that ensure policy implementation, rule of law, and public accountability, serving as a foundation for fiscal stability and international funding.
* [[Government Emergency Ordinances]] - Executive legislative instruments issued during emergencies that carry the force of law, whose frequent application can undermine regulatory stability and legislative predictability.
* [[Green Transition]] - The structural and economic shift toward sustainable practices, identified as a critical lever for restoring productivity, aligning fiscal policy, and driving innovation financing within broader macroeconomic frameworks.
* [[HATVP]] - A French independent administrative authority responsible for ensuring transparency in public life, managing asset declarations, and regulating lobbying activities.
* [[Healthcare]] - The organized provision of medical services and systems for maintaining and restoring health.
* [[housing affordability|Housing Affordability]] - The degree to which housing costs are manageable relative to household incomes, serving as a critical indicator of social fairness and economic inequality.
* [[Hungary]] - A Central European EU member state navigating economic stagnation and persistent inflation as of 2025, requiring fiscal consolidation, structural reforms, and governance improvements to unlock suspended EU Funds.
* [[IMF]] - The International Monetary Fund (IMF) is an international organization that conducts Article IV consultations to evaluate member states' economic policies, providing targeted recommendations for fiscal consolidation, structural reforms, monetary stability, and long-term macroeconomic resilience.
* [[IMF Article IV Consultation]] - A periodic economic assessment conducted by the International Monetary Fund to evaluate member countries' macroeconomic policies, fiscal sustainability, structural reform progress, and financial sector stability, with recent assessments highlighting challenges in fiscal consolidation, AI integration, and pension reforms.
* [[Inflation]] - A sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services, characterized by divergent regional trajectories driven by labor markets, energy policies, indirect taxes, and global commodity prices, with central banks targeting gradual moderation to structural benchmarks.
* [[Inflation Targeting]] - A monetary policy framework wherein a central bank publicly announces and commits to a specific inflation rate target to guide expectations, anchor prices, and stabilize the macroeconomy.
* [[innovation]] - A systemic economic driver characterized by research and development investment, science-business collaboration, and the translation of technological advances into business dynamism and productivity growth.
* [[Innovation and Digitalization]] - Strategic shift towards productivity-driven growth through acceleration of innovation, venture capital expansion, and development of digital/STEM skills.
* [[Innovation Diffusion]] - The process by which research and development outputs, technological advancements, and operational best practices spread from leading organizations to the broader economy, particularly SMEs and adjacent sectors.
* [[Institutional Assessment]] - A core evaluation pillar in sovereign creditworthiness frameworks that examines policymaking effectiveness, institutional stability, civil society accountability, transparency, debt payment culture, and security risks.
* [[International Monetary Fund]] - An international organization that conducts economic monitoring, policy assessments, and provides strategic recommendations to member countries to ensure macroeconomic stability, fiscal sustainability, and structural resilience, often engaging with regional frameworks like the European Union.
* [[Investment]] - The allocation of resources, capital, or effort into assets or projects with the expectation of generating future benefits or growth.
* [[Investor Citizenship Scheme]] - A policy framework allowing foreign nationals to acquire citizenship through financial investment, which has faced significant legal challenges in Malta.
* [[Italy]] - A Southern European economy navigating fiscal consolidation, structural reforms, and demographic headwinds while maintaining resilient labor markets and financial sector stability.
* [[Judicial Independence]] - A core rule of law principle ensuring the judiciary operates free from external interference; across EU member states including Austria, Belgium, France, Hungary, Italy, Malta, Poland, and Slovakia, perceived independence varies significantly, with ongoing reforms addressing appointment mechanisms, prosecutorial autonomy, systemic backlogs, digital oversight, judicial council safeguards, and deep institutional crises.
* [[Justice Laws]] - Legislative frameworks and reforms governing Romania's justice system, focusing on judicial independence, institutional mandates, and operational transparency.
* [[Justice System Efficiency]] - Assessment of the speed, resource allocation, and digitalization of judicial processes, highlighting challenges with backlogs, structural deficiencies, and digital oversight risks in Belgium.
* [[justice system reform|Justice System Reform]] - Structural and procedural changes aimed at enhancing judicial efficiency, independence, digitalisation, and accountability within the legal framework.
* [[Labor Force Participation]] - A macroeconomic indicator measuring the share of the working-age population engaged in or seeking work, identified as a critical structural reform for unlocking long-term growth potential.
* [[Labor Market]] - The labor market represents the ecosystem of employment and labor supply, characterized by region-specific structural dynamics such as record employment in Italy, exceptional tightness in Malta, and wage-driven recovery facing demographic headwinds in Slovakia, all requiring targeted reforms to sustain productivity and mitigate structural challenges.
* [[Labor Market Dynamics]] - Refers to the interplay of tight labor conditions, strong nominal wage growth, and demographic pressures affecting Poland's economic outlook and inflation trajectory.
* [[Labor market flexibility]] - The capacity of a labor market to adjust wages, employment conditions, and benefit structures in response to economic shocks and demographic changes, often achieved through reforms to wage-setting mechanisms and social safety nets.
* [[Labor Market Reforms]] - Structural policy measures aimed at expanding labor supply and improving workforce participation to offset demographic headwinds and support economic recovery.
* [[Labor Productivity]] - A measure of economic efficiency representing the amount of goods and services produced per hour of work or per worker.
* [[Labour Market]] - The economic ecosystem governing employment, workforce participation, and labor conditions. Characterized in recent EU assessments by exceptional headline performance in some member states alongside widespread structural bottlenecks, including skills shortages, declining working hours, and demographic participation gaps.
* [[Labour Market Participation]] - The proportion of the working-age population that is employed or actively seeking work, characterized in Belgium by record employment levels coexisting with low activity rates among vulnerable groups and structural disincentives to labour supply.
* [[Labour Productivity]] - A measure of economic efficiency representing the amount of goods and services produced per hour of labor.
* [[Legislative Simplification]] - Systematic efforts to streamline, consolidate, and reduce the complexity of national legislation to improve legal clarity, administrative efficiency, and the business environment.
* [[Lobbying]] - Regulatory transparency and ethics framework related to public interest interactions, overseen by the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) and integrated into anti-corruption measures.
* [[Lobbying Reform]] - Legislative initiative to regulate lobbying activities, currently stalled in Belgium with the new government yet to decide on draft rules as per the 2025 Rule of Law Report.
* [[lobbying regulation]] - Regulatory frameworks governing interactions between stakeholders and public officials, focusing on transparency, disclosure, and alignment with anti-corruption efforts.
* [[Local Government Spending]] - Subnational government expenditures identified as a key area for fiscal consolidation and rationalization in France's 2025 IMF Article IV Consultation.
* [[Local-Currency Rating]] - A sovereign credit assessment evaluating a government's ability to meet debt obligations denominated in its domestic currency, typically constrained relative to foreign-currency ratings.
* [[Macroeconomic Policy]] - Strategic frameworks and interventions used by governments and central banks to influence aggregate economic performance, encompassing fiscal consolidation, financial stability measures, and structural reforms aimed at sustainable growth and productivity.
* [[Magisterial Inquiries]] - A specialized judicial mechanism in Malta for investigating serious crimes, notably subject to recent legislative changes that have raised concerns regarding high-level accountability and access to justice.
* [[Magistrates]] - Judicial officers in France whose status, independence, and operational efficiency are central to the national justice system and rule of law framework.
* [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]] - The central bank of Hungary, responsible for issuing currency, formulating monetary policy, and maintaining price stability within a formal inflation targeting framework.
* [[Malta]] - A Mediterranean EU member state characterized by robust macroeconomic performance and employment rates, but facing significant structural challenges in research investment, decarbonization, water stress, and social inclusivity.
* [[Media Freedom]] - Media freedom represents the legal and operational safeguards protecting journalists, ensuring editorial independence of public service broadcasting, and maintaining a pluralistic media landscape against market consolidation and legal intimidation.
* [[Media Ownership Transparency]] - Mechanisms and initiatives to ensure clear, accessible public information about media ownership to safeguard pluralism, accountability, and democratic oversight.
* [[Media Pluralism]] - The diversity of media ownership, content, and voices within a society, encompassing regulatory independence, transparent funding mechanisms, protections for journalistic safety, and compliance with EU media mandates. Recent assessments highlight divergent national trajectories across the EU, including ongoing reforms in Austria and Belgium, systemic challenges in Hungary, mixed progress in Italy regarding regulatory stability and journalist protections, emerging regulatory shifts and unresolved safety challenges in Malta, active legislative recalibration alongside transitional funding models in Poland, and persistent challenges in Romania regarding public service independence and media ownership transparency.
* [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan]] - A seven-year fiscal adjustment framework proposed by the Italian authorities to consolidate public finances and place high public debt on a downward trajectory.
* [[Monetary Assessment]] - A core pillar in S&P Global Ratings' sovereign creditworthiness framework that evaluates exchange rate regimes, monetary policy credibility, price stability, and financial market depth.
* [[Monetary Policy]] - Monetary policy refers to the centralized management of money supply, interest rates, and credit conditions by a central bank or monetary authority to achieve macroeconomic objectives such as price stability and sustainable growth, with recent applications in Euro area tightening, Hungarian stabilization, and Poland's inflation-targeting framework.

* [[Neighbouring Rights]] - A legislative and legal framework designed to ensure fair compensation for press content when utilized by digital platforms.
* [[Net General Government Debt]] - A fiscal metric representing gross general government debt minus financial assets, used as a core indicator for assessing sovereign fiscal performance and debt sustainability.

* [[Population Aging]] - A demographic trend characterized by a rising proportion of older individuals in a population, which constrains economic growth, strains public pension systems, and necessitates structural labor market reforms.
* [[Portalis]] - A digitalisation tool within the French justice system aimed at improving judicial processes and efficiency, though subject to implementation challenges and technical issues.
