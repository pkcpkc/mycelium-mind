# Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation

## Summary
Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation (SLAPPs) are legal actions filed primarily to censor, intimidate, or silence critics by burdening them with the cost of a defensive legal fight. In Poland's 2025 rule of law assessment, a notable decrease in SLAPP incidents has been reported, coinciding with ongoing legislative efforts to implement EU anti-SLAPP directives aimed at safeguarding journalistic sources and media pluralism.

## Key Details
- **Definition & Purpose:** SLAPPs are typically strategic or meritless lawsuits designed to deter public participation in matters of public interest by imposing significant financial, temporal, and psychological strain on defendants until they withdraw their criticism or activism.
- **Target Demographics:** Primarily targets journalists, activists, academics, whistleblowers, and civil society organizations to suppress dissent and protect editorial independence.
- **EU Regulatory Framework:** The European Union is advancing anti-SLAPP directives to harmonize member state protections, mandating early dismissal procedures for clearly abusive claims and shifting legal costs to defendants who file SLAPPs.
- **Poland Context:** Recent rule of law evaluations highlight a reduction in SLAPP filings, with the government drafting reforms to align national legislation with EU anti-SLAPP standards and strengthen protections for journalistic sources and civic engagement.
