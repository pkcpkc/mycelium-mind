# Magistrates

## Summary

Magistrates in France are judicial officers responsible for adjudicating cases and upholding the rule of law. Their professional status, institutional independence, and operational efficiency are critical to the functioning of the justice system. Recent legislative reforms and budgetary measures have aimed to modernize career pathways, strengthen ethical safeguards, and address systemic challenges such as court backlogs and public perception of independence.

## Key Details

- **Status Reform (2023):** The Law of 20 November 2023 reformed the status of magistrates, notably by repealing age limits for admission and introducing 360° performance evaluations for court presidents to enhance accountability.
- **Independence & Institutional Defense:** The Superior Council of Magistracy actively defends judicial independence, particularly in response to personal attacks on magistrates following national elections. New ethics measures have been specifically adopted for magistrates.
- **Resource Allocation & Digitalisation:** Budgetary efforts prioritize increasing the number of magistrates to alleviate chronic court backlogs, particularly in bribery and family law matters. Implementation of digital tools like Portalis continues to face technical hurdles that impact processing speed.
- **Efficiency & Backlog Mitigation:** The Ministry of Justice has launched "flash missions" to explore solutions for reducing case resolution times and improving sentence execution. Average times to resolve cases remain high, prompting ongoing judicial simplification efforts.
- **Whistleblower & Ethical Frameworks:** Increasing whistleblower reports have prompted calls for stronger support and protection mechanisms within the judiciary. Enhanced resources have been allocated to prosecution bodies like the PNF and OCLCIFF to handle complex cases involving magistrates and public officials.
- **European Compliance:** France continues to face challenges in implementing European Court of Human Rights judgments, with a significant number of leading judgments pending regarding judicial standards, procedure fairness, and executive enforcement.
