# Fiscal Deficit

## Summary

The fiscal deficit represents the shortfall between a government's total revenue and its total expenditure over a specific period, indicating the need for borrowing or deficit reduction measures. Within recent international economic assessments, it serves as a critical indicator of fiscal sustainability and consolidation progress across different regional frameworks. Projections highlight divergent trajectories aligned with local policy priorities: the Maltese economy anticipates a steady decline to 3.2% of GDP in 2025 as part of EU-aligned consolidation efforts, while Poland's deficit widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 due to expansionary domestic measures, prompting IMF recommendations for a structured medium-term adjustment plan targeting 2.9% by 2028.

## Key Details

- Projected to decline to 3.2% of GDP in 2025, reflecting steady fiscal consolidation and improved revenue management in the Maltese/EU context.
- Widened to 5.9% of GDP in 2024 in Poland, driven by permanent increases in public sector wages, social benefits, and elevated defense spending.
- Closely tracked alongside public debt metrics, with trajectories stabilizing below the 50% threshold in Malta and targeting 60-65% of GDP in Poland.
- Supports broader macroeconomic resilience driven by strong domestic demand and robust service sector exports, while monitoring inflation and monetary policy interactions.
- Monitored by the European Commission and IMF to ensure alignment with fiscal sustainability frameworks, with Poland's authorities introducing a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan.
- Impacted by structural factors including tax policy, administrative efficiency, strategic investments (e.g., EU Recovery and Resilience Facility), and the need for frontloading consolidation measures to rebuild buffers.
- IMF recommendations emphasize improving fiscal transparency, including the establishment of an independent fiscal council and medium-term consolidation to 2.9% by 2028 in Poland.
