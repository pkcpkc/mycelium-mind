# Financial sector resilience

## Summary

Financial sector resilience refers to the ability of a nation's financial institutions and markets to absorb economic shocks, maintain liquidity and capital adequacy, and continue functioning effectively during periods of stress. In the context of the Slovak Republic's 2025 economic assessment, resilience is characterized by a well-capitalized and liquid banking system capable of withstanding severe macro-financial shocks. However, systemic vulnerabilities persist in the real estate sector, particularly regarding upcoming mortgage repricing and high commercial office vacancy rates. Sustaining this resilience requires careful macroprudential management, targeted supervisory enhancements, and robust crisis preparedness.

## Key Details

- **Banking System Strength:** The domestic banking sector remains well-capitalized and liquid, with stress tests confirming its capacity to absorb severe macro-financial shocks.
- **Real Estate Vulnerabilities:** Residential and commercial real estate (CRE) markets face notable risks, including upcoming mortgage repricing pressures and elevated office vacancy rates.
- **Macroprudential Policy:** Authorities are advised to maintain the current macroprudential stance while exploring the adoption of a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer (pnCCyB) and closing loopholes in borrower-based measures.
- **Supervisory Enhancement:** Continuous improvement is needed in the oversight of less significant financial institutions to ensure consistent risk mitigation and compliance.
- **Crisis Management Framework:** Strengthening the national crisis management framework is critical, with emphasis on ensuring the operational independence and legal protection of financial supervisors.
