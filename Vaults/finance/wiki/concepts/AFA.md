# AFA

## Summary

The Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA) is France's national authority tasked with preventing and detecting corruption, promoting compliance with anti-corruption standards, and supporting the implementation of the Sapin II framework. In the 2025 Rule of Law Report, the AFA is highlighted as a key institution maintaining France's relatively low perception of corruption while undergoing strategic reorganization to enhance its operational scope and effectiveness.

## Key Details

- **Organizational Reform:** The AFA has been restructured to clearly separate public and private sector functions, allowing for more targeted compliance monitoring and enforcement strategies.
- **Institutional Ecosystem:** Works in tandem with complementary bodies such as the High Authority for Transparency in Public Life (HATVP) for asset declarations, the Central Office for Combating Corruption and Tax Offences (OCLCIFF) for investigations, and the National Financial Prosecutor (PNF) for prosecutions.
- **Performance Metrics:** Contributes to France's strong standing in the Corruption Perceptions Index (scoring 67/100), reflecting effective institutional oversight and deterrence.
- **Whistleblower & Sectoral Focus:** Processes increasing whistleblower reports, calls for enhanced protection mechanisms, and leads targeted initiatives against corruption in high-risk areas like organized drug trafficking and port activities.
- **Legislative Alignment:** Operates within a broader anti-corruption framework that includes new ethics measures for parliamentarians, ministers, and magistrates, alongside ongoing efforts to safeguard civic space and public integrity.
