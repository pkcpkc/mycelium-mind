# Real estate market

## Summary

The real estate market encompasses the dynamics of residential and commercial property sectors across various economies. Regional manifestations differ significantly: in Malta, the sector has experienced renewed activity driven by foreign workers and tourists, leading to rising rents and concentrated banking exposures. Conversely, in Slovakia, IMF assessments highlight vulnerabilities stemming from upcoming mortgage repricing cycles and elevated commercial office vacancy rates. Across jurisdictions, the sector's performance is closely intertwined with broader macroeconomic conditions, fiscal frameworks, and financial stability, necessitating targeted macroprudential oversight and structural policy adaptations.

## Key Details

- **Regional Demand & Supply Dynamics:** In Malta, market activity correlates with robust service-sector growth and an influx of foreign workers and tourists, creating upward pressure on rental prices and straining infrastructure capacity.
- **Financing & Repricing Vulnerabilities:** In Slovakia, residential and commercial real estate (CRE) markets face headwinds from upcoming mortgage repricing and elevated office vacancy rates, which could dampen local economic resilience.
- **Concentrated Financial Exposures:** Banking sectors in multiple jurisdictions maintain significant exposures to real estate, creating systemic vulnerabilities that require continuous monitoring and targeted supervision.
- **Macroprudential & Fiscal Policy Interplay:** Regulators are urged to maintain or adjust macroprudential stances, including exploring positive neutral countercyclical capital buffers (pnCCyB) and closing loopholes in borrower-based measures. Additionally, market-value-based property taxes are increasingly viewed as tools for fiscal consolidation and revenue stabilization.
- **Infrastructure & Governance Strain:** Real estate growth can strain national infrastructure and public services, while weak governance and corruption risks may impede the effective deployment of public funds and EU investments tied to housing and commercial development.
