# Foreign-Currency Rating

## Summary

[[Foreign-Currency Rating]] is a sovereign credit rating designation used primarily by S&P Global Ratings to assess the creditworthiness of a national government or monetary authority specifically for its obligations denominated in foreign currency. It serves as the baseline reference point for determining corresponding local-currency ratings and is a central component of the sovereign rating methodology.

## Key Details

- **Methodology Framework:** Evaluated under the [[Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017)]], which forms one half of the overall sovereign rating alongside local-currency considerations.
- **Local-Currency Uplift Relationship:** Local-currency ratings are typically capped at one notch above the foreign-currency rating. This one-notch uplift is permitted only if the sovereign maintains independent monetary policy, possesses deep local capital markets, and has sufficient institutional and fiscal flexibility to prioritize domestic obligations.
- **Distress Scenarios:** In severe distress (e.g., when the [[Foreign-Currency Rating]] is near 'CCC'), local and foreign-currency ratings may diverge significantly if the default risks differ materially between currency types.
- **Monetary Union Considerations:** Members of a [[Monetary Union]] have their external and monetary scores adjusted for policy synchronization and liquidity constraints, which directly impacts the foreign-currency rating baseline.
- **Rating Determination:** The indicative rating derived from the Institutional and Economic Profile and Flexibility and Performance Profile matrix typically falls within one notch of the final foreign-currency rating, subject to supplemental adjustment factors for extreme external or fiscal stress.
