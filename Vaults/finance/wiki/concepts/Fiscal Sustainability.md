# Fiscal Sustainability

## Summary

Fiscal sustainability refers to the ability of a government to maintain its current spending and tax policies without compromising its ability to meet future financial obligations, ensuring long-term economic stability. It involves maintaining a manageable debt-to-GDP ratio, achieving structural primary surpluses to rebuild fiscal buffers, and implementing reforms to address long-term expenditure pressures. 

Recent analyses highlight diverse regional challenges: the IMF's 2025 Article IV consultation on Hungary emphasizes high-quality adjustment measures to mitigate risks from state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and off-balance-sheet liabilities. Conversely, the 2025 Country Report on Italy highlights a "snowball" effect from high interest costs and the need to manage the lagged fiscal impacts of past tax credits while navigating a path toward a positive primary budget balance.

## Key Details

- **Structural Targets:** Implementation of policies aimed at achieving a structural primary surplus (e.g., approximately 1.75% of GDP in some contexts) to rebuild fiscal buffers and stabilize public debt levels.
- **Debt and Financing:** Public debt ratios remain elevated (e.g., ~73.5% of GDP in Hungary). In Italy, high public debt remains a primary vulnerability, where high financing costs and risk premia can constrain growth potential and necessitate credible consolidation strategies.
- **Expenditure Rationalization:** Strategies include rationalizing the public wage bill, streamlining public administration, and containing long-term expenditures related to pensions and healthcare to ensure intergenerational equity.
- **Revenue and Subsidy Reform:** Broadening the tax base and phasing out distortive subsidies (such as retail energy subsidies and windfall taxes) in favor of targeted cash transfers to improve efficiency and market signals.
- **Risk Mitigation:** Enhanced monitoring of State-Owned Enterprises (SOEs) and off-balance-sheet contingent liabilities is critical to preventing hidden fiscal risks from undermining macroeconomic stability.
- **Growth Synergies:** Fiscal sustainability is closely linked to structural reforms that enhance competitiveness, unlock external financing (e.g., EU Funds), and foster investment recovery, thereby supporting the "denominator effect" on debt-to-GDP ratios.
- **Policy Instruments:** Utilization of frameworks like the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) to support economic growth while balancing fiscal constraints.
