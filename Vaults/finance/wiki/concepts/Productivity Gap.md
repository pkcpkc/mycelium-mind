# Productivity Gap

## Summary

The Productivity Gap refers to the persistent shortfall in economic output (GDP) relative to the resources, labor, and capital invested. In the context of the Italian economy, it is characterized by a struggle to translate economic activity into high-value growth due to structural, technological, and educational barriers.

## Key Details

- **Innovation Capacity:** Driven by low R&D expenditure (0.52% of GDP) and a lack of private investment in high-tech sectors.
- **Skills Mismatch:** A significant contributor where the labor force's qualifications do not align with market needs, particularly in construction, healthcare, and ICT.
- **Capital Market Limitations:** Heavy dependence on bank credit rather than capital markets limits funding for innovative startups and high-growth ventures.
- **Research Commercialization:** A university system where research careers are unattractive due to low remuneration and job insecurity, hindering the transition of academic research into commercial applications.
- **Regional Disparities:** Geographic imbalances, specifically the "Mezzogiorno" (South) lagging in infrastructure and GDP per head, exacerbate the national productivity gap.
