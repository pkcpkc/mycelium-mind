# National Human Rights Institution

## Summary
A National Human Rights Institution (NHRI) is an independent body mandated to protect and promote human rights at the national level. Within the context of the 2025 Rule of Law Reports, the establishment or formalization of an NHRI is highlighted as a critical missing component in institutional checks and balances for both Italy and Malta. In Italy, efforts to establish such an institution have stalled, while in Malta, draft legislation remains pending. This absence or delay contributes to broader concerns regarding fundamental rights protection and institutional accountability in both jurisdictions.

## Key Details
- **Establishment Status (Italy):** Efforts to establish a National Human Rights Institution have stalled, marking a gap in the country's framework for protecting fundamental freedoms and ensuring accountability.
- **Establishment Status (Malta):** Draft laws to establish an NHRI are still pending, contributing to limited advancement in institutional checks and balances.
- **Rule of Law Implications:** The lack of a fully operational NHRI in both countries is noted as a structural shortcoming under the institutional checks and balances pillar, alongside concerns about fundamental rights oversight and democratic safeguards.
- **International Standards:** NHRI functions are generally aligned with the Paris Principles to ensure independence, pluralism, and effective monitoring of human rights compliance.
