# Automatic wage indexation

## Summary

Automatic wage indexation is a policy mechanism that automatically adjusts wages in response to changes in inflation, productivity, or other macroeconomic indicators. In the Belgian context, this system has historically contributed to rigid labor cost structures that impact national competitiveness and fiscal sustainability. The IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation identifies it as a critical area requiring structural overhaul to improve labor market flexibility and alleviate mounting pressures on public finances.

## Key Details

- Historically embedded within the Belgian 1996 wage law framework, designed to protect purchasing power but now criticized for reducing labor-cost competitiveness and amplifying cyclical fiscal deficits.
- The IMF strongly advocates overhauling the indexation system to decouple automatic adjustments from rigid inflation metrics, allowing for more dynamic wage setting aligned with productivity and broader economic conditions.
- Reforms are positioned as a cornerstone of Belgium's structural adjustment agenda, working in tandem with measures to limit unemployment benefits, raise the effective retirement age, and shift tax burdens from labor to capital.
- Addressing indexation rigidity is seen as essential to widening the income gap between work and non-work, thereby incentivizing labor force participation and reducing long-term fiscal vulnerabilities.
