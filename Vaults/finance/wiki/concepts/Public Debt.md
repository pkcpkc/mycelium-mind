# Public Debt

## Summary

Public debt represents the accumulated financial obligations and borrowings of a sovereign government. In recent macroeconomic assessments, this metric has become a central focus for fiscal policy and international oversight across multiple European economies. Analysis of the 2025 EU Commission Country Reports highlights that high public debt, coupled with elevated debt-servicing costs and a lack of stable medium-term budgetary frameworks, poses significant risks to economic stability. In France, specifically, the general government deficit reached 5.8% of GDP in 2024 with public debt climbing to 113%, a situation exacerbated by tax revenue shortfalls, pension indexation to high inflation, and local government spending. Across the region, debt sustainability is increasingly tied to structural reforms, productivity growth, and the management of demographic shifts.

## Key Details

- **Debt Trajectories & Drivers:** Austria's public debt stood at 81% of GDP in 2024, elevated by a fiscal deficit of 4.7% following economic contraction. Belgium's debt is projected to surpass 120% of GDP by 2030 under unchanged policies, reflecting persistent structural deficits. France's debt reached 113.1% of GDP in 2024, driven by recovery costs and fiscal slippages, with an expected increase to 118.4% by 2026 due to a persistent general government deficit (5.8% in 2024) and high interest payments on sovereign debt. Hungary's public debt stands at approximately 73.5% of GDP; while financing costs and high risk premia constrain growth, notable fiscal consolidation has occurred in recent years. Italy's public debt remains persistently high at 135.3% of GDP, though fiscal performance improved in 2024 with a headline deficit of 3.4% and a primary surplus of 0.4%, driven by strong revenue collection and improved tax compliance. Slovakia's debt sustainability is closely monitored following a deficit peak of 5.8% of GDP in 2024, necessitating immediate corrective measures to prevent debt trajectory deterioration.
- **Consolidation Targets & Pathways:** The IMF advocates for structured, medium-term adjustment plans. Austria targets reducing the structural deficit below 2% of GDP. Belgium requires a seven-year plan targeting an annual structural primary balance improvement of 0.6 percentage points. France aims to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029 through a frontloaded structural effort, requiring effective spending reviews and a rationalization of tax expenditures. For Hungary, the IMF recommends a structural primary surplus target of approximately 1.75% of GDP by the medium term. Italy's Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) outlines a gradual seven-year adjustment, with staff recommending an additional primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027. Slovakia's government has introduced a four-year consolidation plan targeting a headline deficit reduction to 2.2% of GDP by 2028, with IMF staff endorsing the trajectory as appropriately ambitious and urging a balanced approach that prioritizes expenditure-based measures.
- **Expenditure & Structural Reforms:** Debt sustainability strategies prioritize pension and healthcare reforms, labor market adjustments, and efficiency gains. Austria, Belgium, and France focus on lowering pension replacement rates, capping wage growth, and overhauling indexation systems. Hungary is advised to contain long-term pension and healthcare expenditures, phase out distortive retail energy subsidies, and rationalize the wage bill. Italy faces pressing needs to expand the labor supply (particularly among women and older workers), upgrade skills, modernize public administration, and boost human capital. In Slovakia, consolidation relies on trimming departmental budgets, phasing out temporary energy support, and streamlining public spending to cushion the impact of revenue measures while safeguarding growth-friendly investments.
- **Revenue Mobilization & Taxation:** Recommendations focus on broadening the tax base and rationalizing spending. Austria, Belgium, and France emphasize targeted increases in property, inheritance, and excise taxes, with a strategic push to shift the burden from labor to capital. In France, rationalizing current spending is prioritized given high tax-to-GDP ratios. Hungary is recommended to broaden the tax base and phase out market-distorting measures. Italy is advised to improve tax compliance, rationalize current spending, and phase out unproductive incentives to strengthen fiscal buffers without stifling private sector dynamism. Slovakia is advised to reduce VAT exemptions and implement market-value-based property taxes to improve revenue collection efficiency while minimizing distortions.
- **Macroeconomic & Structural Headwinds:** Persistent inflation, sluggish productivity, and demographic aging compound debt servicing challenges. Austria, Belgium, and France face labor market fragmentation and regulatory bottlenecks. Hungary contends with deepening geoeconomic fragmentation, potential delays in [[EU Funds]] disbursements, and the fiscal impact of upcoming elections. Italy's demographic trends are intensifying, with population aging constraining potential growth and straining pension systems. Slovakia contends with an aging population, labor shortages, and over-reliance on the automotive sector, which necessitates active labor market policies and innovation acceleration to sustain growth. Italy also faces downside risks from escalating global trade tensions, tighter financial conditions, and macro-critical climate-related shocks. Implementation risks remain elevated across the region due to political dynamics, market distortions, and the need to restore competitiveness.
- **Governance & Market Distortions:** Recent assessments highlight that high public debt is often exacerbated by a lack of stable medium-term budgetary frameworks and excessive expansionary biases. In some cases, the use of poorly targeted subsidized loans to households and corporations is identified as a significant distortion of financial market functioning, complicating the path toward sustainable debt management.
