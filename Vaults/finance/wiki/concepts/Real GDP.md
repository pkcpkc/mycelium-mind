# Real GDP

## Summary

Real Gross Domestic Product (Real GDP) is an economic metric used to measure the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders during a specific period, adjusted for price changes (inflation). It serves as a primary indicator of economic growth and standard of living, allowing for the comparison of production levels across different years or between different nations.

## Key Details

- **Inflation Adjustment:** Unlike Nominal GDP, Real GDP removes the effects of price increases, providing a clearer picture of whether an economy is actually producing more goods and services.
- **Economic Growth Indicator:** It is the standard metric for calculating annual economic growth rates (e.g., the projected 0.6% growth for France in 2025).
- **Productivity Correlation:** Real GDP is closely linked to labour productivity; structural weaknesses in skills and high production taxes can hinder the growth of Real GDP.
- **Investment Influence:** Subdued investment levels and high corporate debt can act as headwinds to Real GDP expansion.
- **Contextual Factors:** Growth in Real GDP is influenced by private consumption, monetary policy, energy costs, and the successful implementation of recovery plans like the RRP.
