# Business Dynamism

## Summary

Business dynamism refers to the rate of new firm entry, market exit, and the continuous reallocation of capital and labour across enterprises. In Belgium's economic landscape, it remains subdued due to structural barriers that hinder SME growth and limit the spillover effects of corporate research and development.

## Key Details

- **Regulatory and Sectoral Friction:** High regulatory burdens and restrictive service sectors create significant entry barriers and limit operational flexibility for new and growing firms.
- **Concentrated Innovation Diffusion:** Although national R&D spending reaches 3.3% of GDP, innovation benefits are heavily concentrated among a few large corporations rather than diffusing to SMEs and startups.
- **Competitiveness Constraints:** Elevated energy costs and persistent skilled labour shortages suppress investment and reduce overall competitiveness, particularly in technical and STEM-intensive industries.
- **SME Limitations:** The lack of innovation spillovers, combined with high compliance costs and labour market disincentives, disproportionately stifles the scalability and survival rates of small and medium-sized enterprises.
