# Magisterial Inquiries

## Summary

Magisterial inquiries are a specialized judicial procedure in the Maltese legal system designed to investigate serious criminal offences, particularly those involving high-ranking public officials or complex corruption cases. The mechanism has recently faced significant scrutiny following a legislative reform that increased the threshold for initiating such inquiries, raising concerns among legal experts and civil society about reduced accessibility and potential impediments to accountability in high-level offences.

## Key Details

- **Purpose & Function:** Serves as a critical investigative tool for probing serious crimes that may require judicial oversight beyond standard police or prosecutorial procedures, often addressing systemic corruption or offences involving state actors.
- **Recent Legislative Reform:** A recent amendment increased the threshold required to trigger a magisterial inquiry. This change has drawn strong criticism for potentially creating procedural barriers that hinder thorough investigations into serious crimes.
- **Impact on Accountability:** The raised threshold is viewed as a structural challenge to the [[Anti-Corruption Framework]], with experts warning it may shield high-level offenders from scrutiny and undermine public confidence in the justice system.
- **International Assessment:** Identified in the [[EU Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report Malta]] as an area requiring attention to ensure alignment with European standards on effective judicial investigation, transparency, and institutional checks and balances.
- **Broader Context:** Operates within a justice sector where perceived judicial independence remains high, yet resource shortages and procedural hurdles continue to impact the efficiency and accessibility of legal remedies.
