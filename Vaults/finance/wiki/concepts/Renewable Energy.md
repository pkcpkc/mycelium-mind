# Renewable Energy

## Summary
Renewable energy refers to power generated from naturally replenishing sources such as solar irradiance, wind, hydrological cycles, and geothermal heat. It is a critical enabler of global decarbonization strategies and the transition toward sustainable development. In regional policy assessments, such as the 2025 European Commission report on Malta, renewable energy deployment remains a strategic priority, though current capacity often lags behind fossil fuel dependencies due to infrastructural, financial, and regulatory bottlenecks. In the context of France's 2025 economic landscape, the acceleration of renewable energy deployment is identified as a key recommendation to meet ambitious greenhouse gas reduction targets.

In the Polish context (2025), renewable energy is a central component of the transition toward a green economy, specifically aimed at decarbonizing the district heating sector (currently 69% coal-reliant) and addressing high electricity prices for industrial consumers caused by heavy fossil fuel reliance.

In Romania (2025), renewable energy has reached a significant milestone, accounting for 48% of electricity generation in 2024. However, the sector faces structural bottlenecks in storage and grid capacity, alongside high energy prices averaging 103 EUR/MWh.

## Key Details
- **Generation Sources & Technology:** Predominantly includes solar photovoltaics, wind turbines, hydroelectric systems, and geothermal plants. Solar energy is frequently highlighted as a primary expansion vector in sun-rich regions and island economies.
- **Market & Policy Dynamics:** Adoption rates are often constrained by persistent fossil fuel subsidies, which distort market pricing and delay necessary decarbonization transitions. Legislative reforms targeting energy taxation and grid access are essential to level the playing field.
- **Regional Implementation Status:** 
    - **Malta:** Renewable energy capacity remains minimal relative to total electricity demand. Solar installations constitute a modest share of the national grid, which continues to heavily rely on imported fossil fuels for baseload and peak power.
    - **Romania:** While making strides in generation share (48%), the focus remains on overcoming high costs and grid limitations to ensure affordability.
- **Financing & Infrastructure Development:** Green infrastructure projects, including renewable energy deployment and grid modernization, are increasingly catalyzed by EU funding mechanisms such as the [[Recovery and Resilience Facility]] (RRF) and Cohesion Policy, which provide targeted capital for sustainable transitions.
- **Socioeconomic & Environmental Alignment:** Directly supports the achievement of broader [[Sustainable Development Goals]] (SDGs), particularly clean energy access, climate action, and sustainable industrialization, while mitigating environmental stressors like water scarcity and air pollution.
- **Strategic Targets & Investment:** In France, the transition is characterized by a milestone of 95% low-carbon electricity (largely nuclear-driven), but the sector faces investment uncertainty due to a lack of firm 2030 renewable energy targets. Recommendations include accelerated deployment of solar, wind, and hydrogen, alongside associated storage technologies to support the green transition.
- **Industrial & Technological Integration (Poland):** Poland serves as a major manufacturer of lithium batteries (60% of EU production), providing a critical hardware foundation for the energy transition. Key focus areas include improving grid flexibility through storage and demand-side response to mitigate high industrial energy costs.
- **Subsidy Reform:** There is a specific policy call to phase out subsidies for coal mining and diesel in agriculture to facilitate a more competitive environment for renewable alternatives.
- **Incentive Mechanisms (Romania):** Recommendations for the Romanian market include shifting the fiscal burden from electricity to fossil fuels to incentivize electrification, and implementing "contracts for difference" (CfD) and power purchase agreements (PPAs) to support industrial decarbonization.
- **Decommissioning:** Specific policy targets include the accelerated decommissioning of coal and lignite plants (notably by 2025 in Romania) to clear space for renewable expansion.
