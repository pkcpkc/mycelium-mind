# Public Finances

## Summary

Public finances encompass the financial activities of a government, including revenue collection, expenditure allocation, debt management, and overall fiscal sustainability. In the context of recent European economic assessments, deteriorating public finances are characterized by rising headline deficits and escalating public debt trajectories, often driven by structural pressures such as demographic shifts, rigid social protection systems, and constrained macroeconomic growth.

## Key Details

- **Fiscal Trajectory & Debt:** Headline deficits are projected to reach 5.4% of GDP in 2025, with public debt expected to climb toward 126.4% of GDP by 2035 without targeted policy intervention.
- **Demographic Pressures:** A primary driver of fiscal deterioration is the substantial rise in [[Ageing Costs]], particularly pension liabilities and long-term care expenditures, which are forecast to surge significantly through 2070.
- **Policy & Planning Gaps:** The existing [[Fiscal Framework]] requires strengthening, as national multiannual fiscal planning systems remain underdeveloped, risking deviations from medium-term stability targets and undermining institutional credibility.
- **Intersection with Social Policy:** Fiscal sustainability is tightly coupled with [[Tax and Benefits System]] design, where high labour taxes and non-means-tested benefits create disincentives to work, necessitating reforms that shift the tax burden and limit benefit duration.
- **Macroeconomic Impact:** Deteriorating public finances exacerbate economic deceleration, constrain private investment, and reduce fiscal space to fund green transition, digitalisation, and infrastructure modernization.
