# Asset Declarations

## Summary

Asset declarations are governance instruments intended to promote integrity and prevent conflicts of interest by requiring public officials to disclose their financial assets. Within the context of Belgium's rule of law assessment, these declarations are currently characterized by significant limitations: they lack independent verification and robust transparency mechanisms, with access restricted solely to criminal investigations rather than being available for general public scrutiny.

## Key Details

- **Verification Deficiencies:** Asset declarations currently lack independent verification mechanisms, which undermines the reliability of the disclosures and limits their effectiveness as a standalone integrity tool.
- **Restricted Transparency:** Mechanisms for public access are insufficient; declarations remain accessible only during the course of criminal investigations, hindering ongoing oversight and accountability.
- **Anti-Corruption Context:** The limitations of asset declarations contribute to enforcement gaps within the broader anti-corruption framework, highlighting a need for reform to enhance transparency and deterrence.
- **Reform Indicators:** Identified as a persistent challenge in the 2025 Rule of Law Report, signaling a requirement for legislative or procedural updates to improve verification and accessibility.
