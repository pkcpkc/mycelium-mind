# GDP per Capita

## Summary

GDP per capita is a macroeconomic indicator that measures the average economic output produced per person in a given region or country. In sovereign credit analysis, it serves as a foundational anchor for evaluating a nation's overall wealth and productive capacity, which directly influences its ability to service debt.

## Key Details

- **Core Metric in Economic Assessment**: Under the [[S&P Global Ratings]] Sovereign Rating Methodology (2017), GDP per capita is the primary anchor for the [[Economic Assessment]] pillar, establishing a baseline for creditworthiness based on national income levels.
- **Methodological Adjustments**: The raw metric is systematically adjusted for growth prospects, economic diversity, and data quality to better reflect sustainable economic resilience rather than relying on nominal output alone.
- **Wealth vs. Debt Servicing Capacity**: Higher GDP per capita generally correlates with stronger institutional capacity, broader tax bases, and greater resilience to external shocks, though it is evaluated alongside structural economic factors.
- **Rating Matrix Integration**: It forms a critical component of the [[Institutional and Economic Profile]], which intersects with fiscal and external metrics to determine indicative sovereign credit ratings.
