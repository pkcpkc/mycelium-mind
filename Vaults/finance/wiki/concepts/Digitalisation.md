# Digitalisation

## Summary

Digitalisation refers to the adoption and implementation of digital technologies to modernize institutional processes and improve operational efficiency. In the context of France, it encompasses strategic efforts to reduce case backlogs and streamline administrative workflows within the justice system, as well as broader economic goals to bridge the digital gap in the private sector, particularly among Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs).

## Key Details

- **Judicial Reform:** Central to ongoing initiatives, with a specific focus on digitalising the justice system to address chronic efficiency challenges and reduce case resolution times.
- **Core Infrastructure:** Implementation relies on core digital infrastructure, most notably the **[[Portalis]]** platform, which serves as a primary tool for modernizing court operations and case management.
- **Implementation Challenges:** While progress is recognized, persistent challenges remain regarding implementation speed, technical stability, and the need for sustained budgetary support to ensure successful deployment.
- **Optimization Strategies:** The Ministry of Justice employs targeted assessments and "flash missions" to optimize digital tool integration alongside broader structural measures aimed at improving sentence execution and overall judicial efficiency.
- **SME Adoption Gap:** A significant economic challenge exists in the private sector, where only 68.5% of SMEs have reached a basic level of digitalisation, lagging behind the European Union average.
- **Administrative Barriers:** Despite reforms like the ASAP law, a high percentage of businesses (84%) still find administrative procedures complex, highlighting a need for further digital simplification.
