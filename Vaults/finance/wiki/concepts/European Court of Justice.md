# European Court of Justice

## Summary

The European Court of Justice (ECJ) serves as the highest judicial authority in the European Union, tasked with ensuring that EU law is interpreted and applied consistently across all member states. It adjudicates disputes between EU institutions, national governments, businesses, and individuals, and provides binding preliminary rulings on questions of EU law referred by domestic courts. In recent years, the ECJ has been a central mechanism for enforcing EU legal standards and institutional compliance, notably issuing landmark rulings that directly impact member state policies and constitutional frameworks.

## Key Details

- Functions as the ultimate arbiter of EU law, guaranteeing uniform legal interpretation and preventing divergent national applications of treaties and regulations.
- Holds jurisdiction over infringement proceedings, preliminary rulings, and appeals concerning the legality of EU acts and member state compliance.
- In April 2025, ruled against Malta's investor citizenship scheme, declaring it incompatible with EU law and marking a significant legal and political setback for the policy.
- Works in tandem with other supranational judicial bodies, such as the European Court of Human Rights, to reinforce democratic safeguards, institutional accountability, and the rule of law.
- Regularly cited in EU Commission Rule of Law Reports as a critical benchmark for assessing judicial independence, legal certainty, and the enforcement of EU fundamental rights.
