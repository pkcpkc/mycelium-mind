# Fiscal Policy

## Summary

[[Fiscal Policy]] refers to the government's strategic use of taxation and public spending to influence macroeconomic performance, ensure debt sustainability, and support structural reforms. In the context of recent IMF assessments, fiscal policy is characterized by sustained consolidation efforts to address high public debt and improve primary balances. Italy's approach focuses on rationalizing tax expenditures, leveraging investments like the NRRP to support growth, and implementing a seven-year adjustment plan. Malta's 2025 consultation emphasizes accelerating consolidation under the EU’s new fiscal framework, phasing out fixed energy subsidies to redirect funds toward green investment and health, and aligning long-term planning with national visions like [[Malta Vision 2050]]. Additionally, the 2025 EU Commission report on Hungary highlights fiscal policy challenges including high inflation, elevated debt-servicing costs, and a lack of a stable medium-term budgetary framework, which often results in expansionary bias and reduced transparency. Both cases highlight the critical role of tax compliance, administrative efficiency, and structural adjustments in navigating economic cycles and external shocks.

## Key Details

- **Fiscal Performance:** In 2024, Italy's fiscal metrics improved significantly with the headline deficit contracting to 3.4% of GDP and a primary surplus of 0.4% achieved, bolstered by strong revenue collection and better [[Tax Compliance]].
- **Debt Reduction Targets:** To place public debt, which stood at 135.3% of GDP in Italy, on a firmly downward trajectory, IMF staff recommend an additional primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027.
- **Consolidation Measures:** Adjustments should focus on rationalizing [[Tax Expenditures]], improving administrative efficiency, phasing out unproductive incentives, and ensuring all new spending is fully compensated.
- **Long-Term Planning:** The [[Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan]] (MTFSP) outlines a seven-year adjustment path to mitigate risks from adverse interest-growth differentials and rising demographic spending pressures. Malta is concurrently developing a long-term fiscal framework aligned with its [[Malta Vision 2050]].
- **Hungary Fiscal Context:** Hungary faces a high public debt of 73.5% of GDP (2024) with elevated servicing costs. The report criticizes a lack of stable medium-term budgetary framework and the use of poorly targeted subsidized loans to households and corporations, which distorts financial market functioning.
- **Growth-Supporting Spending:** Government expenditure, particularly infrastructure investment under the [[National Recovery and Resilience Plan]] (NRRP), contributed to 0.7% real GDP growth in 2024 by supporting net exports and economic activity, while redirected funds in Malta target innovation and health services.
- **Tax & Regulatory Alignment:** Reforming corporate taxation to comply with international standards, such as the EU’s [[Pillar II]] directive, is essential to prevent revenue leakage. This requires clear roadmaps for [[Corporate Income Tax]] adjustments and robust [[Anti-Money Laundering]] frameworks to maintain investor confidence.
- **Subsidy Rationalization:** Phasing out fixed [[Energy]] subsidies is recommended to redirect fiscal resources toward productive green investment, digitalization, and public services without exacerbating budget deficits.
- **Risks and Vulnerabilities:** Inefficient implementation of spending programs, delays in structural reforms, external shocks, persistent wage pressures, and potential cyber threats could undermine consolidation efforts and productivity gains.
