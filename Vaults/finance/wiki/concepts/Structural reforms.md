# Structural reforms

## Summary

Structural reforms are systemic policy interventions designed to improve economic efficiency, foster long-term growth, and stabilize public finances by addressing underlying structural imbalances. In contemporary macroeconomic management, they are essential for enhancing labor-cost competitiveness, rationalizing fiscal burdens, and adapting to demographic and environmental challenges. Recent macroeconomic assessments, particularly the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation for France, emphasize that sustained structural reforms are critical for facilitating fiscal consolidation, boosting productivity, and navigating geoeconomic fragmentation while maintaining institutional credibility. Parallel assessments for Hungary underscore their necessity in closing persistent productivity gaps, enhancing international competitiveness, and unlocking critical external financing mechanisms such as EU Funds through comprehensive governance and rule-of-law improvements. Similarly, Italy's 2025 IMF consultation highlights that pressing structural challenges require accelerated reform momentum to offset demographic headwinds, address persistently high public debt (135.3% of GDP), and sustain moderate economic expansion through targeted productivity-boosting measures, robust tax compliance, and full implementation of recovery frameworks. 

Emerging analyses, such as the IMF's 2025 report on Poland, further illustrate that structural reforms are pivotal for sustaining long-term convergence in economies facing demographic pressures and external funding dependencies. In these contexts, reforms must prioritize the rapid integration of refugees and foreign workers into labor markets, modernize vocational training ecosystems, expand childcare infrastructure, and secure credible climate transition pathways to maintain competitiveness amid shifting global supply chains and monetary tightening cycles. 

Contemporary assessments, including the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation for Romania, highlight how structural reforms are increasingly deployed to manage widening twin deficits and restore macroeconomic stability. In Romania, costly pension reforms and rising public wages initially exacerbated fiscal and current account imbalances (reaching 8.7% and 8.2% of GDP, respectively). In response, authorities implemented a comprehensive reform package targeting deficit reduction below 6% of GDP by 2026 and below 3% by 2030. This case underscores the broader global trend where structural reforms now explicitly integrate fiscal consolidation measures, public investment management improvements, State-Owned Enterprise (SOE) governance enhancements, and accelerated absorption of EU/NGEU funds to unlock growth potential while navigating persistent inflationary pressures and energy transition demands.

Parallel assessments, such as the IMF's 2025 Article IV Consultation for Slovakia, illustrate how structural reforms are deployed to navigate modest growth recovery amid persistent inflationary pressures and significant medium-term fiscal challenges. The Slovak case underscores the necessity of expenditure-led consolidation to reverse widening deficits, alongside targeted reforms to the constitutional debt brake and the Council for Budgetary Responsibility. It further highlights how structural adjustments must concurrently address labor shortages and demographic headwinds through Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs), boost female and elderly labor participation, and streamline migrant integration. Moreover, the report emphasizes that governance reforms—specifically combating corruption and enhancing judicial independence—are directly tied to unlocking suspended EU funds, while expeditious implementation of the EU’s ETS II for transport and buildings is critical for meeting long-term climate targets without stifling economic resilience.

## Key Details

- **Labor Market & Wage Setting:** Overhaul automatic wage indexation systems and historical wage-setting frameworks to reduce fiscal pressures and enhance flexibility. Key interventions include widening the income gap between employment and non-employment, limiting unemployment benefits, promoting lifelong learning, increasing female labor force participation, and facilitating migrant integration to address skill mismatches and reduce labor market fragmentation. In contexts like Italy, expanding the labor supply specifically among women and older workers is critical, alongside upgrading skills to address persistent youth unemployment and low female participation rates relative to EU averages. Parallel assessments for Poland highlight the necessity of streamlining vocational training systems and removing barriers to integrate refugees and foreign workers, ensuring that strong nominal wage growth does not trigger persistent inflationary spirals in tight labor markets. Recent IMF analyses for Romania further emphasize boosting female labor force participation and managing nominal wage growth pressures to support disinflationary consolidation without stifling domestic demand. In emerging consolidating economies like Slovakia, structural adjustments must heavily leverage Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs) to mitigate acute labor shortages and demographic aging, while simultaneously improving integration pathways for migrant workers and encouraging higher participation rates among women and older cohorts to sustain productivity growth.
- **Fiscal & Revenue Restructuring:** Implement a sustained, frontloaded structural adjustment plan to improve the structural primary balance, prioritizing the rationalization of current spending across central, local, and social security levels. Given already elevated tax-to-GDP ratios, further broad-based tax increases should be avoided while gradually shifting the burden toward capital gains taxation and reducing broad tax expenditures. In high-debt environments like Italy, sustained consolidation is essential to place debt trajectories on a downward path (e.g., targeting an additional 3% primary surplus by 2027), requiring the rationalization of tax expenditures, improved administrative efficiency, and careful management of interest-growth differentials through multi-year structural plans like the Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan. Recent IMF recommendations for Poland emphasize frontloading consolidation to stabilize debt around 60–65% of GDP, establishing independent fiscal councils for transparency, and avoiding permanent expansions in public wages or defense spending that undermine medium-term fiscal buffers. In emerging consolidating economies like Romania, comprehensive 2025–26 reform packages (including VAT increases, higher excise and dividend taxes, and freezes on public sector wages and pensions) demonstrate how targeted revenue mobilization and expenditure management are synchronized to bring fiscal deficits below 6% by 2026, with further medium-term adjustments targeting <3% by 2030 to stabilize sovereign debt and restore market confidence. Complementing these approaches, Slovakia's reform roadmap underscores the efficacy of expenditure-led consolidation strategies, including trimming departmental budgets, phasing out temporary energy support, and reforming VAT exemptions. Additionally, safeguarding the national fiscal framework requires proactive reforms to constitutional debt brakes and strengthening independent budgetary oversight bodies to prevent disruptive consolidation cycles and ensure long-term debt sustainability.
- **Pension & Demographic Adaptation:** Adjust pension frameworks to raise the effective retirement age (e.g., the 2023 reform raising it to 64), directly counteracting economic and fiscal pressures from aging population dynamics. Further harmonization of pension systems across sectors and the promotion of healthy aging policies are recommended to sustain long-term viability. Demographic pressures are intensifying globally, with sharp rises in old-age dependency ratios constraining potential growth and straining public finances. For converging economies like Poland, aging populations directly threaten pension adequacy and constrain labor supply, necessitating proactive reforms that align retirement systems with evolving workforce realities while expanding childcare infrastructure to support dual-income households. The tension between pension adequacy and fiscal sustainability is a recurring theme, as seen in Romania's recent reforms where costly pension expansions initially widened deficits, prompting subsequent consolidation measures to rebalance long-term viability against short-term fiscal constraints. In economies facing acute demographic headwinds and sector-specific labor shortages (e.g., Slovakia's automotive-dependent structure), structural reforms must prioritize policy packages that extend working lives, modernize pension indexing mechanisms, and invest in intergenerational mobility to prevent structural unemployment alongside aging.
- **Product Market & Business Environment:** Streamline insolvency regimes, reduce regulatory barriers, and foster competition to improve firm dynamics and total factor productivity. Expand access to risk capital for high-growth firms, deepen single market integration, and coordinate industrial policies to enhance corporate competitiveness and improve domestic capital allocation. Reviving private sector dynamism through better risk capital access and deepening EU single market integration are highlighted as vital for overcoming productivity lags, while industrial policy must be carefully targeted and coordinated at the EU level to avoid market distortions.
- **Financial Sector Stability:** Maintain robust macroprudential buffers and accelerate legislative implementation of financial sector assessment recommendations. Emphasis is placed on continued monitoring of non-bank financial institutions, cyber risks, and climate-related vulnerabilities to ensure resilience against moderate systemic risks and emerging threats. In specific regional contexts, financial sectors may demonstrate robust profitability and low non-performing loan ratios, yet vigilance remains crucial regarding vulnerabilities in less significant institutions, sovereign-bank nexus risks, and potential loan quality deterioration amid trade uncertainties. For economies with underdeveloped capital markets, structural reforms must also prioritize deepening domestic financing channels, resolving legacy debt overhangs (e.g., foreign-currency mortgage disputes), and avoiding regulatory tightening that could stifle private credit recovery. Recent IMF assessments highlight the need for calibrated macroprudential policies to monitor rising consumer credit and unhedged foreign exchange corporate loans, while advocating for gradual increases in exchange rate flexibility to enhance external resilience and address competitiveness gaps without triggering financial instability. In markets experiencing rapid credit expansion or sectoral concentration, structural reforms must also address real estate market vulnerabilities, including residential and commercial property price dynamics, upcoming mortgage repricing risks, and elevated vacancy rates. This involves exploring positive neutral countercyclical capital buffers (pnCCyB), closing borrower-based measure loopholes, and strengthening supervision of less significant institutions to prevent localized shocks from propagating through the broader financial system.
- **Innovation & Green Transition:** Strengthen federal-regional coordination, establish coherent national environmental strategies, and implement carbon pricing mechanisms alongside the phased removal of fossil fuel subsidies. Advance energy diversification to mitigate external vulnerabilities while concurrently leveraging strategic growth sectors through enhanced innovation financing, streamlined R&D tax expenditures, and targeted development in Artificial Intelligence. Accelerating the transition to renewable energy, investing in resilient infrastructure, and leveraging recovery funds for public administration streamlining and human capital development are recognized as macro-critical priorities to enhance energy security and mitigate climate risks. Recent policy assessments stress that credible climate transitions are essential for long-term competitiveness, requiring extended carbon pricing frameworks (such as the EU Emissions Trading System 2 or ETS2), accelerated permitting for green infrastructure projects, and socially managed phase-outs of legacy energy sectors to prevent regional economic shocks. Specifically, expeditious implementation of ETS II for the transport and buildings sectors, coupled with gradual increases in environmental levies, provides a structured pathway to meet 2030 and 2050 climate targets while minimizing economic disruption and fostering green technology adoption.
- **Governance & Institutional Frameworks:** Implement comprehensive governance and rule-of-law reforms to strengthen institutional credibility, ensure transparent regulatory environments, and align with international funding conditionality. In multiple recent assessments, these institutional improvements are explicitly linked to unlocking suspended external financing (e.g., EU Funds) and restoring long-term investor and market confidence. Timely implementation of national recovery plans is vital to modernize infrastructure, streamline public administration, and ensure that structural adjustment measures are underpinned by credible institutional execution. This includes establishing independent fiscal oversight bodies to enhance budget transparency, enforce medium-term structural plans, and anchor market expectations during consolidation phases. Contemporary reform agendas increasingly prioritize improving public investment management systems and enhancing State-Owned Enterprise (SOE) governance to reduce fiscal risks, improve capital allocation efficiency, and accelerate the absorption of EU/NGEU funds, thereby transforming policy commitments into tangible infrastructure and productivity gains. Furthermore, structural reforms must address deep-seated governance risks by enhancing judicial independence, strengthening anti-corruption frameworks, and improving public procurement efficiency. Strengthening these institutional pillars is consistently shown to be a prerequisite for maintaining investor confidence, ensuring the uninterrupted disbursement of critical external grants, and fostering a predictable regulatory environment conducive to long-term private investment.
