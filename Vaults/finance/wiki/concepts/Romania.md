# Romania

## Summary

Romania is a Southeastern European country and EU member state whose economy is currently characterized by a period of macroeconomic adjustment. Following sluggish growth in 2024, the nation is implementing comprehensive fiscal reforms and structural modernization to address widening twin deficits, manage inflationary pressures, and stabilize sovereign debt amid a challenging global environment.

## Key Details

- **Economic Growth Trajectory:** Real GDP grew by 0.9% in 2024, with projections for gradual recovery at 1.0% (2025) and 1.4% (2026), driven by accelerated NGEU-funded investments and moderated private consumption.
- **Twin Deficits & Fiscal Consolidation:** The fiscal deficit reached 8.7% of GDP and the current account deficit widened to 8.2% in 2024, largely due to pension reforms and rising public wages. Authorities are targeting a deficit below 6% by 2026 and under 3% by 2030.
- **Fiscal Reform Package (2025–26):** Includes VAT rate increases, higher excise and dividend taxes, and a freeze on public sector wages and pensions to restore market confidence.
- **Inflation & Monetary Policy:** Headline inflation surged to 9.9% in September 2025 following energy price cap removals. The National Bank of Romania maintains a benchmark rate of 6.5%, with rate cuts contingent on sustained moderation in inflation and wage growth.
- **Financial Sector Fundamentals:** Banks maintain strong capital adequacy and liquidity, though vulnerabilities include a growing sovereign-bank nexus, unhedged FX corporate loans, and rapid consumer credit expansion.
- **Structural & Labor Reforms:** Priority areas include accelerating stalled EU funds, improving public investment management, enhancing State-Owned Enterprise (SOE) governance, boosting female labor force participation, and advancing the low-carbon transition via ETS2.
