# Energy Transition

## Summary

The Energy Transition refers to the structural shift in energy production and consumption, moving away from carbon-intensive fossil fuels toward low-carbon and renewable energy sources. It involves systemic changes in infrastructure, technology, policy, and economic models to achieve climate goals and ensure energy security.

## Key Details

- **Decarbonisation Drivers:** A critical component of the transition involves phasing out fossil fuel subsidies and reducing dependency on high-carbon energy sources (e.g., Russian fossil fuels).
- **Renewable Integration:** While production of renewable sources (such as solar energy) can surge rapidly, the transition requires significant investment in grid flexibility and storage infrastructure to manage intermittent supply.
- **Industrial Leadership:** Certain regions may lead in specific segments of the transition, such as electric vehicle (EV) battery manufacturing.
- **Economic Barriers:** High energy prices for businesses and the need for a circular economy are key hurdles that must be addressed to ensure a just transition.
- **Policy Frameworks:** Progress is often tied to specific initiatives like REPowerEU and the implementation of Recovery and Resilience Plans (RRP).
