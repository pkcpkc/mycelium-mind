# Sovereign Rating

## Summary

The Sovereign Rating represents the analytical framework and methodology established by S&P Global Ratings to evaluate the creditworthiness of sovereign governments and monetary authorities. First comprehensively updated in December 2017, the framework replaced earlier criteria by introducing a structured five-pillar evaluation model and refining key metrics such as debt measurement and external liquidity ratios. It provides a systematic approach to determining credit ratings across local and foreign currencies, incorporating adjustments for monetary sovereignty, institutional strength, and macroeconomic stability.

## Key Details

- **Five-Pillar Evaluation Framework**: Core assessments are divided into Institutional, Economic, External, Fiscal, and Monetary pillars, each scored from 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest).
- **Institutional Assessment**: Evaluates policymaking effectiveness, stability, transparency, debt payment culture, and security risks.
- **Economic Assessment**: Primarily anchored by GDP per capita, adjusted for growth prospects, economic diversity, and data quality.
- **External Assessment**: Analyzes currency status, liquidity relative to financing needs, and external indebtedness using narrow net external debt and Current Account Payments (CAP).
- **Fiscal Assessment**: Split into fiscal performance/flexibility (tracking changes in net debt/GDP and revenue/expenditure flexibility) and Debt Burden (considering debt levels, interest costs, funding structure, and contingent liabilities).
- **Monetary Assessment**: Weighs exchange rate regimes (40% weight) against monetary policy credibility, price stability, and financial market depth (60% weight).
- **Rating Matrix & Aggregation**: Pillar scores combine into an Institutional and Economic Profile and a Flexibility and Performance Profile, which intersect to produce an indicative rating.
- **Currency Rating Mechanics**: Local-currency ratings are capped at one notch above foreign-currency ratings (previously two), permitted only if the sovereign has independent monetary policy, deep local capital markets, and sufficient fiscal/institutional flexibility.
- **Adjustments & Overrides**: Supplemental factors can adjust the indicative rating by up to one notch for extreme risks or exceptionally strong financial positions. Transition dynamics and temporary shifts are factored into final ratings.
- **Monetary Union Provisions**: Special rules apply to union members, adjusting external/monetary scores for policy synchronization and liquidity constraints, with monetary authorities rated via weighted averages of members plus flexibility uplifts.
- **Contingent Liability Integration**: Banking system risks and other contingent liabilities are evaluated using the BICRA framework and integrated into the fiscal debt burden assessment.
