# Carbon Tax

## Summary

A carbon tax is a fiscal policy designed to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by putting a price on carbon content. It serves as a key mechanism for internalizing the environmental costs of production and consumption, shifting the economic burden from society to the polluters. In the context of regional reports like Slovakia's, it is identified as a critical component of rebalancing the tax mix toward environmental sustainability.

## Key Details

- **Policy Objective:** To incentivize industries and consumers to transition toward low-carbon technologies and energy sources.
- **Fiscal Rebalancing:** Recommended as a way to shift tax burdens away from labor (which currently accounts for a disproportionately high share of revenue) and toward capital and environmental outputs.
- **Industrial Impact:** Particularly relevant for industrial sectors (which can constitute a significant portion of GDP) to mitigate the environmental impact of high emissions.
- **Green Transition:** Acts as a fundamental driver for the "Green Transition" by making fossil-fuel-dependent activities more expensive relative to renewable alternatives.
- **Economic Reform:** Viewed as a tool to address fiscal sustainability by diversifying revenue streams and encouraging decarbonization.
