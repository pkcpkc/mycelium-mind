# EU Single Market

## Summary

The EU Single Market is a cornerstone of European economic integration designed to ensure the free movement of goods, services, capital, and people. Recent policy assessments emphasize deepening the Single Market as a critical structural lever to boost competitiveness, unlock economies of scale, and enhance capital market financing for businesses. Key focus areas include advancing the Savings and Investment Union and harmonizing corporate regulations to reduce fragmentation and support productivity growth.

## Key Details

- **Savings and Investment Union:** A primary initiative to deepen the Single Market, aimed at improving cross-border capital allocation, risk-sharing, and investment flows across member states.
- **Regulatory Harmonization:** Proposals for a harmonized "28th corporate regime" seek to standardize rules, reduce compliance burdens, and facilitate seamless cross-border operations for companies.
- **Capital Market Access:** The Single Market serves as a vital mechanism to enhance access to financing for startups and SMEs, diversifying funding sources beyond traditional bank lending.
- **Economies of Scale:** By removing barriers and aligning standards, the Single Market enables firms to expand their operational scale, contributing to efficiency gains and economic resilience.
- **Structural Reform Context:** Integration measures are identified as essential complements to fiscal consolidation and labor market reforms, particularly in addressing demographic headwinds and global trade uncertainties.
