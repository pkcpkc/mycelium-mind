# Tax and Benefits System

## Summary

Belgium's tax and benefits framework is characterized by high labour taxes and non-means-tested social transfers that create strong disincentives to work, contributing to an unemployment trap and prolonged economic inactivity. To address deteriorating public finances and rising ageing costs, the federal government is implementing structural reforms aimed at shifting the tax burden away from labour, tightening unemployment benefit eligibility, and introducing a bonus-malus pension mechanism to ensure long-term fiscal sustainability.

## Key Details

- **Work Disincentives & Unemployment Trap:** High effective labour taxes and generous non-means-tested benefits discourage active labour market participation, particularly among low-skilled workers.
- **Unemployment Benefit Reform:** Federal plans stipulate limiting unemployment benefits to a maximum of two years to accelerate reintegration into the labour market.
- **Tax Burden Restructuring:** Strategic shift in revenue collection away from labour taxation towards alternative sources to improve competitiveness and employment incentives.
- **Pension System Restructuring:** Introduction of a bonus-malus mechanism to align retirement incentives with fiscal sustainability goals, directly addressing forecasted surges in ageing costs through 2070.
- **Integration with Fiscal Framework:** Reforms are critical to preventing deviations from medium-term fiscal trajectories and supporting the broader goal of stabilizing public debt projections.
