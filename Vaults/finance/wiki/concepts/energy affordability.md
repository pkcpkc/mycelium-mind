# Energy Affordability

## Summary

Energy affordability encompasses the cost burden of energy resources on households and businesses, directly influencing economic competitiveness, social equity, and the feasibility of sustainable transitions. In the context of Austria's macroeconomic landscape, high energy prices have emerged as a significant structural challenge, contributing to industrial weakness and eroded competitiveness. While the nation has made progress in renewable energy integration, issues regarding supply diversification, grid infrastructure, and subsidy structures continue to impact the overall affordability landscape.

## Key Details

- **Industrial Cost Pressures:** High industrial energy prices are a primary driver of Austria's industrial weakness and have eroded the country's competitiveness since 2019, contributing to a broader economic recession.
- **Supply Security and Diversification:** The end of Russian gas imports has necessitated efforts to diversify energy supplies to stabilize costs and ensure reliable access for consumers and industries.
- **Infrastructure Bottlenecks:** Delays in grid expansion hinder the efficient integration of renewable energy sources, potentially limiting the benefits of Austria's 87% renewable electricity mix on price stability.
- **Subsidy Reform:** The report highlights the need to phase out environmentally harmful fossil fuel subsidies, which can distort market prices and create inefficiencies in the energy sector.
- **Transition Dynamics:** Accelerating permitting for net-zero industries and modernizing the energy framework are essential to balance decarbonisation goals with the imperative of maintaining affordable energy access.
