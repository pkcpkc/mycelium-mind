# Fiscal consolidation

## Summary

Fiscal consolidation refers to structured government policies aimed at reducing budget deficits and stabilizing public debt, typically implemented through targeted spending reforms, revenue base broadening, and medium-term structural plans. In recent macroeconomic assessments, it emphasizes balancing near-term flexibility to allow automatic stabilizers to function during economic downturns with a disciplined medium-term framework designed to achieve sustainable fiscal targets. Internationally, multilateral frameworks (e.g., IMF Article IV consultations) frequently recommend multi-year consolidation pathways to address deteriorating debt trajectories and structural imbalances.

Recent analyses, such as the **2025 Country Report for [[Romania]]** and the **2025 Country Report - France**, highlight fiscal consolidation as a critical tool for nations undergoing rapid economic convergence or facing substantial deficit slippage. In these contexts, consolidation is not merely about austerity but serves as a vehicle for structural transformation—moving from models based on low labor costs toward innovation-driven growth. It requires a resolute path to reduce government and current account deficits (twin deficits) while simultaneously addressing high inflation and structural vulnerabilities like R&D underinvestment, regulatory burdens, and tax revenue shortfalls.

## Key Details

- **Deficit Reduction & Debt Trajectory Management:** A primary objective is to reduce the structural fiscal deficit (e.g., below 2-3% of GDP) while managing long-term debt trajectories. Recent assessments emphasize frontloaded structural efforts and multi-year Medium-Term Fiscal Plans to reverse trajectories exceeding 100-120% of GDP. Country-specific targets, such as Poland's medium-term goal to lower the deficit from 5.9% to 2.9% of GDP by 2028, Romania's phased reduction to below 6% by 2026 and under 3% by 2030, and Slovakia's targeted reduction from 5.8% to 2.2% of GDP by 2028, illustrate how consolidation pathways are tailored to national debt ceilings and growth capacities.
- **Phased Implementation Strategy:** Advises near-term fiscal flexibility to support automatic stabilizers during downside scenarios, paired with bold medium-term consolidation measures to ensure long-term sustainability. In the case of France, this involves a recommended multi-year fiscal adjustment (extended to 7 years) focused on spending reviews and rationalizing tax expenditures to counteract "substantial deficit slippage."
- **Institutional Transparency & Governance:** Successful consolidation increasingly requires robust institutional frameworks to build credibility and mitigate political risks. This includes establishing independent fiscal councils, improving budget transparency, accelerating stalled EU fund disbursements, enhancing public investment management, and strengthening governance frameworks for state-owned enterprises (SOEs) to prevent fiscal drains. Recent frameworks also emphasize constitutional and legislative safeguards, such as reforming national debt brakes ahead of fiscal thresholds.
- **Structural Spending & Benefit Reforms:** Prioritizes gradually lowering high pension replacement rates, rationalizing social benefits, capping or reforming public sector wage increases (including automatic wage indexation systems), enhancing healthcare system efficiency, and eliminating environmentally harmful subsidies. Recent measures, such as Romania's freeze on public sector wages and pensions, demonstrate how temporary fiscal brakes are used alongside permanent structural reforms to offset demographic pressures.
- **Revenue Mobilization & Tax Restructuring:** Focuses on broadening the tax base through strategic increases in property, inheritance, gift, and excise taxes, alongside a gradual shift in the fiscal burden from labor to capital. In France, consolidation strategies specifically recommend aligning production taxes with EU averages and rationalizing tax expenditures to address revenue shortfalls.
- **Labor Market & Productivity Alignment:** Consolidation efforts are increasingly necessitated by demographic aging pressures and skills mismatches, requiring interventions to widen the income gap between work and non-work, limit unemployment benefits, promote lifelong learning, and improve product market regulation. Expanding high-quality early childhood education and care (ECEC) is identified as a critical lever to boost maternal labour force participation.
- **Preservation of Strategic Investment:** Emphasizes that consolidation must not indiscriminately cut public spending; priority is given to preserving and enhancing investments in infrastructure, education, green transition initiatives, and AI-driven innovation to support long-term growth.
- **Macro-Policy Synchronization & Financial Stability:** Fiscal consolidation is increasingly analyzed in tandem with monetary and financial sector policies. For example, credible fiscal adjustment can create necessary fiscal space for monetary easing when inflation aligns with targets. In practice, central banks like the [[National Bank of Romania]] have adopted cautious stances until inflation and wage growth show sustained moderation.
- **Implementation & Political Risks:** Consolidation pathways face elevated risks due to domestic political dynamics, potential revenue shortfalls, public resistance to austerity measures, and external shocks. Successful execution requires coordinated industrial policies, clear communication of medium-term targets, and adaptive risk management for geopolitical uncertainties.
- **Ageing Costs & Social Fairness Pressures:** Demographic shifts and rising healthcare and pension expenditures frequently push structural deficits above conventional thresholds. Consolidation strategies must therefore integrate targeted tax reforms to shift burdens away from labour, strengthen social safety nets for vulnerable groups, and address rising housing affordability pressures.
- **Innovation, Regulation & SME Financing:** High regulatory burdens and complex permitting procedures can stifle business dynamism. Modernizing professional services regulations, streamlining administrative processes, and expanding access to risk financing and venture capital for SMEs are essential components of fiscal consolidation to unlock innovation.
- **Green Transition & Industrial Competitiveness:** Fiscal adjustment must carefully balance environmental objectives with industrial competitiveness. While phasing out environmentally harmful fossil fuel subsidies is a priority, consolidation frameworks must mitigate risks associated with high industrial energy prices and grid expansion delays.
