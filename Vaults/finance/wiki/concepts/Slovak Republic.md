# Slovak Republic

## Summary

The Slovak Republic is a landlocked nation in Central Europe and a member of the European Union and the Euro Area. The economy is currently undergoing a transition characterized by modest growth recovery, inflation moderation, and a pressing need for fiscal consolidation. Key structural vulnerabilities include an aging population, labor market constraints, over-concentration in the automotive sector, and governance risks that threaten institutional quality and EU fund absorption. The nation is implementing reforms to stabilize public finances, strengthen financial resilience, and foster long-term productivity growth.

## Key Details

### Economic Performance
- **Growth Trajectory:** GDP growth accelerated to 2.0% in 2024, driven by robust private consumption and real wage growth. Near-term projections indicate a deceleration to 1.8% in 2025 due to fiscal consolidation, with a rebound to 2.1% in 2026 supported by external demand and EU investments.
- **Inflation:** Peaked above 15% in early 2023; currently moderating with a gradual return to the 2% target projected for early 2027. Recent upticks attributed to indirect tax increases and global food prices.
- **Fiscal Position:** The headline deficit reached 5.8% of GDP in 2024. A four-year consolidation plan aims to reduce the deficit to 2.2% of GDP by 2028. The IMF recommends prioritizing expenditure-based measures, including VAT exemption reforms, property tax implementation, budget trimming, and phasing out temporary energy support. Reforming the constitutional debt brake before 2026 is critical for sustainability.

### Financial Sector
- **Resilience:** Banking sector remains well-capitalized and liquid, with stress tests confirming resilience to severe shocks.
- **Vulnerabilities:** Risks persist in the real estate market, including residential mortgage repricing and elevated commercial office vacancy rates.
- **Policy Recommendations:** Maintain current macroprudential stance, explore a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer (pnCCyB), and enhance supervision of less significant institutions.

### Structural Challenges & Reforms
- **Labor Market:** Faces headwinds from demographic aging and labor shortages. Recommendations include expanding Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs), boosting participation among women and the elderly, streamlining migrant integration, and reforming pension systems.
- **Diversification:** Economy is over-reliant on the automotive sector; acceleration of innovation and deepening integration with the European single market are urged to foster cross-border capital flows and diversification.
- **Governance:** Weak institutional quality poses risks to EU fund disbursement. Urgent reforms needed to combat corruption, enhance judicial independence, and improve public administration efficiency.
- **Environment:** Commitment to implementing the EU's ETS II for transport and buildings, raising environmental levies, and meeting 2030/2050 climate targets.
