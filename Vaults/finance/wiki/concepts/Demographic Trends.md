# Demographic Trends

## Summary
Demographic trends refer to significant, long-term shifts in population composition, most notably population aging and rising dependency ratios. These trends act as critical macroeconomic drivers that directly influence labor supply, potential economic growth, public spending trajectories (particularly for pensions and healthcare), and long-term fiscal sustainability. In advanced economies, intensifying demographic shifts often constrain growth and strain public finances, necessitating targeted structural reforms to mitigate adverse economic impacts.

## Key Details
- **Population Aging & Dependency Ratios:** Rising old-age dependency ratios reduce the proportion of working-age individuals, which constrains potential output and places increasing strain on pension and healthcare systems.
- **Labor Market Dynamics:** Shrinking or aging workforces highlight the need to expand labor supply, particularly by increasing participation rates among underutilized groups such as women and older workers.
- **Fiscal & Debt Sustainability:** Demographic spending pressures contribute to structural fiscal imbalances and complicate public debt management, often requiring sustained primary surpluses or targeted spending rationalization to maintain fiscal stability.
- **Growth & Productivity Link:** Adverse demographic shifts can dampen productivity growth, innovation, and human capital development unless actively offset by skill upgrades, technology adoption, or targeted structural reforms.
- **Policy Implications:** Governments typically respond with structural reforms—including retirement age adjustments, education and training investments, and labor market flexibility measures—to sustain economic dynamism despite unfavorable demographic headwinds.
