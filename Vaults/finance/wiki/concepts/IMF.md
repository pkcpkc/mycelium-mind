# IMF

## Summary

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) is an international organization that conducts Article IV consultations to evaluate member states' economic policies and provide targeted recommendations for fiscal, monetary, and structural stability. Recent assessments, including the 2025 reviews of Hungary, Italy, and Romania, focus on addressing economic stagnation, persistent inflation, widening twin deficits, and structural inefficiencies. The IMF provides tailored policy guidance to restore macroeconomic balance, foster sustainable growth, improve governance frameworks, and unlock international funding. For instance, the 2025 Romania consultation highlighted the need for comprehensive fiscal consolidation (targeting deficit reductions below 6% by 2026 and 3% by 2030), cautious monetary stances to anchor inflation, financial sector vigilance regarding sovereign-bank nexuses, and accelerated structural reforms such as EU/NGEU fund absorption, state-owned enterprise (SOE) governance improvements, and alignment with mechanisms like ETS2. The IMF increasingly emphasizes climate resilience, demographic challenges, productivity-driven reforms, and data-dependent policy coordination to navigate global uncertainties and trade uncertainties.

## Key Details

- **Article IV Consultations:** Regular, country-specific assessments that review economic developments, policy implementation, and macroeconomic stability. Recent rounds (e.g., 2025 reviews of Hungary, Italy, and Romania) emphasize addressing sluggish growth, widening twin deficits, and costly pension/wage dynamics.
- **Fiscal Policy Guidance:** Emphasizes high-quality fiscal consolidation to rebuild buffers and stabilize public debt. Recommendations include implementing comprehensive reform packages (e.g., VAT/excise/dividend tax adjustments, public wage and pension freezes), targeting structural primary surpluses, broadening the tax base, phasing out distortive subsidies, and establishing Medium-Term Fiscal Frameworks (e.g., targeting deficits below 6% of GDP within two years and under 3% by 2030).
- **Monetary Policy Recommendations:** Advocates for a cautious, data-dependent monetary stance to durably anchor inflation expectations. Central banks are advised to pause rate cuts until wage growth and core inflation show sustained moderation, while also gradually increasing exchange rate flexibility to enhance resilience against external shocks and restore competitiveness.
- **Financial Sector Oversight:** Highlights supervisory vigilance for emerging vulnerabilities including the sovereign-bank nexus, sizable unhedged foreign exchange corporate loans, buoyant consumer credit growth, commercial real estate risks, and pressures on less significant institutions. Calibrated macroprudential policies are recommended to safeguard asset quality.
- **Structural & Governance Reforms:** Prioritizes streamlined insolvency frameworks, expanded access to risk capital, accelerated absorption of EU/NGEU funds, improved public investment management, and enhanced state-owned enterprise (SOE) governance. Labor market enhancements focus on increasing female and older worker participation to offset demographic headwinds, alongside advancing low-carbon transitions via mechanisms like ETS2.
- **Climate & Energy Security:** Increasingly treats accelerating the transition to renewable energy, investing in resilient infrastructure, and mitigating macro-critical climate-related shocks as essential. Policy guidance addresses energy price volatility, carbon pricing mechanisms (e.g., ETS2), and trade competitiveness in a fragmented global economy.
- **Policy Coordination & Data Dependency:** Works collaboratively with national authorities (central banks, finance ministries) and employs rigorous data-dependent approaches to navigate high economic uncertainty, global trade tensions, geoeconomic fragmentation, and geopolitical risks.
