# Italy

## Summary

Italy continues to exhibit moderate economic expansion and a resilient labor market, though it faces pressing structural challenges that require accelerated reform momentum. Recent IMF assessments highlight a successful return of inflation toward target and improved fiscal metrics, yet persistent high public debt, weak productivity growth, and intensifying demographic pressures necessitate sustained policy adjustments. The implementation of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) and targeted structural reforms are deemed critical to modernizing infrastructure, boosting labor supply, and ensuring long-term macroeconomic stability amid global uncertainties.

## Key Details

- **Economic & Labor Market Performance:** 2024 real GDP grew by 0.7%, supported by NRRP infrastructure spending and net exports. The labor market reached record employment levels (62.7% employment rate), though female participation remains below the EU average and youth unemployment persists. Inflation has cooled to ~1.7%, aligning closer to the ECB's target.
- **Fiscal Position & Debt Trajectory:** The headline deficit narrowed to 3.4% of GDP with a 0.4% primary surplus in 2024, driven by strong revenue collection and improved tax compliance. However, public debt remains elevated at 135.3% of GDP. Authorities have outlined a Medium-Term Fiscal Structural Plan (MTFSP) targeting an additional 3% primary surplus by 2027 through tax expenditure rationalization and efficiency gains.
- **Structural Reforms & Demographics:** Population aging is straining pension systems and constraining potential growth. Boosting labor supply (especially women and older workers), upgrading skills, and fostering private sector dynamism are prioritized. Full NRRP implementation is essential to streamline public administration, boost human capital, and modernize infrastructure.
- **Financial Sector & Macroprudential Framework:** Major banks demonstrate robust profitability, sound asset quality, and ample capital buffers. Macroprudential settings are adequate, though vigilance is required regarding vulnerabilities in less significant institutions and the sovereign-bank nexus. AML/CFT frameworks have seen recent strengthening.
- **Outlook & Risk Environment:** Growth is projected to moderate to 0.5% in 2025 before a slight pickup to 0.8% in 2026. Downside risks outweigh upside potential due to global trade tensions, geopolitical conflicts, tighter financial conditions, and climate-related shocks. Accelerating the green transition and energy security investments are flagged as macro-critical priorities.
