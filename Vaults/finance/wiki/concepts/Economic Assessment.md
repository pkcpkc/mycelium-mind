# Economic Assessment

## Summary

[[Economic Assessment]] is a foundational pillar within sovereign credit rating frameworks, primarily deployed by [[S&P Global Ratings]], to evaluate a country's underlying economic fundamentals and its capacity to sustain income generation. It serves as the economic component of a broader five-pillar scoring model that collectively determines sovereign creditworthiness. The assessment measures the underlying economic foundation by analyzing GDP per capita, growth trajectories, and economic diversification to project future tax capacity, ensuring a forward-looking evaluation of fiscal sustainability and structural resilience.

## Key Details

- **Evaluation Scale:** Scored on a 1-to-6 scale (1 = strongest, 6 = weakest) as part of the sovereign rating methodology.
- **Primary Anchor:** Grounded in [[GDP per Capita]] as the baseline metric for economic development and living standards.
- **Adjustment Factors:** Refined based on growth prospects, economic diversity, and the quality/reliability of economic data.
- **Tax Capacity Projection:** Explicitly utilized to forecast future government revenue potential based on economic trajectories and structural resilience.
- **Framework Integration:** Aggregated with the [[Institutional Assessment]] to form the [[Institutional and Economic Profile]], which is then cross-referenced with fiscal and monetary scores to derive indicative sovereign ratings.
- **Methodological Evolution:** Formalized in the 2017 update to sovereign rating criteria, shifting focus toward structural economic resilience, transition dynamics, and forward-looking indicators rather than static historical snapshots.
- **Discretion & Replication:** The methodology explicitly allows for [[Discretionary Judgment]] by analysts, which has inspired citizen science initiatives to systematically apply S&P's criteria across European nations to document transparency, consistency, and areas of opaque adjustment.
