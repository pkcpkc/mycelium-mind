# Public Procurement

## Summary

Public procurement refers to the systematic processes through which public authorities acquire goods, services, and works. Within the European Union's rule of law monitoring framework, it is consistently identified as a high-risk sector for corruption, graft, and market distortion across multiple member states. Recent assessments highlight divergent national challenges: Austria requires continuous oversight by judicial and competition authorities to mitigate systemic risks, while Hungary exhibits fragmented oversight and preferential treatment for state-aligned entities, though competition has seen slight improvements. In Malta, public procurement is evaluated within the broader anti-corruption and institutional checks and balances review under the 2025 EU Rule of Law Report. Similarly, Poland's landscape illustrates these systemic vulnerabilities, with public procurement remaining a high-risk sector for graft despite some successful investigations into bid-rigging and embezzlement. In Romania, corruption risks in public procurement persist, prompting ongoing digitalization efforts under the Recovery and Resilience Plan to enhance transparency and mitigate systemic vulnerabilities. In Slovakia, the 2025 EU Rule of Law Report notes that recent public procurement reforms have inadvertently lowered oversight safeguards, increasing corruption risks for businesses and deterring investment. Furthermore, the 2025 Country Report on Poland identifies public procurement as a key economic entity within a broader context of fiscal sustainability and the need for improved public spending efficiency in large-scale infrastructure projects.

## Key Details

- Flagged as a high-risk sector for corruption and graft in the 2025 EU Rule of Law Report, with specific national assessments highlighting Austria's need for judicial oversight and Hungary's fragmented regulatory environment.
- Subject to continuous scrutiny by national bodies (e.g., Austria’s Court of Audit and competition authorities) to ensure market integrity and compliance with EU procurement directives.
- In Hungary, competition has slightly improved, but significant risks persist due to fragmented oversight mechanisms and preferential treatment granted to state-aligned entities.
- Integral component of national anti-corruption frameworks, requiring transparent case allocation, robust integrity checks, and alignment with European standards to prevent political or market distortion.
- Ongoing regulatory scrutiny aims to close transparency gaps, strengthen institutional checks and balances, and ensure fair access for all market participants.
- Referenced in the 2025 EU Rule of Law Report for Malta as part of the ongoing assessment of anti-corruption enforcement and institutional safeguards.
- In Poland, public procurement remains a high-risk sector for graft, though recent years have seen successful investigations into bid-rigging and embezzlement, indicating gradual enforcement improvements.
- National anti-corruption efforts in Poland are being tested by weak political party financing regulations and broad immunities for top executives, which continue to hinder full accountability in procurement processes.
- [[Romania]] faces persistent corruption risks in public procurement, driving ongoing digitalization initiatives under the Recovery and Resilience Plan to strengthen oversight mechanisms and improve market transparency.
- In Slovakia, public procurement reforms have inadvertently lowered oversight safeguards, increasing corruption risks for businesses and deterring investment, as highlighted in the 2025 EU Rule of Law Report.
- Identified in the 2025 Country Report for Poland as a key entity requiring improved public spending efficiency, particularly regarding large-scale infrastructure projects and fiscal sustainability.
- In Romania, public procurement is a key entity within the economic transition toward high-tech investment and infrastructure modernization, where digitalization of public services is a primary recommendation to improve transparency.
- Linked to the Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) in Romania as a critical area for executing milestones and targets to unlock EU funding.
- In Slovakia, public procurement is specifically identified as a core area for governance reform; the 2025 Country Report emphasizes that strengthening anti-corruption measures and improving governance are essential to mitigate the risks introduced by recent reform oversights.
