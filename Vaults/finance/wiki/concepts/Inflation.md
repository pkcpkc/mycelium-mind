# Inflation

## Summary

Inflation refers to the sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services within an economy. Recent macroeconomic assessments highlight divergent regional dynamics alongside converging structural pressures. In Austria, inflation has eased from historical peaks but remains structurally elevated at approximately 3 percent, driven by sticky services sector prices and robust nominal wage growth. Conversely, in Romania, headline inflation surged to 9.9% in September 2025 following the removal of electricity price caps and tax adjustments, with core inflation remaining elevated due to strong nominal wage growth and services demand. Meanwhile, in the Slovak Republic, inflation peaked above 15% in early 2023 but has since moderated, experiencing a temporary uptick in late 2024 and early 2025 due to higher indirect taxes and global food prices, with projections indicating a gradual return to the 2% target by early 2027. Additionally, Hungary experienced a significant inflationary period peaking at 17.0% in 2023, contributing to a slow economic recovery and high debt-servicing costs. Despite these differences in magnitude and drivers, all three economies face persistent core inflationary pressures linked to labor markets, energy transitions, and fiscal adjustments, necessitating cautious monetary stances and coordinated structural reforms to ensure price stability.

## Key Details

- **Regional Trajectories:**
  - *Austria:* Easing from peaks but structurally elevated at ~3%, consistently above the euro area average, influenced by sticky services sector prices.
  - *Romania:* Headline inflation spiked to 9.9% in September 2025 due to energy cap removal and tax adjustments; core inflation remains high due to wage and services pressures.
  - *Slovak Republic:* Peaked above 15% in early 2023, followed by a broad moderation. A temporary rebound in late 2024/early 2025 was driven by indirect tax hikes and global food prices, with a projected return to the 2% target by early 2027.
  - *Hungary:* Peaked at 17.0% in 2023, creating a challenging environment for economic growth and contributing to high public debt-servicing costs.
- **Core Drivers:** Persistent inflation across these regions is consistently linked to sticky services prices, strong nominal wage growth (e.g., averaging 7–8% in Austria), robust services demand, energy policy shifts (e.g., cap removals), indirect tax adjustments, and global commodity volatility. These factors create risks of a wage-price spiral and complicate policy transmission.
- **Monetary & Fiscal Policy Responses:** Central banks are adopting cautious stances to anchor expectations. For instance, the [[National Bank of Romania]] (NBR) paused rate cuts and maintained the benchmark rate at 6.5%, while the [[IMF]] staff recommends that rate reductions should only resume once inflation and wage growth show sustained moderation. In Slovakia, aggressive [[fiscal consolidation]] (aiming to reduce the deficit from 5.8% in 2024 to 2.2% of GDP by 2028) is being implemented alongside monetary measures to restore macroeconomic stability without stifling the post-recession recovery.
- **Macroeconomic Impact:** Increases household cost-of-living pressures, influences the transmission of [[Monetary Policy]], and complicates [[Fiscal Consolidation]] by raising public and private wage bill burdens. Divergent inflation rates also affect real exchange rates, competitiveness, and trade balances within the Euro Area.
- **Structural Risks & Policy Implications:**
  - Persistent inflation differentials threaten long-term competitiveness and export margins, particularly when coupled with demographic aging and labor market rigidities.
  - Requires coordinated monetary tightening, structural labor market reforms to improve productivity, and targeted fiscal measures (e.g., VAT adjustments, public wage freezes, expenditure-based consolidation) to anchor inflation expectations.
  - Upside risks include domestic wage pressures, energy price volatility, indirect tax reforms, global food supply chains, and geopolitical uncertainties affecting trade barriers.
