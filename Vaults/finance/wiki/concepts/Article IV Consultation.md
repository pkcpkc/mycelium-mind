# Article IV Consultation

## Summary

The Article IV Consultation is the cornerstone of the International Monetary Fund's (IMF) surveillance mandate, serving as a periodic review of member countries' economic developments and policies. The process involves extensive data collection, analysis by IMF staff, and policy discussions with national authorities. It concludes with the publication of an IMF Staff Report detailing economic assessments and policy recommendations, followed by Executive Board conclusions that summarize the institution's consensus view. Recent consultations, such as the 2025 reviews of Hungary and Italy, illustrate the framework's role in addressing multifaceted challenges including inflation control, fiscal sustainability, structural competitiveness, demographic headwinds, and productivity gaps. More recently, the 2024 consultation for [[Malta]] highlighted the IMF's focus on managing capacity constraints from rapid service-sector growth, advancing fiscal consolidation under new EU frameworks, aligning corporate taxation with Pillar II directives, and balancing economic expansion with climate resilience and financial stability. Expanding its global surveillance reach, the 2025 consultation for [[Romania]] underscored the framework's utility in navigating complex dual challenges, including sluggish growth, widening twin deficits, and necessary fiscal consolidation amid persistent inflationary pressures and structural bottlenecks. Similarly, the 2025 review for the [[Slovak Republic]] highlighted an economy navigating a complex transition characterized by modest growth recovery, persistent inflationary pressures, and significant medium-term fiscal challenges, with a strong focus on aggressive [[fiscal consolidation]], financial sector strengthening, and structural reforms to address demographic headwinds and governance risks.

## Key Details

- **Process and Outputs**: The consultation cycle typically results in the publication of a Staff Report outlining economic analysis, risks, and policy recommendations, followed by Executive Board conclusions endorsing the staff's assessment. Timelines vary but generally span several months of staff discussions, report drafting, and board review.
- **Policy Focus Areas**: Assessments generally evaluate fiscal policy, monetary policy, structural reforms, financial sector stability, external balance, and demographic/economic resilience. Recommendations often target macroeconomic stabilization, structural efficiency, risk management, long-term growth potential, and alignment with supranational regulatory frameworks.
- **Hungary 2025 Assessment**:
    - **Economic Context**: Hungary faced economic stagnation and persistent inflation above the 3 percent target, with public debt at approximately 73.5 percent of GDP and regulatory interventions distorting market signals.
    - **Fiscal Recommendations**: Emphasis on rebuilding fiscal buffers with a target structural primary surplus of approximately 1.75 percent of GDP, broadening the tax base, phasing out distortive retail energy subsidies and price/margin caps, and enhancing monitoring of state-owned enterprises.
    - **Monetary Policy Guidance**: Advocacy for a tight monetary stance through 2026 to anchor inflation expectations, including the phase-out of retail margin caps and voluntary interest rate ceilings that hinder monetary transmission.
    - **Structural and Governance Reforms**: Priority on closing productivity gaps, streamlining insolvency frameworks, advancing the green transition, and implementing rule-of-law reforms to unlock suspended EU Funds and improve investment confidence.
    - **Outlook and Risks**: Projections indicated modest recovery with GDP growth reaching 0.7 percent in 2025 and 2 percent in 2026, alongside inflation deceleration. Key risks included geoeconomic fragmentation, delays in EU Funds disbursements due to conditionality, and fiscal impacts from national elections.
- **Italy 2025 Assessment**:
    - **Economic and Fiscal Performance**: The economy experienced moderate expansion (0.7% real GDP growth in 2024) with a resilient labor market and improving fiscal metrics (deficit at 3.4% of GDP, primary surplus of 0.4%). However, public debt remains critically high at 135.3% of GDP. The IMF recommends an additional primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2027, achievable through rationalizing tax expenditures, improving efficiency, and phasing out unproductive incentives.
    - **Structural and Demographic Challenges**: Persistent productivity growth deficits and an aging population pose significant headwinds. Full implementation of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) is deemed vital to modernize infrastructure and streamline public administration. Future reforms must target expanding labor supply (particularly women and older workers), upskilling, revitalizing private sector investment, and deepening EU single market integration.
    - **Financial Sector Stability**: The banking sector shows robustness with record profitability, low non-performing loan ratios, and adequate macroprudential buffers. Vigilance is advised regarding vulnerabilities in Less Significant Institutions (LSIs), the sovereign-bank nexus, and potential credit deterioration amid trade uncertainties. Progress in AML/CFT frameworks is also noted.
    - **Outlook and Risks**: Growth is projected to moderate to 0.5% in 2025 before reaching 0.8% in 2026, supported by peak NRRP spending. Downside risks dominate due to global trade tensions, regional conflicts, tighter financial conditions, and macro-critical climate/energy security shocks. Efficient NRRP implementation and timely structural reforms are critical to realizing upside potential.
- **Malta 2024 Assessment**:
    - **Economic Context**: The economy experienced a decade of robust expansion driven by export-oriented service sectors like [[Tourism]] and [[Online Gaming]], with GDP per capita converging to the EU average. Growth moderated to an estimated 5% in 2024, with inflation stabilizing near the 2% target. However, rapid influxes of foreign workers and tourists have strained infrastructure, public services, and the [[Real Estate]] market, driving up rents and creating tight labor market conditions heavily reliant on non-EU migration.
    - **Fiscal and Tax Policy Recommendations**: The IMF urged accelerated consolidation under the EU’s new fiscal framework and the establishment of a long-term fiscal framework aligned with [[Malta Vision 2050]]. Key recommendations include phasing out fixed energy subsidies to redirect funds toward green investment, innovation, and health services. A clear roadmap for [[Corporate Income Tax]] reform is required to comply with the EU’s [[Pillar II]] directive, preventing revenue leakage.
    - **Structural and Innovation Reforms**: To transition from a labor-intensive growth model to a productivity-driven one, the report emphasizes expanding [[Venture Capital Fund]] support, reforming education to boost STEM enrollment and digital skills, and accelerating [[Innovation and Digitalization]]. Continued progress in [[Anti-Money Laundering]] standards and judicial reforms is critical for maintaining investor confidence.
    - **Financial Stability and Climate Goals**: While the banking sector remains well-capitalized and liquid, supervisors are urged to raise the sectoral systemic risk buffer to address concentrated exposures in residential and commercial real estate, close data gaps, and enhance cyber risk assessments. Structurally, meeting ambitious [[Climate Change Mitigation]] targets requires accelerating renewable energy adoption and promoting sustainable tourism practices to balance economic gains with environmental resilience.
    - **Outlook and Risks**: Medium-term projections indicate continued outperformance of broader European averages, though structural bottlenecks will temper potential. Downside risks are tilted toward geopolitical tensions, domestic wage pressures, and cyber threats. Timely implementation of the [[Recovery and Resilience Plan]] and structural reforms will be vital for managing growth sustainability.
- **Romania 2025 Assessment**:
    - **Economic Context & Outlook**: The economy recorded modest growth of 0.9% in 2024, projected to recover gradually to 1.0% in 2025 and 1.4% in 2026, supported by accelerated [[NGEU]]-funded investments and moderated private consumption.
    - **Fiscal Consolidation & Deficits**: The primary challenge involves widening twin deficits, with the fiscal deficit reaching 8.7% of GDP and the current account deficit at 8.2% in 2024, driven by costly pension reforms and rising public wages. The authorities implemented a comprehensive 2025–26 reform package (VAT increases, higher excise/dividend taxes, public wage/pension freezes) targeting a deficit reduction below 6% by 2026 and below 3% by 2030 to stabilize public debt.
    - **Inflation & Monetary Policy**: Headline inflation surged to 9.9% in September 2025 following the removal of electricity price caps and tax adjustments, with core inflation remaining elevated by wage growth and services demand. The [[National Bank of Romania]] (NBR), led by Governor [[Mugur Isarescu]], paused rate cuts, maintaining the benchmark at 6.5%. The IMF advises resuming easing only after sustained inflation moderation and advocates for greater exchange rate flexibility to bolster competitiveness and shock absorption.
    - **Financial Sector Stability**: The banking sector maintains robust capital adequacy and liquidity, but faces emerging vulnerabilities from a growing sovereign-bank nexus, sizable unhedged foreign currency corporate loans, and rapid consumer credit growth. Enhanced supervisory vigilance and calibrated macroprudential policies are recommended to safeguard asset quality.
    - **Structural Reforms & Long-term Growth**: Critical priorities include unlocking stalled EU funds, improving public investment management, and reforming [[State-Owned Enterprises]] governance. Boosting labor force participation (especially among women) and advancing the low-carbon transition via mechanisms like [[ETS2]] are vital for sustaining growth potential.
    - **Outlook & Risks**: Growth projections face downside risks from geopolitical uncertainties and potential trade barriers, while upside inflation risks persist due to domestic wage pressures and energy volatility. Authorities broadly align with staff recommendations, emphasizing fiscal discipline and structural modernization.
- **Slovakia 2025 Assessment**:
    - **Economic Context & Outlook**: Growth accelerated to 2.0 percent in 2024 following a sharp slowdown in 2022–2023, driven by robust private consumption fueled by real wage growth and expanded pension benefits. Near-term outlook points to a deceleration to 1.8 percent in 2025 due to planned [[fiscal consolidation]], with a projected rebound to 2.1 percent in 2026 as external demand improves and EU-funded investments continue. Inflation peaked above 15 percent in early 2023, has largely moderated but experienced a temporary uptick in late 2024 and early 2025 due to higher indirect taxes and global food prices, with projections indicating a gradual return to the 2 percent target by early 2027.
    - **Fiscal Consolidation & Debt Sustainability**: A central focus is reversing a widening deficit that reached 5.8 percent of GDP in 2024. The government’s four-year plan aims to reduce the headline deficit to 2.2 percent of GDP by 2028, a target endorsed by IMF staff as appropriately ambitious. Recommendations prioritize expenditure-based measures over revenue increases to maintain balance, suggesting reforms such as reducing VAT exemptions, implementing market-value-based property taxes, trimming departmental budgets, and phasing out temporary energy support. Safeguarding the national fiscal framework requires reforming the constitutional debt brake before 2026 and strengthening the Council for Budgetary Responsibility to prevent disruptive consolidation and ensure long-term debt sustainability.
    - **Financial Sector Resilience**: The banking system remains well-capitalized and liquid, with stress tests confirming its ability to withstand severe macro-financial shocks. Nevertheless, vulnerabilities persist in the residential and commercial real estate markets, particularly regarding upcoming mortgage repricing and elevated office vacancy rates. The report advises maintaining the current macroprudential stance while exploring the adoption of a positive neutral countercyclical capital buffer (pnCCyB) and closing loopholes in borrower-based measures. Enhancement of supervision for less significant institutions and strengthening the crisis management framework are also emphasized.
    - **Structural, Demographic & Governance Reforms**: The economy faces headwinds from an aging population, labor shortages, and over-reliance on the automotive sector. Sustaining productivity growth requires accelerating innovation, expanding Active Labor Market Policies (ALMPs), and deepening integration with the European single market. Addressing demographic pressures necessitates policies to boost labor force participation among women and the elderly, streamline migrant integration, and reform pension systems. Governance reforms are urgently needed to combat corruption, enhance judicial independence, and improve public administration efficiency, as weak institutional quality currently threatens the disbursement of critical EU funds.
    - **Environmental & Climate Policies**: On the environmental front, Slovakia is urged to expeditiously implement the EU’s ETS II scheme for transport and buildings while gradually raising environmental levies to meet its 2030 and 2050 climate targets.
    - **Outlook & Risks**: Moderate growth recovery is projected, but downside risks remain from geopolitical uncertainties, domestic wage pressures, and potential delays in EU fund absorption tied to governance conditionality.
- **Key Entities**: Engagements involve the IMF Executive Board, IMF staff teams (e.g., [[Oya Celasun]], [[Stefania Fabrizio]]), national central banks (e.g., [[Magyar Nemzeti Bank]], [[ECB]], [[National Bank of Romania]]), government authorities, and relevant supranational bodies (e.g., [[European Union]]). National frameworks like the NRRP, [[Malta Vision 2050]], and independent fiscal councils frequently intersect with consultation recommendations.
