# Twin Deficits

## Summary
The twin deficits refer to a macroeconomic phenomenon where an economy experiences both a significant government budget deficit and a substantial current account deficit simultaneously. In the 2025 IMF assessment of Romania, this imbalance emerged as fiscal pressures from pension reforms and public wage increases pushed the budget deficit to 8.7% of GDP, while widening trade and investment imbalances expanded the current account deficit to 8.2% of GDP. This dual shortfall constrains economic growth, elevates inflationary pressures, and necessitates coordinated fiscal and monetary policy responses to restore stability.

## Key Details
- **Macroeconomic Mechanism**: Fiscal deficits often stimulate domestic demand, increasing imports and widening the trade gap. Concurrently, government borrowing can drive up interest rates, attracting foreign capital that appreciates the domestic currency, further eroding export competitiveness and reinforcing the current account shortfall.
- **Romania Case Study (2024–2025)**: The simultaneous deterioration of both deficits contributed to sluggish GDP growth (0.9% in 2024) and forced the government to implement a comprehensive consolidation package. Measures included VAT rate increases, higher excise and dividend taxes, and a freeze on public sector wages and pensions.
- **Policy Targets**: Authorities aim to reduce the combined fiscal burden below 6% of GDP by 2026 and achieve a structural deficit under 3% by 2030 to stabilize public debt, restore market confidence, and ensure long-term sustainability.
- **Monetary & Inflation Linkages**: Deficit-driven demand pressures and energy price adjustments contributed to headline inflation peaking at 9.9% in September 2025, prompting the [[National Bank of Romania]] to maintain a tight monetary stance (6.5% benchmark rate) until nominal wage growth and services demand show sustained moderation.
- **Structural Vulnerabilities**: The imbalance highlights underlying weaknesses in revenue collection, public investment efficiency, and competitiveness, underscoring the need for structural reforms to improve [[State-Owned Enterprises]] governance, accelerate [[EU Funds]] deployment, and boost labor force participation.
