# Productivity Growth

## Summary

Productivity Growth represents the increase in output per unit of input, functioning as a fundamental driver for sustainable economic expansion and fiscal consolidation. In the context of Italy's 2025 IMF Article IV Consultation, productivity growth has been persistently weak compared to peer economies, constrained by limited innovation, low research and development (R&D) investment, and a shortage of high-skilled workers. Addressing these bottlenecks through structural reforms and effective implementation of recovery plans is deemed essential to lift potential growth and offset adverse demographic trends.

## Key Details

- **Persistent Weakness:** Productivity growth in Italy lags behind peer economies, limiting the country's ability to sustain high growth rates and manage public debt burdens.
- **Structural Constraints:** Key barriers include limited innovation capacity, insufficient R&D investment, and a scarcity of high-skilled labor, which hinder efficiency gains.
- **Role of NRRP:** Full and timely implementation of the National Recovery and Resilience Plan (NRRP) is critical to modernize infrastructure, streamline public administration, and boost human capital, thereby enhancing productivity.
- **Reform Priorities:** Future structural reforms must focus on expanding the labor supply (particularly among women and older workers), upgrading skills, reviving private sector dynamism through improved access to risk capital, and deepening EU single market integration.
- **Demographic Headwinds:** Structural reforms are identified as critical to durably lifting growth and offsetting the economic constraints imposed by population aging and rising dependency ratios.
- **Risks to Gains:** Inefficient implementation of the NRRP or delays in structural reforms pose significant downside risks that could further dampen productivity gains and long-term growth prospects.
