# Andrej Karpathy

## Biography & Context

Andrej Karpathy is a prominent AI researcher and educator renowned for his foundational work in [[Deep Learning]], [[Computer Vision]], and [[Large Language Models]]. He served as the Director of AI at [[Tesla]], where he led the computer vision team for [[Tesla Autopilot]] and the Optimus robot, overseeing data labeling, neural network training, and deployment on custom inference chips. Prior to his tenure at Tesla, Karpathy was a founding member of [[OpenAI]] in 2015 and currently focuses on midtraining and synthetic data generation.

Karpathy earned his PhD from [[Stanford University]], conducting research under the supervision of [[Fei-Fei Li]]. During his academic development, he completed rotations and collaborated with several leading figures including [[Daphne Koller]], [[Andrew Ng]], [[Sebastian Thrun]], and [[Vladlen Koltun]]. He previously completed his master's studies at the University of British Columbia, working with [[Michiel van de Panne]] on robotics simulation. His early fascination with deep learning was sparked by attending [[Geoff Hinton]]'s lectures at the University of Toronto.

As a dedicated educator, Karpathy designed and taught [[Stanford University]]'s [[CS 231n]] course, which expanded into one of the university's largest enrollments. He shares his expertise through a popular [[YouTube]] channel featuring series like "Zero to Hero" and in-depth tutorials on building models from scratch. His open-source contributions include influential projects such as [[micrograd]], [[char-rnn]], [[Arxiv-Sanity]], [[neuraltalk2]], and [[ConvNetJS]].

His research portfolio encompasses significant advancements in image captioning, recurrent neural networks, and visual-semantic alignments. Karpathy has been a featured keynote speaker at major industry events, including [[NVIDIA GTC]] alongside [[Jensen Huang]], and has shared insights on various podcasts such as [[Lex Fridman]], [[GPU Mode]], [[No Priors]], and the [[Dwarlesh]] podcast.
