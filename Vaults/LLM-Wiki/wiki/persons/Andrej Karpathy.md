## Biography & Context

Andrej Karpathy is an AI researcher, educator, and engineer renowned for his contributions to deep learning, computer vision, and large language models. He is a founding member of [[OpenAI]], joining in 2015 and later returning to lead a team focused on midtraining and synthetic data generation. At [[Tesla]], he served as Director of AI, where he led the computer vision team for Autopilot and briefly Tesla Optimus, overseeing in-house data labeling, neural network training, and deployment on Tesla's custom inference chip.

Karpathy earned his PhD from [[Stanford]] with a focus on convolutional and recurrent neural networks and their applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and their intersection. He conducted rotation work with [[Daphne Koller]], [[Andrew Ng]], [[Sebastian Thrun]], and [[Vladlen Koltun]] during his first-year rotation program before joining the Stanford Vision Lab under [[Fei-Fei Li]]. Prior to Stanford, he completed an MSc at the [[University of British Columbia]], working with [[Michiel van de Panne]] on learning controllers for physically-simulated figures, and a BSc at the [[University of Toronto]], where he first became involved in deep learning by attending [[Geoffrey Hinton]]'s classes and reading groups.

Karpathy designed and was the primary instructor for Stanford CS231n, which grew from 150 students in 2015 to 750 by 2017, becoming one of Stanford's largest classes. He maintains a popular YouTube channel featuring the Zero to Hero playlist (covering LLM internals from scratch, including building GPT, makemore, and micrograd) and general audience content on LLMs.

He has built numerous influential open-source projects including micrograd (a scalar-valued autograd engine), char-rnn (a character-level language model), arxiv-sanity (research paper discovery tool), neuraltalk2 (image captioning), and ConvNetJS (browser-based deep learning library). Karpathy is also known for competing against an early ConvNet on ImageNet image categorization, earning the nickname "reference human for ImageNet."

He has published extensively in top-tier venues including ICML, ICLR, CVPR, NIPS, and TACL. Notable blog posts include "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks" (2015), "A Recipe for Training Neural Networks" (2019), and "Software 2.0" (2017). His research ethos is to "train deep neural nets on large datasets."

Karpathy has delivered numerous featured talks including State of GPT at Microsoft Build 2023, Tesla AI Day 2021, AI for Full Self-Driving at CVPR 2021, and Multi-Task Learning in the Wilderness at ICML 2019. He is actively engaged on X (formerly Twitter) and GitHub for the latest updates on his work.
