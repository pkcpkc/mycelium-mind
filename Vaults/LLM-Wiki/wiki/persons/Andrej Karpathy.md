## Biography & Context

Andrej Karpathy is a prominent AI researcher and educator known for his foundational contributions to [[Deep Learning]], [[Computer Vision]], and [[Large Language Models]]. He currently works at [[OpenAI]] focusing on midtraining and synthetic data generation, following his tenure as a founding member of the organization in 2015. Previously, Karpathy served as the Director of AI at [[Tesla]], where he led the computer vision team for [[Tesla Autopilot]] and Optimus, overseeing data labeling, neural network training, and deployment on custom inference chips.

Karpathy earned his PhD from [[Stanford University]] under the guidance of [[Fei-Fei Li]], with academic rotations and collaborations involving [[Daphne Koller]], [[Andrew Ng]], [[Sebastian Thrun]], and [[Vladlen Koltun]]. His early academic journey included a master's at the University of British Columbia working with [[Michiel van de Panne]] on robotics simulation, and his foundational interest in deep learning was sparked by attending [[Geoff Hinton]]'s lectures at the University of Toronto.

As an educator and content creator, Karpathy designed and taught [[Stanford University]]'s [[CS 231n]], which became one of the university's largest courses. He has expanded his educational reach through a popular [[YouTube]] channel featuring series like "Zero to Hero," LLM deep dives, and tutorials on building models from scratch. His research portfolio spans image captioning, recurrent neural networks, and visual-semantic alignments, often published alongside notable researchers such as [[Justin Johnson]], [[Diederik P. Kingma]], and [[Tim Salimans]]. Karpathy has shared his insights as a keynote speaker at events like [[NVIDIA GTC]] and has been featured on numerous podcasts discussing AI development and industry trends.
