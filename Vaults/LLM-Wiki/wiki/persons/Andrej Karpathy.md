## Biography & Context

Andrej Karpathy is an AI researcher and educator known for creating influential educational content on deep learning and artificial intelligence. He was a founding member of OpenAI in 2015, working there as a research scientist before leaving to join Tesla.

At Tesla, Karpathy served as Director of AI, where he led the computer vision team responsible for Tesla Autopilot and briefly for Tesla Optimus. His team handled all in-house data labeling, neural network training, and deployment on Tesla's custom inference chip. ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

During his PhD at Stanford University (2011–2016), advised by Fei-Fei Li, Karpathy focused on convolutional and recurrent neural networks applied to computer vision and natural language processing. He designed and became the primary instructor for CS 231n: Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition, which grew from 150 enrolled students in 2015 to 750 students in 2017, becoming one of the largest classes at Stanford. ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

Karpathy held early research positions at Google Brain (2011, unsupervised learning from videos), Google Research (2013, supervised learning on YouTube videos), and DeepMind (2015, deep reinforcement learning) before returning to OpenAI to build a new team focused on midtraining and synthetic data generation. ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

He earned his MSc at the University of British Columbia, working with Michiel van de Panne on learning controllers for physically-simulated figures, and his BSc at the University of Toronto with a double major in computer science and physics. At Toronto, he attended Geoff Hinton's classes and reading groups, which sparked his interest in deep learning. ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

Karpathy is best known for his popular YouTube educational content, including the Neural Networks: Zero to Hero lecture series, the "Let's build GPT" tutorial (3.5M views), and the "Building makemore" series. His educational videos cover both technical deep dives into LLM fundamentals and practical guides for general audiences. ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

As a creator of open-source projects, Karpathy has built micrograd (a scalar-valued autograd engine), char-rnn (a character-level language model), arxiv-sanity (a research paper discovery tool), neuraltalk2 (an image captioning project), and ConvNetJS (a deep learning library written in JavaScript). ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

His PhD thesis, "Connecting Images and Natural Language" (2016), produced influential work on dense captioning, image-sentence mapping, and visual-semantic alignments. He is sometimes jokingly referred to as the "reference human" for ImageNet because he competed against an early ConvNet on categorizing images into 1,000 classes. ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))

Notable publications include World of Bits (ICML 2017), PixelCNN++ (ICLR 2017), DenseCap (CVPR 2016 Oral), Deep Fragment Embeddings (NIPS 2014), and the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge paper (JCV 2015). ([Andrej_Karpathy.md](assets/2026-06-20/Andrej_Karpathy.md))
