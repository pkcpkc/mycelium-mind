# Andrej Karpathy — Bio & Portfolio

This document is the personal bio and portfolio page of **Andrej Karpathy**, an AI researcher, educator, and engineer known for his work in deep learning, computer vision, and large language models.

## Career Overview

Karpathy is a founding member of **OpenAI** (2015), where he later returned to lead a team focused on midtraining and synthetic data generation. He served as Director of AI at **Tesla**, where he led the computer vision team for **Autopilot** and briefly **Tesla Optimus**. His team handled in-house data labeling, neural network training, and deployment on Tesla's custom inference chip. The Autopilot system improves driving safety with the long-term goal of achieving Full Self-Driving at scale.

## Education

Karpathy earned his PhD from **Stanford** with a focus on convolutional and recurrent neural networks and their applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and their intersection. His PhD advisor was **Fei-Fei Li** at the Stanford Vision Lab. He also worked with **Daphne Koller**, **Andrew Ng**, **Sebastian Thrun**, and **Vladlen Koltun** during his first-year rotation program.

Prior to Stanford, he completed an MSc at the **University of British Columbia** (2009–2011), working with **Michiel van de Panne** on learning controllers for physically-simulated figures (machine learning for agile robotics in simulation). He received his BSc from the **University of Toronto** (2005–2009) with a double major in computer science and physics and a minor in mathematics, where he first became involved in deep learning by attending **Geoffrey Hinton**'s classes and reading groups.

## Internships

- **Google Brain** (2011): Learning-scale unsupervised learning from videos
- **Google Research** (2013): Large-scale supervised learning on YouTube videos
- **DeepMind** (2015): Deep reinforcement learning with **Koray Kavukcuoglu** and **Vlad Mnih**

## Teaching & Education

Karpathy designed and was the primary instructor for **Stanford CS231n: Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition**, the first deep learning class at Stanford. It grew from 150 students in 2015 to 750 by 2017, becoming one of Stanford's largest classes.

His YouTube channel features two parallel tracks:
- **Technical track**: The **Zero to Hero** playlist, covering LLM internals from scratch including building **GPT**, **makemore**, and **micrograd** with detailed explanations of backpropagation, activations, gradients, and batch normalization.
- **General audience track**: Deep dives into **LLMs** like **ChatGPT**, practical guides on using **LLMs**, and an introductory talk on large language models.

## Featured Talks

Karpathy has delivered numerous talks across the AI community, including **State of GPT** at Microsoft Build 2023, **Tesla AI Day** 2021, **AI for Full Self-Driving** at CVPR 2021 and ScaledML 2020, **Multi-Task Learning in the Wilderness** at ICML 2019, and **PyTorch at Tesla** at PyTorch DevCon 2019. Notable appearances include NVIDIA GTC 2015 with **Jensen Huang**, YC AI Startup School 2025, GPU Mode 2024, UC Berkeley AI Hackathon 2024, Lex Fridman Podcast 2022, Robot Brains Podcast with **Pieter Abbeel** 2021, and the Heroes of Deep Learning event with **Andrew Ng** in 2017.

## Projects

Karpathy has built numerous influential open-source projects:

- **micrograd**: A tiny scalar-valued autograd engine implementing backpropagation (reverse-mode autodiff) over a dynamically built DAG with a **PyTorch**-like API.
- **char-rnn**: A **Torch** character-level language model using **LSTMs**, **GRUs**, and **RNNs**.
- **arxiv-sanity**: A tool for discovering, sorting, and recommending research papers from Arxiv.
- **neuraltalk2**: An early image captioning project in **Lua Torch**.
- **ConvNetJS**: A deep learning library written entirely in **JavaScript**, enabling browser-based demos for training convolutional neural networks.
- **ulogme**: A privacy-first productivity tracker.

He is also known for competing against an early **ConvNet** on **ImageNet** image categorization, earning the nickname "reference human for ImageNet."

## Selected Publications

Karpathy has published extensively in top-tier venues including ICML, ICLR, CVPR, NIPS, and TACL. Key papers include:

- **World of Bits** (ICML 2017): An open-domain platform for web-based agents.
- **PixelCNN++** (ICL 2017): A **PixelCNN** implementation with discretized logistic mixture likelihood.
- **DenseCap** (CVPR 2016, Oral): Fully convolutional localization networks for dense captioning, co-authored with **Justin Johnson** and **Fei-Fei Li**.
- **Deep Visual-Semantic Alignments for Generating Image Descriptions** (CVPR 2015, Oral).
- **Large-Scale Video Classification with Convolutional Neural Networks** (CVPR 2014, Oral).
- **Grounded Compositional Semantics** (TACL 2013): Finding and describing images with sentences, co-authored with **Richard Socher**, **Quoc V. Le**, **Christopher D. Manning**, and **Andrew Ng**.
- **The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks** (2015 blog post).
- **A Recipe for Training Neural Networks** (2019 blog post).
- **Software 2.0** (2017 blog post).

His full publication record is available on Google Scholar.

## Writing & Blog

Karpathy maintains three blogs, with his GitHub blog being the oldest. Notable posts include "A from-scratch tour of **Bitcoin** in Python", "A Recipe for Training Neural Networks", "The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks", and "Software 2.0".

## Philosophy

Karpathy's stated research ethos is to "train deep neural nets on large datasets." He is actively engaged on **X** (formerly Twitter) and **GitHub** for the latest updates on his work.
