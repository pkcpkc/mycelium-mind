# Cross-referencing

## Summary

Cross-referencing is a systematic linking mechanism in knowledge management systems that connects related concepts, entities, and sources to build a persistent, evolving knowledge graph. In LLM-driven wiki architectures, it serves as the primary means by which isolated documents are transformed into an interlinked network where knowledge compounds, consistency is maintained, and synthesis becomes effortless.

## Key Details

- **Automated Entity Linking**: During the ingest phase, schema-driven LLM agents automatically detect named entities and concepts in new sources, then generate bidirectional links to existing wiki pages without manual intervention.
- **Compounding Knowledge Structure**: Each cross-reference strengthens the associative network, allowing the wiki to surface related information, identify gaps, and support deeper contextual synthesis during query operations.
- **Maintenance Burden Reduction**: By delegating link creation, orphan detection, and consistency validation to LLM agents, the pattern shifts routine bookkeeping from humans to machines, enabling users to focus on high-level curation and strategic thinking.
- **Integration with Lint Operations**: Cross-references are continuously audited by periodic lint processes that resolve stale links, contradictory claims, and fragmented entity pages, ensuring the knowledge base remains accurate and cohesive over time.
- **Historical Continuity**: The mechanism operationalizes Vannevar Bush's original vision of associative trails from the [[Memex]], adapted for modern digital, AI-assisted knowledge curation and incremental building workflows.
