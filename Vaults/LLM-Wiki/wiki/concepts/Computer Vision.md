# Computer Vision

## Summary

Computer Vision is a branch of artificial intelligence focused on enabling machines to interpret, process, and understand visual data from the world. It intersects heavily with deep learning, neural networks, and natural language processing to solve tasks ranging from image classification and object detection to image captioning and autonomous navigation. Pioneering contributions by researchers like Andrej Karpathy have significantly advanced the field through work on visual-semantic alignments, recurrent neural networks for vision, and large-scale computer vision education via platforms like [[CS 231n]].

## Key Details

- **Core Architectures**: Relies heavily on [[Convolutional Neural Networks]] (CNNs) and backpropagation for hierarchical feature extraction and image recognition.
- **Education & Frameworks**: Groundbreaking educational resources like [[CS 231n]] and interactive frameworks such as [[ConvNetJS]] have democratized access to vision research and model building.
- **Datasets & Benchmarks**: [[ImageNet]] remains a foundational dataset for training, evaluating, and advancing vision models.
- **Multimodal & Captioning**: Projects like [[neuraltalk2]] demonstrate the integration of vision with language models for image captioning and visual-semantic alignment.
- **Autonomous Systems**: Applied extensively in [[Tesla Autopilot]] for real-time object detection, lane tracking, and 3D scene understanding.
- **Research Tools**: Utilities like [[Arxiv-Sanity]], [[micrograd]], and [[char-rnn]] support broader AI/CV research workflows, experimentation, and literature discovery.
