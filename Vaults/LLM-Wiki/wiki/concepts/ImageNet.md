# ImageNet

## Summary

ImageNet is a large-scale visual database designed to support research in visual object recognition and image classification. Developed by Fei-Fei Li and her team at Stanford University, it provided the massive annotated dataset that directly enabled the deep learning revolution in computer vision.

## Key Details

- **Hierarchical Organization:** Images are annotated according to the WordNet hierarchy, spanning over 20,000 categories and millions of labeled images.
- **ILSVRC Benchmark:** The annual ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge established standardized evaluation metrics that drove rapid architectural innovation in convolutional neural networks and deep learning.
- **Deep Learning Catalyst:** The dataset's unprecedented scale made end-to-end training of deep neural networks feasible, directly enabling breakthrough models like AlexNet and modern vision architectures.
- **Open Research Resource:** Released with a permissive research license, it became the foundational dataset for training and evaluating visual AI systems across academia and industry.
- **Educational & Research Impact:** Widely integrated into foundational AI curricula (e.g., [[CS 231n]]) and leveraged by researchers like [[Andrej Karpathy]] for early computer vision, neural network visualization, and deep learning pedagogy.
