# Reinforcement Learning

## Summary

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a core branch of machine learning focused on how intelligent agents ought to take actions in an environment to maximize the notion of cumulative reward. Unlike supervised learning, it does not require explicitly labeled correct/incorrect data; instead, it learns through direct interaction, balancing exploration of new strategies with exploitation of known successful paths.

## Key Details

- **Mathematical Foundation**: Typically formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), defining states, actions, transition dynamics, reward functions, and discount factors to optimize long-term returns.
- **Core Algorithms**: Ranges from value-based methods (e.g., Q-Learning, Deep Q-Networks) and policy gradient approaches (e.g., REINFORCE, PPO) to hybrid actor-critic architectures that estimate both policy and value functions.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning**: Integrates deep neural networks to handle high-dimensional sensory inputs, enabling breakthroughs in complex domains like game playing, robotics, and autonomous navigation.
- **Industrial Applications**: Heavily utilized in developing autonomous systems, including Tesla Autopilot for decision-making and path planning, as well as resource optimization in large-scale infrastructure.
- **Research Evolution**: Continues to advance through multi-agent systems, offline RL, and alignment with human preferences. Key academic collaborations and workshops have driven innovations in sample efficiency and safe exploration strategies.
