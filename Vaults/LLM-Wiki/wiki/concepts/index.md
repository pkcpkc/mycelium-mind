# Concepts

* [[Arxiv-Sanity]] - An influential open-source project created by Andrej Karpathy that serves as a tool for browsing and filtering academic papers on arXiv.
* [[background]] - The area behind the primary subject in a visual composition, often manipulated to control focus and provide environmental context.
* [[Backpropagation]] - A foundational algorithm for training neural networks by efficiently computing gradients of the loss function with respect to weights using the chain rule.
* [[beard]] - Facial hair growth covering the chin and jawline, ranging from short stubble to full coverage.
* [[char-rnn]] - An influential open-source character-level recurrent neural network implementation created by Andrej Karpathy for educational and practical language modeling purposes.
* [[Citations]] - Standardized references for external sources within a knowledge bundle, enabling traceability and attribution in the Open Knowledge Format.
* [[complexion]] - The visible appearance, coloring, and texture of a person's skin, frequently used in visual analysis and portrait photography.
* [[Computer Vision]] - A field of artificial intelligence focused on enabling computers to interpret, analyze, and understand visual information from the world.
* [[Concept]] - A fundamental document type within the Open Knowledge Format representing abstract ideas, technologies, or non-person entities, distinguished by a required type field for agent routing and filtering.
* [[ConvNetJS]] - A JavaScript-based deep learning framework for defining, training, and running neural networks directly in web browsers or Node.js.
* [[Convolutional Neural Networks]] - A class of deep neural networks specialized for processing grid-like topology data, such as images, by applying learnable filters across spatial dimensions to extract hierarchical features.
* [[Cross-linking]] - A mechanism for connecting concepts using standard markdown links to form a directed graph that conveys semantic relationships.
* [[Cross-referencing]] - A systematic linking mechanism in knowledge management systems that connects related concepts, entities, and sources to build a persistent, evolving knowledge graph.
* [[CS 231n]] - A flagship deep learning and computer vision course at Stanford University designed by Andrej Karpathy.
* [[Deep Learning]] - A subset of machine learning utilizing multi-layered artificial neural networks to enable complex pattern recognition, representation learning, and autonomous decision-making across diverse domains.
* [[Frontmatter]] - A YAML metadata block placed at the beginning of markdown files to define structured, machine-parsable data for knowledge concepts.
* [[hairline|Hairline]] - The boundary line of the scalp where head hair begins, often characterized by variations in shape, density, and progression such as receding at the temples.
* [[head-and-shoulders]] - A photographic and artistic compositional format framing a subject from the top of the head to just below the shoulders, emphasizing facial features and upper-body posture.
* [[ImageNet]] - A large-scale visual database designed for use in visual object recognition software research, catalyzing the deep learning revolution.
* [[Incremental Knowledge Building]] - A knowledge management pattern where AI agents continuously compile, synthesize, and maintain a persistent, interlinked wiki from raw sources, allowing knowledge to compound over time with minimal human maintenance.
* [[Index Files]] - A structural convention in the Open Knowledge Format used to enable progressive disclosure of knowledge within hierarchical bundles.
* [[Ingest-Query-Lint Workflow]] - A three-phase operational pattern for maintaining a persistent, interlinked knowledge base using LLMs, involving document ingestion, synthesis-based querying with feedback filing, and periodic consistency checking.
* [[Knowledge Bundle]] - A hierarchical collection of individual knowledge concepts structured according to the Open Knowledge Format (OKF) specification.
* [[Knowledge Maintenance]] - The automated, incremental process of updating, cross-referencing, and synthesizing a knowledge base using LLMs to reduce human maintenance burden.
* [[Large Language Models]] - A foundational class of artificial intelligence models focused on processing and generating human language, extensively covered in modern AI education and industry research.
* [[lighting]] - The quality, direction, and source of illumination applied to a subject, particularly used in portrait photography to shape features and establish mood.
* [[LLM Wiki]] - A pattern for building persistent, interlinked personal knowledge bases using LLMs to incrementally compile and maintain a wiki from raw sources, reducing maintenance burden through automated cross-referencing and synthesis.
* [[Log Files]] - A structural convention within the Open Knowledge Format used for tracking change history.
* [[man]] - A human male, often characterized by distinct physical traits, casual attire, and expressive facial features.
* [[Memex]] - A hypothetical microfilm-based information storage and retrieval system conceptualized by Vannevar Bush in 1945, establishing the foundational paradigm for personal knowledge curation and associative linking.
* [[micrograd]] - A minimal, single-file Python implementation of a neural network with automatic differentiation, designed for educational purposes.
* [[Natural Language Processing]] - A subfield of artificial intelligence focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language.
* [[Neural Networks]] - Computational architectures inspired by biological brains that learn patterns through weighted connections, serving as the foundation for modern deep learning and artificial intelligence.
* [[neuraltalk2|NeuralTalk2]] - An open-source deep learning project by Andrej Karpathy for generating natural language descriptions of images using combined CNN and LSTM architectures.
* [[Obsidian]] - A markdown-based knowledge management application used to build and maintain persistent, interlinked personal wikis, often integrated with LLM workflows for automated synthesis and cross-referencing.
* [[Open Knowledge Format]] - A draft specification defining a minimal, human- and agent-friendly markdown-based format for structuring, linking, and exchanging knowledge bundles across systems.
* [[OpenAI]] - A prominent artificial intelligence research organization founded in 2015, recognized for advancing large language models, midtraining methodologies, and synthetic data generation.
* [[Persistent Wiki]] - A pattern for building personal knowledge bases where information is compiled once and maintained incrementally by LLMs, creating a compounding artifact with deep cross-references that transcends standard RAG.
* [[portrait]] - A focused visual representation of a person, typically emphasizing facial features, expression, and upper-body framing.
* [[PyTorch]] - An open-source machine learning framework built on the Torch library, widely used for deep learning research, computer vision, and large language model development.
* [[RAG]] - A standard architectural pattern that augments large language model outputs with dynamic external retrieval, typically lacking persistent knowledge accumulation across queries.
* [[Reinforcement Learning]] - A machine learning paradigm where agents learn optimal behaviors through trial-and-error interactions with an environment to maximize cumulative reward.
* [[Schema-driven LLM]] - A methodological approach where Large Language Models operate according to predefined structural schemas to ensure consistency, automate cross-referencing, and maintain persistent knowledge bases.
* [[Stanford University]] - A premier research university and global hub for artificial intelligence, computer vision, and deep learning education.
* [[stubble]] - Facial hair characterized by short, trimmed growth on the chin and jawline, often appearing as a light beard or shadow.
* [[t-shirt]] - A casual, short-sleeved garment typically featuring a round neckline and no collar.
* [[Tesla Autopilot]] - An advanced driver-assistance system developed by Tesla that leverages computer vision and deep learning to enable automated driving capabilities.
* [[Versioning]] - A mechanism for tracking, recording, and managing historical changes and revisions to knowledge concepts and bundles over time.
