# Hairline

## Summary

The hairline is the anatomical boundary separating the scalp from the forehead, marking where head hair begins. In visual and anthropometric contexts, it serves as a key facial feature that influences perceived age, symmetry, and overall aesthetic composition. The hairline can vary widely in shape and density, with a receding hairline specifically denoting the gradual backward progression or thinning of hair at the frontal and temple regions.

## Key Details

- **Anatomical Boundary**: Defines the transition zone between facial skin and scalp hair, often following natural growth patterns rather than a perfectly straight line.
- **Receding Pattern**: Characterized by thinning or migration of hair at the temples and frontal area, frequently associated with natural aging, genetics (androgenetic alopecia), or hormonal changes.
- **Visual Impact in Portraiture**: In close-up photography, the hairline frames the upper face, affecting how light and shadow interact with the forehead and influencing perceived facial proportions.
- **Shape Variability**: Ranges from straight across to curved, rounded, or asymmetrical, with natural irregularities being common and normal across diverse populations.
- **Contextual Role in Appearance**: When documented in portrait analysis, a receding hairline at the temples often contributes to a mature, approachable aesthetic, especially when paired with complementary features like stubble and warm lighting.
