# head-and-shoulders

## Summary

The head-and-shoulders format is a standard framing technique used in photography, portraiture, and visual media. It focuses on capturing the subject's face, neck, and upper torso (typically down to the mid-chest or collarbone), making it a foundational composition for professional headshots, identification documents, and intimate portraits. This framing style minimizes environmental distractions to prioritize facial expressions, eye contact, and upper-body language.

## Key Details

- **Framing Scope**: Encompasses the crown of the head, eyes, nose, mouth, neck, and shoulders, occasionally extending to the upper chest.
- **Emphasis**: Prioritizes facial expressions, gaze direction, and subtle posture cues over contextual background elements.
- **Variations**: Ranges from tight crops (focusing heavily on the face and eyes) to standard medium-close shots that include more of the torso, clothing, and shoulders.
- **Usage Context**: Widely employed in professional networking (e.g., LinkedIn), corporate branding, identification photography (passports, IDs), and artistic portraiture to establish connection, authority, or approachability.
- **Lighting & Background**: Typically utilizes controlled or soft natural lighting to highlight facial features, with backgrounds kept simple, muted, or blurred (shallow depth of field) to maintain subject focus.
- **Psychological Impact**: Creates a sense of intimacy and direct engagement with the viewer, often perceived as more personal and approachable than full-body or distant shots.
