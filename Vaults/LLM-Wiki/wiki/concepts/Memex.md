# Memex

## Summary

The Memex (Memory Extender) is a conceptual information storage and retrieval device proposed by Vannevar Bush in 1945. It envisions a private, curated knowledge store that allows users to accumulate information and navigate it through associative trails rather than linear paths, serving as a direct precursor to modern hypertext and personal knowledge management systems.

## Key Details

- **Origin:** Conceptualized in Vannevar Bush's 1945 essay "As We May Think".
- **Core Mechanism:** Utilizes a microfilm-based architecture to store vast amounts of documents and enables users to create custom "associative trails" that link related information non-linearly.
- **Primary Function:** Acts as a personal knowledge management system, emphasizing user-directed curation, synthesis, and the ability to retain contextual pathways through accumulated data.
- **Legacy & Influence:** Directly inspired the development of hypertext, computer science architecture, and modern digital wiki frameworks like [[LLM Wiki]] and [[Obsidian]].
- **Philosophical Impact:** Shifted the paradigm from passive information consumption to active, user-maintained knowledge trails and personalized information retrieval.
